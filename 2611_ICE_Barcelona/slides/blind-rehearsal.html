A blinding shift is recovered when its reference is near the truth

  • The test — Martin Crocce's of 24 November: add 30 shifts to a noiseless Euclid DR1 South vector, run the full chain on each (38 parameters, nuisances sampled, as he asked on 20 November), and plot recovered minus injected $(\Omega_\mathrm{m}, S_8)$. The dashed circle is $0.3\sigma$, the pass level registered for the CMBX blind. 90 shifted chains, 30 per set of points.
  • First step — the truth sits at the blind's reference, as in the group's plan. All 60 chains (3×2pt, and 5×2pt with SPT-3G Main and Summer) are within $0.12\sigma$, and $\sigma(S_8)$ keeps its size within 9 %. This case passes by construction: $T(\mathrm{ref}) + T(\mathrm{shifted}) - T(\mathrm{ref})$ is exactly $T(\mathrm{shifted})$, so the chain is the forecast at another cosmology. It tests projection effects and numerics, not whether the added shift is the right one.
  • Second step — the truth sits off the reference and the blind does not know it: galaxy biases 10 % higher, $A_\mathrm{IA}$ 0.55 for 0.85, $\log T_\mathrm{AGN}$ 7.9 for 7.7, and $S_8$ 0.777 for 0.839. The 5×2pt misses by a scatter of $0.29\sigma$ in $S_8$ and in $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, up to $0.6\sigma$ in either; 10 of 30 stay inside the circle. The mean miss is zero ($-0.04$ and $+0.01\sigma$), so an ensemble average hides it.
  • Third step — the same 30 parameter shifts, best fits instead of chains, with noise (two realizations of the full 6×2pt vector, the same noise in every probe set). With one fixed reference off the truth (red) the $S_8$ miss scatters by 0.27, 0.45, 0.31, 0.36 and $0.50\sigma$ for cosmic shear, 2×2pt, 3×2pt, 5×2pt and 6×2pt. With the reference at the best fit of the unblinded 3×2pt vector, the same one for every probe set (blue), it is 0.11, 0.07, 0.09, 0.11 and $0.17\sigma$, and at most $0.38\sigma$. Counted inside the joint $0.3\sigma$ circle in $(\Omega_\mathrm{m}, S_8)$, of 60: 25, 8, 10, 11 and 13 against 59, 46, 52, 52 and 38.
One shift for every probe

Crocce's plan applies the same shift to the 3×2pt, 2×2pt and 1×2pt. With a fixed reference off the truth each probe moves by a slightly different amount, so one blind changes the $S_8$ difference between cosmic shear and 2×2pt by a scatter of 0.3 to $0.4\sigma$ over the 30 shifts, up to $0.7\sigma$. A blinded internal-consistency test would see that as a mild tension that is not in the data. With the shared 3×2pt reference it is 0.04 and $0.15\sigma$ for the two noise draws, up to $0.4\sigma$.

Where the miss comes from

The miss is the product of the shift and the offset of the reference, so it is linear in both: at half the offset the 3×2pt $S_8$ miss halves ($0.11\sigma$ against $0.24\sigma$). By source, for the 3×2pt: cosmology alone $0.19\sigma$, galaxy bias alone $0.08\sigma$ ($0.18\sigma$ in $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$), intrinsic alignments $0.02\sigma$, baryons $0.02\sigma$. The offsets chosen are large on purpose: the truth's $S_8$ is $5.9\sigma$ from the reference's, at the level where weak lensing measures it low.

The alternative of scaling the vector

Multiplying by $T(\mathrm{shifted})/T(\mathrm{ref})$ recovers $S_8$ to $0.3\sigma$ on a noiseless vector ($\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ to $0.4\sigma$), but with noise the 3×2pt $S_8$ misses by up to $0.7\sigma$. Muir et al. (2020, arXiv:1911.05929, footnote 1) warn that scaling rescales the noise as well as the signal, and it does: the best-fit $\chi^2$ changes by a factor 0.82 to 1.21 for cosmic shear and 0.84 to 1.18 for the 5×2pt, and for cosmic shear it follows the hidden $S_8$ shift with a correlation of 0.98. A blind that shows the shift in the goodness of fit is not usable. The added shift, with either reference, leaves $\chi^2$ unchanged within 1 %.

What the reference at the fit would need

The blinding pipeline would fit the unblinded 3×2pt vector, use that fit as the reference and keep it sealed with the shift. A full best fit takes minutes on one node with all 38 parameters, so it is cheap; the cost is that the pipeline then holds an unblinded fit. This is a best-fit test with the right model on two noise draws: no model error, no chains, no mocks, and it is applied to no blind. The same shift in $(A_s, \omega_c, h)$ from a different reference is a different shift in $S_8$, up to $5.9\sigma$ for the 6×2pt here against $3.7\sigma$ from the fixed one, so the blue points carry the larger shifts. $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ still misses by 0.12 to $0.25\sigma$ of scatter, up to $0.65\sigma$ for the 2×2pt.

Best fits against chains

The recovery test at the posterior's maximum, all 30 shifts batched, takes 3 minutes where a chain takes 10 to 25 minutes for each shift. On the 30 off-reference noiseless 5×2pt chains the two agree to $0.05\sigma$ in $S_8$ and $0.06\sigma$ in $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ (correlation 0.99, worst case 0.13 and $0.15\sigma$). Three shifts each rerun as cosmic-shear and 2×2pt chains agree with their best fits to $0.12\sigma$ in $S_8$ and $0.2\sigma$ in $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$.

Setup

The DR1 forecast's vector, covariance, priors and scale cuts (the DR1 forecast slide), with the 15 November CLOE through its JAX adapter, $n_s$ held in the shift. Widths on a shifted vector are not the unshifted widths: $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})$ changes by a factor 0.75 to 1.34 with where the shift lands. An earlier rehearsal on one GLASS mock, with two throwaway blinds and eight parameters, recovered its shifts within $0.75\sigma$ (the Napoli 2610 deck). The real TR1 blind was never opened.
