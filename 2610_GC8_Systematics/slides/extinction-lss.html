CIB predicts much of the measured $\bar\rho$

$\rho_\ell=C_\ell^{XT}/\sqrt{C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}C_\ell^{TT}}$; $\bar\rho$ = Gaussian GLS mean.

The frames show measured means without visibility correction, add the CIB prediction, then apply density visibility correction. All compress the same full range, $100\lt\ell\lt3000$, into a GLS constant-rho mean with its error. Density switches from without to with visibility correction in both cycles; shear is LensMC E, kappa is SPT-3G. Positive means are not a claim that every band is positive, or that every tracer rejects zero. There is no monotonic-redshift claim: Cycle-1 density bin 6 is below bin 5. Cycle-1 low-redshift shear means are mostly within one or two Gaussian errors of zero; high-redshift shear and kappa carry the clearer support.

In these exploratory figures, $C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}$ is the release-specific fiducial tracer signal model, with no shot, shape or reconstruction noise, averaged uniformly over exact integer science bins. It is neither an observed total auto nor the exact masked-auto bandpower-window average. $C_\ell^{TT}$ is the measured template auto; for CIB comparisons it is the odd-ring × even-ring spectrum with the cached interpolation transfer correction. The normalization is held fixed in the error propagation; the errors are the full Gaussian cross blocks divided by the normalization outer product. This is a model-normalized signal-correlation estimate; it avoids clipping negative noise-subtracted shear autos. It is not bounded to lie within [-1,1] in a noisy realization. No denominator uncertainty is included. The GLS mean is a descriptive compression, not a fitted physical constant-rho model.

Extinction cannot directly obscure a CMB-lensing reconstruction; the joint sign, high-redshift shear and CIB-route comparison support the LSS interpretation. This does not rule out real Galactic selection effects or CMB reconstruction foreground response. B-mode evidence is from the preceding scorecard, not a rho normalization for B.
