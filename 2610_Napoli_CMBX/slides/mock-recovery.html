Mock κ crosses recover the input to 10-seed noise

Twelve CMB-lensing cross-spectra from the GLASS 6×2pt mocks: galaxy density × κ in the top row, shear × κ in the bottom row, tomographic bins 1 to 6 from left to right. Points are the mean of $\ell C_\ell$ over 10 mock realizations, the black line is the input theory spectrum, and each row shares one y axis so the bins compare directly: the shear cross grows with source redshift, the density cross much less so. The mocks use the ACT DR6 lensing mask and noise and the TR1 Euclid mask, and are measured with the production NaMaster pipeline.

The error bars on the spectra are $\sigma_1,$ the seed-to-seed scatter of a single realization (the sample standard deviation over the 10 seeds), so they show what one survey of this size is worth; they are not the Gaussian error bars of the data. The strip under each panel is the residual, mean minus input, in units of $\sigma_1.$ The mean of 10 seeds is known to $\sigma_1/\sqrt{10} \approx 0.32\,\sigma_1$ (the shaded band and the strip error bars), so an unbiased pipeline should put about two points in three inside the band.

That is what we see. Over all 180 bandpowers the mean $|\Delta|$ is $0.23\,\sigma_1$ (0.22 for density, 0.25 for shear), against $0.25\,\sigma_1$ expected from noise alone; the signed mean is $+0.007\,\sigma_1,$ and no single spectrum's mean offset exceeds $0.18\,\sigma_1.$ Treating the bands as independent, $\sum 10\,(\Delta/\sigma_1)^2 = 154$ over 180 bands. The largest point, $0.96\,\sigma_1$ in shear bin 1 at $\ell \approx 220,$ is about 3 standard errors of the mean, about what one expects once among 180 bands. A full-covariance $\chi^2$ is the next step.
