# Wolz, Farah, Reischke, Alonso+ — *Analytical covariances for catalogue-based pseudo-$C_\ell$s* (arXiv:2607.14843)

Brief for JC, 2026-09. Source: `~/Documents/projects/talks/2609_JC_covariances/papers/2607.14843/source/main.tex` (881 lines).

## 1. Thesis

Catalogue-based pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimators (Baleato Lizancos & Alonso 2407.21013) skip pixelisation entirely: you compute spherical harmonics directly at the source positions, so there is no pixel window, no aliasing, and shot noise appears as an exactly-known self-pair floor you subtract. The problem is the *covariance*. The workhorse analytic covariance for pseudo-$C_\ell$ — the improved Narrow Kernel Approximation (iNKA) — needs real-space **products of masks**, and a catalogue mask is a sum of Dirac deltas: the product of two different catalogues' masks is identically zero, and a catalogue's mask squared diverges. This paper regularises that. Two moves: (i) replace each point source with a Gaussian "cloud" of angular size set by the mean interparticle separation, which band-limits the mask and makes mask products finite and computable; (ii) split every two-point correlator into distinct-pair and self-pair contributions, treat the distinct pairs with iNKA and the self-pairs **exactly** — they behave algebraically like an inhomogeneous white-noise term, so the existing iNKA signal/noise machinery of García-García+ (2010.09717) applies verbatim. They validate against an exact $\mathcal{O}(N^4\ell_{\max}^4)$ brute-force summation (feasible only for sparse samples) and against 1000-realisation sample covariances across FRBs, DES-Y3-like cosmic shear, DESI-LRG-like clustering, and a 16-case × {auto,cross} grid of field-type combinations. It is in public `NaMaster`.

## 2. The machinery

### 2.0 Setup: what a catalogue field *is*

The whole paper follows from one definitional choice (Eqs. `eq:mask_delta`, `eq:masked_cat_map`): the catalogue's mask **is** the set of sources.

$$w_a(\hat n)\equiv\sum_i w_i\,\delta^D(\hat n,\hat n_i),\qquad \tilde a(\hat n)=\sum_i w_i a_i\,\delta^D(\hat n,\hat n_i)$$

so $w^a_{\ell m}=\sum_i w_i Y^*_{\ell m,i}$ and $\tilde a_{\ell m}=\sum_i w_i a_i Y^*_{\ell m,i}$, evaluated with the irregular-grid SHT of Reinecke+ (`ducc`, 2304.10431). *Intuition:* the survey window and the sampling are the same object — there is no separate "footprint map". *Assumption:* none yet, this is exact; the price is that everything downstream that wanted a smooth $w$ now has deltas in it.

The auto-spectra pick up self-pair floors that are known exactly and subtracted (`eq:Nw`, `eq:Na`):

$$\tilde N_w\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_i w_i^2,\qquad \tilde N_a\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_i w_i^2 a_i^2$$

This is the estimator's selling point: "completely immune to any white-noise component (homogeneous or otherwise)". Fig. 1 shows $\tilde C^w_\ell$ for the *Quaia* mask flattening onto $\tilde N_w$ well before the interparticle scale.

The statement of the problem, verbatim (§2.3): *"For catalogue-based masks (Eq. 2.16), such products are either zero, in the case of cross-correlations between different catalogues, or they diverge for auto-correlations (since they involve products of coincident delta functions). Regularising this behaviour is the main topic of this paper."*

### 2.1 The baseline: NKA and what "improved" adds

Gaussian pseudo-$C_\ell$ covariance reduces to correlators $\langle\tilde a_{\ell m}\tilde c^*_{\ell'm'}\rangle=\sum_{\ell''m''}\mathcal M_{\ell m,\ell''m''}(w_a)\mathcal M^*_{\ell'm',\ell''m''}(w_c)C^{ac}_{\ell''}$ (`eq:general_2pt`). **NKA**: $\mathcal M$ is narrowly peaked around $\ell''\simeq\ell$, so pull $C_{\ell''}\to C_{(\ell,\ell')}\equiv(C_\ell+C_{\ell'})/2$ out of the sum; the remaining $\mathcal M\mathcal M^*$ sum collapses onto the *product* mask, giving (`eq:nka_simple`)

$$\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C^{ab}_\ell,\tilde C^{cd}_{\ell'})=C^{ac}_{(\ell,\ell')}C^{bd}_{(\ell,\ell')}\,\Xi_{\ell\ell'}(w_aw_c,w_bw_d)+(c\leftrightarrow d)$$

with $\Xi_{\ell\ell'}(w_1,w_2)\equiv\sum_{\ell''}\frac{2\ell''+1}{4\pi}\tilde C^{w_1w_2}_{\ell''}\begin{pmatrix}\ell&\ell'&\ell''\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}^2$ (`eq:Xi`). *Assumption:* narrow kernel — i.e. the mask is not too complicated and $C_\ell$ not too steep across the kernel width.

**iNKA adds exactly two things** (both from 2010.09717), and it matters for the Nagura comparison that these are the *only* two:
1. **Mode-coupled effective spectrum** (`eq:clbar0`): replace $C^{ac}_\ell\to\overline C^{ac}_\ell\equiv\langle\tilde C^{ac}_\ell\rangle/\langle w_aw_c\rangle_{\rm sky}$ — use the *mask-convolved* spectrum divided by the mean squared mask, not the theory spectrum. This partially restores the mode coupling thrown away when $C_{\ell''}$ was pulled out of the sum, and is *"important, particularly for complicated masks, where the core assumption of the NKA … is less accurate."*
2. **Inhomogeneous white noise treated exactly** (`eq:general_2pt_wnoise`): an extra $\delta^K_{ac}\mathcal M_{\ell m,\ell'm'}(\Omega_{\rm pix}\sigma_a^2w_a^2)$ term, splitting the covariance into signal-signal / signal-noise / noise-noise pieces.

Note what iNKA does *not* do: it is still a diagonal-in-$C_\ell$ approximation. It sharpens the amplitude and the noise, not the off-diagonal $\ell\ell'$ structure of the kernel. Hold that thought for §6.

### 2.2 The cloud approximation

For *disjoint* catalogues, $\langle w_a w_c\rangle_{\rm sky}=0$ pointwise, but by Parseval $\langle w_aw_b\rangle_{\rm sky}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\ell_{\max}}\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\tilde C^{w_aw_b}_\ell$, and this only vanishes as $\ell_{\max}\to\infty$ after incoherent high-$\ell$ oscillations (amplified by $2\ell+1$) cancel. Truncating at finite $\ell_{\max}$ leaves the physically meaningful overlap area. So: *"Choosing a finite value for $\ell_{\rm max}$ … is equivalent to band-limiting the harmonic transform of the mask or, in other words, treating each source not as a point but as a 'cloud' of finite size $\theta_{\rm c}$."*

Concretely (`eq:wbar`), Gaussian-smooth the mask harmonics:

$$w^a_{\ell m}\rightarrow\bar w^a_{\ell m}\equiv w^a_{\ell m}\phi^a_\ell,\qquad \phi^a_\ell\equiv\exp\!\left[-\tfrac12\ell(\ell+1)\theta_{\rm c}^2\right],\qquad \theta_{\rm c}=\sqrt{\theta_{\rm ip}^2+(\pi/\ell_{\max})^2}$$

*Intuition:* you cannot resolve the mask below the mean source spacing anyway, so smear each source over that scale; the smoothed mask is a continuous function of the sky and its real-space products are finite. *Assumption:* that the $\ell_{\max}$-truncated sums have genuinely converged — Fig. 2 argues they do for $\ell_{\max}\lesssim\ell_{\rm ip}\equiv\pi/\theta_{\rm ip}$, and that *"their ratio is remarkably stable, even at very low values of $\ell_{\rm max}$"*, which is what actually matters since $\overline C_\ell$ is a ratio.

This is the paper's cleverest move and its softest: $\theta_{\rm c}$ is a *choice*, justified by an observed plateau rather than derived. The effective-area overlap enters exactly here — the denominator becomes (`eq:inka_cell`)

$$\overline C^{ab}_\ell\equiv\frac{\langle\tilde C^{ab}_\ell\rangle}{\langle\bar w_a\bar w_b\rangle_{\rm sky}},\qquad \langle\bar w_a\bar w_b\rangle_{\rm sky}=\sum_\ell\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\tilde C^{w_aw_b}_\ell\,\phi^a_\ell\phi^b_\ell$$

i.e. the two catalogues' overlapping area is read off the low-$\ell$ cross-spectrum of their point masks, with each catalogue's own cloud scale $\phi^a,\phi^b$ suppressing the noisy high-$\ell$ tail. Two catalogues with different densities get different smoothings — that asymmetry is the mechanism.

### 2.3 Why self-pairs need separate treatment

For an auto-correlation, split the correlator by whether the two sources coincide (`eq:general_2pt_cat`):

$$\langle\tilde a_{\ell m}\tilde a^*_{\ell'm'}\rangle=\underbrace{\sum_{i\neq j}w_iw_j\langle a_ia_j\rangle Y^*_{\ell m,i}Y_{\ell'm',j}}_{\text{distinct pairs}}+\underbrace{\mathcal M_{\ell m,\ell'm'}\big(\overline{(aw_a)^2}\big)}_{\text{self-pairs}}$$

with $\overline{(aw_a)^2}(\hat n)\equiv\sum_i w_i^2\langle a_i^2\rangle\delta^D(\hat n,\hat n_i)$ (`eq:self_var`) — a local variance map. The point, in their words: *"Eq. 3.10 is strongly reminiscent of the expression for the two-point correlator of a field with white noise … with the self-pairs contribution playing the role of noise, and the sum over distinct pairs that of the signal."* And it is not coincidence: *"the noise-like contribution from self-pairs is an intrinsic aspect of fields sampled at discrete points that penalises sparser samples with larger statistical uncertainties."*

So self-pairs must be (a) *removed* from the smoothed squared mask, else double-counted, and (b) *kept* as a variance contribution. Removal requires the self-pair piece of $\bar w^2$, which is another catalogue field with its own kernel:

$$(\overline{w_a^2})_{\ell m}\equiv\sum_i (w^a_i)^2(\phi^2_a)_\ell Y^*_{\ell m,i},\qquad (\phi^2_a)_\ell=\frac{e^{-\frac14\ell(\ell+1)\theta_{{\rm c},a}^2}}{4\pi\theta_{{\rm c},a}^2}$$

(note $\phi^2$ has half the exponent width and a $1/4\pi\theta_c^2$ amplitude — it is the transform of the *square* of the Gaussian cloud, not the square of the transform). Everywhere the mask product appears you use $\bar w_a\bar w_c-\delta^K_{ac}\overline{w_a^2}$.

### 2.4 The main result (`eq:main_result`)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C^{ab}_\ell,\tilde C^{cd}_{\ell'})=\;
&\overline C^{ac}_{(\ell,\ell')}\overline C^{bd}_{(\ell,\ell')}\,\Xi_{\ell\ell'}\!\left(\bar w_a\bar w_c-\delta^K_{ac}\overline{w_a^2},\;\bar w_b\bar w_d-\delta^K_{bd}\overline{w_b^2}\right) &&\text{[sig-sig]}\\
+\;&\delta^K_{bd}\,\overline C^{ac}_{(\ell,\ell')}\,\Xi_{\ell\ell'}\!\left(\bar w_a\bar w_c-\delta^K_{ac}\overline{w_a^2},\;\overline{(bw_b)^2}\right) &&\text{[sig-noise]}\\
+\;&\delta^K_{ac}\,\overline C^{bd}_{(\ell,\ell')}\,\Xi_{\ell\ell'}\!\left(\overline{(aw_a)^2},\;\bar w_b\bar w_d-\delta^K_{bd}\overline{w_b^2}\right) &&\text{[noise-sig]}\\
+\;&\delta^K_{ac}\delta^K_{bd}\,\Xi_{\ell\ell'}\!\left(\overline{(aw_a)^2},\;\overline{(bw_b)^2}\right)\;+\;(c\leftrightarrow d) &&\text{[noise-noise]}
\end{aligned}
$$

with the effective spectrum now shot-noise-debiased on both numerator and denominator (`eq:inka_cell_final`):

$$\overline C^{ab}_\ell\equiv\frac{\tilde C^{ab}_\ell-\delta_{ab}\tilde N_a}{\sum_{\ell'}\frac{2\ell'+1}{4\pi}\big(\tilde C^{w_aw_b}_{\ell'}-\delta_{ab}\tilde N_w\big)\phi^a_{\ell'}\phi^b_{\ell'}}$$

*Reading it:* the Kronecker deltas are catalogue-identity flags. Four different catalogues → only the first line survives and you are doing smoothed-mask iNKA. All four the same → all four terms fire, and the noise-noise term is the pure shot-noise variance. Everything approximate lives in the $\overline C$'s; the $\Xi$'s and the noise maps are exact given the catalogue.

**One subtlety worth the slide.** The noise-noise term needs $\langle a_i^2\rangle$ per source; the naive ansatz is $\langle a_i^2\rangle\to a_i^2$ (`eq:awa2`). But then its pseudo-$C_\ell$ involves fourth moments, and *"the fact that $\langle a_i^2\rangle\langle a_j^2\rangle\neq\langle a_i^2a_j^2\rangle$ leads to an overestimation of the power spectrum of the local variance map at high $\ell$, which we must correct for"* — analytically, by Wick, subtracting $\tilde N_{\overline{(aw_a)^2}}\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_i w_i^4a_i^4$ (`eq:4point_corr_spin0`). A self-pair correction to a self-pair term.

### 2.5 Momentum fields, clustering, and the exact check

**Momentum fields** ($\pi\sim(1+\delta_g)v$, e.g. kSZ) differ in one respect: the mask is the *expected* density (completeness map or randoms), not the observed sources. With a continuous completeness map, shot noise is not an issue in the mask and the signal-signal kernel reverts to plain iNKA. With randoms, *every* mask ingredient ($\bar w_a$, $\overline{w_a^2}$, $\tilde N_w$, $\tilde C^{w_aw_b}_\ell$) is built from the randoms, not the data.

**Galaxy clustering** is a momentum field with $a_i\equiv1$ and the mean subtracted: $\delta_g=\sum_{d}w_d\delta^D-\sum_{r}w_r\delta^D$. Because randoms also contribute self-pairs, $\overline{(aw_a)^2}=\sum_d w_d^2\delta^D+\sum_r w_r^2\delta^D$ and $\tilde N_a=\frac1{4\pi}(\sum_d w_d^2+\sum_r w_r^2)$; no fourth-moment correction is needed since $a_i$ is deterministic.

**Exact summation** (§3.6) is the internal ground truth: insert $\hat C_\ell=\sum_{\ell_1}[M^{-1}]_{\ell\ell_1}(\tilde C_{\ell_1}-\tilde N_a)$ into the covariance, Wick, use the addition theorem $\sum_m Y^*_{\ell m,i}Y_{\ell m,j}=\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}P_{\ell,ij}$, and everything collapses to a quadruple sum over distinct source pairs (`eq:cov_brute`):

$$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat C^{aa}_\ell,\hat C^{aa}_{\ell'})=2\sum_{\ell_1\ell_2}[M^{-1}]_{\ell\ell_1}[M^{-1}]_{\ell_2\ell'}\sum_{i\neq j,\,p\neq q}\frac{w_iw_jw_pw_q}{(4\pi)^2}P_{\ell_1,ij}P_{\ell_2,pq}\langle a_ia_p\rangle\langle a_ja_q\rangle$$

$\mathcal O(N_{\rm sources}^4\ell_{\max}^4)$, `jax`-implemented, sparse samples only. Appendix `app:brute_nka` proves it reduces to `eq:main_result`. **This is structurally the same object as Nagura+'s "weighted quartic counts"** — see §6.

## 4. Headline numbers and validation regime

- **1000 realisations** for every empirical covariance in the paper.
- **FRBs (§4.1).** CHIME: 3615 real source positions, $\sigma_{\rm DM}=150\ \mathrm{pc\,cm^{-3}}$, 12 log bins over $\ell\in[2,383]$. DSA-like: $5\times10^4$ sources, $\sigma=50$, $\ell_{\max}=300$ (signal-dominated). Right-tail coverage of the nominal 5% PTE: CHIME **5.8%** (exact sum) / **7%** (iNKA); DSA **4.7%** / **4.9%**. Expected density $\bar n\sim10^{-3}\,\mathrm{arcmin^{-2}}$.
- **Cosmic shear (§4.2).** DES Y3, third redshift bin of 2403.13794. $\approx2.5\times10^7$ sources over $\approx4000\ \mathrm{deg^2}$, median $\theta_{\rm ip}=0.15'$. **38 bandpowers to $\ell_{\max}=10{,}000$** — deliberately past any real shear analysis, "to stress-test our approach". $\sigma_e=0.27$/component. Accurate on all scales for $EE$, $\ell\gtrsim10$ for $BB$. Off-diagonals are *"a few per cent of the main diagonal"* and well captured. 5% PTE covered by **4%** (noisy, slight over-estimate of covariance) and **5.9%** (noiseless, slight under).
- **Clustering (§4.3).** DESI Legacy photometric LRG bin 2, $b=2$, lognormal + Poisson from $N_{\rm side}=512$, $\ell_{\max}=1000$, randoms 10× data. 5% PTE covered by **6.8–6.9%** for both mask conventions.
- **General grid (§4.4).** **16 field-type combinations × {auto-catalogue, cross-catalogue} = 32 KS tests**, $\ell\in[2,767]$, 20 bandpowers. Field types: tracer catalogue $C$, pixelated map $M$, clustering $G$, momentum $\Pi$; spins 0 and 2 incl. $EE/EB/BB$; $N_{\rm sources}$ from $10^6$ to $5\times10^7$; positions Poisson-sampled from the *Quaia* selection function at $N_{\rm side}=256$; power-law input $C_\ell\propto(10+\ell)^{-0.7}$.
- **The one failure: case 5, cross-catalogue $(G_0,G_0,G_0,G_0)$, KS PTE $=1.3\times10^{-4}$.** All 31 others pass comfortably (range roughly 0.06–0.97). The authors attribute it to Monte-Carlo noise in the *empirical* covariance destabilising its inverse, not to the method: excluding $\ell>500$ gives PTE 0.36. Note the honest framing — *"based solely on the number of tests performed, we expect a small proportion to exhibit PTEs outside the $(5,95)\%$ range"* — and that cross-catalogue blocks aren't positive definite so their $\chi^2$ isn't even $\chi^2$-distributed.

## 5. Where it can fail

**Authors' own list (§5).** Self-pair noise terms *"may exhibit additional scatter when estimated from extremely sparse catalogues"* — exact in expectation, noisy in practice when $\langle a_i^2\rangle\to a_i^2$ from few sources. And the signal term inherits NKA, so it degrades as:
> - the signal power spectrum becomes steeper,
> - the complexity of the mask (e.g., due to inhomogeneity in the source distribution) grows, or
> - the signal exhibits increasing asymmetry between the $E$- and $B$-mode components for spin fields.

Plus a hard structural limit: **iNKA exists only for spin 0 and 2**, because the spin correlation structure is derived *"by neglecting the spatial derivatives of the mask, and the number of derivatives depends on the spin."* Fallback for other spins — treat $E$ and $B$ as independent scalars — is explicitly flagged as degraded and to-be-tested.

**My reading, beyond what they say.**
1. **$\theta_{\rm c}$ is calibrated, not derived.** $\theta_{\rm c}=\sqrt{\theta_{\rm ip}^2+(\pi/\ell_{\max})^2}$ is a Gaussian ansatz whose only defence is the plateau in Fig. 2, on *one* realisation of *one* mock at $3\times10^4$ sources. No sensitivity scan over $\theta_{\rm c}$, no demonstration that the plateau survives a strongly clustered or hole-riddled footprint. For a catalogue with a widely varying local density, "the" mean interparticle distance is a single number standing in for a field — I would expect this to be the first thing to break on real data with depth variations, and it is untested.
2. **The validation spectra are shallow.** $C_\ell\propto(10+\ell)^{-0.7}$ across the entire 32-case grid, tested for insensitivity to $\alpha$ but only within that family. The authors themselves name "steeper spectrum" as failure mode #1, and then validate on an unusually flat one. The realistic cases (shear, LRG) do use real spectra, so this is a caveat on the *breadth* claim rather than the headline results.
3. **Only the disconnected covariance.** Non-Gaussian terms — connected trispectrum, super-sample covariance — are entirely out of scope. For Stage-IV shear at the $\ell\sim10^4$ they stress-test to, SSC and the connected term are not negligible; the paper's accuracy claim is about reproducing *the Gaussian piece*, not the covariance you would use. Easy to elide in a talk.
4. **Off-diagonals are validated where they're small.** In the shear case they are "a few per cent of the main diagonal", i.e. the regime where getting them wrong barely moves $\chi^2$. The $\chi^2$/KS metrics used throughout are integrated statistics — genuinely the right thing for cosmology, but structurally insensitive to a coherent, low-amplitude off-diagonal error. Which is exactly what Nagura+ found bites in real space.
5. **Circularity risk on real data.** Both $\overline C_\ell$ and the local variance map can be taken from the data itself (NaMaster's default fallback). The authors flag this — it *"introduce[s] realisation-dependent stochastic noise in the analytical covariance and … the measured signal and its covariance become correlated"* — but the entire validation uses the *true* input spectrum to avoid it. So the accuracy demonstrated is an upper bound on what you get in an actual analysis.

## 6. Relation to the two companions

### Nagura, Terasawa, Terawaki & Takada (2605.16186) — real-space 2PCF covariance

Their claim, verbatim from the abstract: *"We find that the covariance of the 2PCF obtained by using the iNKA does not reproduce the covariance measured directly from Gaussian simulations. Although the iNKA accurately models the diagonal structure of the harmonic-space covariance, residual inaccuracies in the off-diagonal components propagate through the Legendre transformation and significantly affect the real-space covariance. In contrast, the weighted quartic-counts method shows better agreement."* Test case: HSC Y3 mask, convergence fields.

**Is it the same iNKA? Yes — the same García-García+ (2010.09717) approximation Wolz+ build on.** Wolz+ generalise its *inputs* (regularised catalogue masks, exact self-pairs); they do not touch the narrow-kernel assumption that Nagura+ identify as the culprit. §2.1 above is the crux: iNKA improves the effective spectrum and the noise treatment, not the $\ell\ell'$ kernel structure.

**Would the leak appear here? I expect the error to be present but invisible under Wolz+'s metrics.** Three reasons: (i) they never Legendre-transform, so they never apply the operator that sums off-diagonals coherently and amplifies a small bias into a large one; (ii) their validation statistics are $\chi^2$/KS on binned bandpowers, which is dominated by the diagonal — precisely the part Nagura+ concede iNKA gets right; (iii) they check only the *first* off-diagonal, where the amplitude is a few percent. Nothing in Wolz+ contradicts Nagura+, and nothing in Wolz+ would have detected the effect Nagura+ report. Conversely, Nagura+'s result does not undermine Wolz+'s claims *as stated* — harmonic-space bandpower covariance — but it does undercut any casual extrapolation to $\xi_\pm$. **This is the single most interesting tension to put on a slide, and neither paper cites resolving it.**

**The methods converge more than the papers admit.** Wolz+'s exact summation (`eq:cov_brute`) — a quadruple sum over distinct source quadruplets weighted by $w_iw_jw_pw_q$ — is essentially Nagura+'s *weighted quartic counts*, in harmonic space and mode-decoupled. So the estimator Nagura+ find *works* is the one Wolz+ use as ground truth and then abandon on cost grounds ($\mathcal O(N^4\ell_{\max}^4)$, sparse samples only). Both papers agree on what's true; they disagree on whether the tractable approximation to it is good enough, and they are asking that question about different data vectors.

### Hadzhiyska & White (2605.28817) — Zeldovich control variates

Orthogonal, and worth 90 seconds as the third leg of the triangle: this is the *simulation* route to covariances, made cheaper rather than replaced. Pair each mock with a Zeldovich realisation sharing initial conditions, use $Y=X-\beta(C-\mu_C)$ with analytic $\beta(k,\ell;k',\ell')$ derived under a Gaussian disconnected approximation; in the monopole the correlation is $\rho(k,k')=r^2(k)r^2(k')$ with $r$ the target–control cross-correlation coefficient. On masked redshift-space lognormal DESI-LRG mocks: **~1 order of magnitude variance reduction on the covariance for $k\lesssim0.05\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc^{-1}}$, factor 2–3 speedup at higher $k$** — $10^3$ paired sims doing the work of $10^4$.

The relation to Wolz+ is complementary, not competitive, and the interesting point is that they lean on each other's weaknesses. Wolz+ are exact where the physics is Gaussian and fail (silently) where it isn't; H&W's mocks carry nonlinearity, RSD, geometry, fibre assignment for free but converge as $\sqrt{2/N_{\rm sim}}$. Note also that H&W's analytic $\beta$ is itself derived *"under a Gaussian disconnected approximation"* — the same disconnected covariance Wolz+ compute. The natural joint move nobody has made: use an analytic catalogue-based Gaussian covariance as the control variate for a mock suite, keeping the analytic piece exact and spending simulations only on the connected residual.

## 7. Five questions for the room

1. **The one the paper can't answer.** Nagura+ find that iNKA's harmonic off-diagonals, accurate enough for bandpowers, are *not* accurate enough once you Legendre-transform to $\xi_\pm$ — on an HSC-like mask. This paper validates the same approximation on bandpowers with $\chi^2$/KS statistics that are diagonal-dominated, and checks only the first off-diagonal. **Does the catalogue-based version inherit that failure, and would this validation suite have seen it if it did?** Nothing here rules it out; the test — Legendre-transform the catalogue iNKA covariance and compare to a real-space sample covariance — is not in either paper.
2. **On $\theta_{\rm c}$.** The cloud size is a single number, $\sqrt{\theta_{\rm ip}^2+(\pi/\ell_{\max})^2}$, defended by a plateau in one realisation of one mock (Fig. 2). Real catalogues have depth-varying density. How does the covariance respond to varying $\theta_{\rm c}$ by a factor of a few — is the plateau wide, or is the prescription doing real work? And should $\theta_{\rm c}$ be a map rather than a scalar for an inhomogeneous survey?
3. **On what's actually being validated.** This is the disconnected covariance only, yet the shear test runs to $\ell=10{,}000$ where SSC and the connected trispectrum are not small. Since the mocks are Gaussian by construction, the validation cannot distinguish "the Gaussian covariance is right" from "the covariance is right". What is the intended division of labour — bolt on a halo-model connected term, and does the catalogue treatment of shot noise change how those terms should be normalised?
4. **On circularity in a real analysis.** Every headline result uses the true input $C_\ell$ for $\overline C_\ell$. NaMaster's fallback is to use the measured $\tilde C_\ell$, which correlates the data vector with its own covariance. How much accuracy is lost, and how much bias does the induced signal–covariance correlation put into parameters? An iterated-covariance test would settle it and isn't here.
5. **On the sales pitch.** The pitch for catalogue-based $C_\ell$s is escaping pixelisation. But the covariance is then computed by *re-smoothing* the catalogue with a Gaussian cloud of the mean interparticle scale — which is what a pixel window does. Have we removed the pixel or moved it from the estimator into the covariance, and is the cloud demonstrably more benign than a well-chosen $N_{\rm side}$ with an exact pixel-window correction?

## 8. Surprising

- **NKA works best exactly where you'd expect it to fail.** The narrow-kernel assumption is about mask smoothness, and a 3615-source FRB catalogue is the least smooth mask imaginable — yet iNKA matches the brute-force sum there. The resolution is in §3.2: the *sparse* limit is precisely where the covariance is dominated by self-pairs, and self-pairs are treated **exactly**. NKA is only doing the signal part, which is subdominant. The approximation is load-bearing in the *dense* regime, not the sparse one — the opposite of the intuition.
- **The $B$-mode result.** Conclusions: *"Remarkably, this is even true in regimes where the standard NKA has been shown to struggle in past analyses, such as the $B$-mode power spectrum covariance in cosmic shear. This is likely due to the much more precise (in fact, exact) treatment of the effective noise due to self-pair correlations."* A known, annoying, practical failure in weak lensing turns out to have been a shot-noise-bookkeeping problem, not a mask-geometry problem.
- **Zero mask overlap is a truncation artefact you keep on purpose.** Two disjoint catalogues have $\langle w_aw_b\rangle_{\rm sky}=0$ exactly; the *correct* answer is the one you get by *not* summing to $\ell_{\max}=\infty$. The effective overlap area is literally what survives before the incoherent high-$\ell$ oscillations cancel it. Delightful, and a good slide.
- **Self-pair corrections nest.** $\tilde N_a$ debiases the spectrum; then the noise-noise covariance term needs its own self-pair subtraction $\tilde N_{\overline{(aw_a)^2}}=\frac1{4\pi}\sum_i w_i^4a_i^4$ — fourth powers of weights and field values, from a Wick contraction of a variance map. Three layers deep in the same structure.
- **Galaxy clustering is a momentum field with $a_i=1$.** A genuinely economical unification: clustering, kSZ momentum, moving-lens stacking and shear all fall out of one covariance expression, distinguished only by what plays the role of the mask (sources vs randoms vs completeness map). That, more than the covariance itself, may be the durable contribution.
