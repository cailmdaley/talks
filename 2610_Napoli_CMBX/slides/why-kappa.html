CMB secondaries: anchors with their own systematics

What the CMB picks up on its way
  • lensing κ: all matter, $z \approx 0.5$–$5$
  • CIB: dusty star formation, $z \approx 1$–$4$
  • tSZ: hot gas in haloes, $z \lesssim 1.5$
  • no shape noise, IA or photo-z on the CMB side

Top: the n(z) of the six Euclid DR1 weak-lensing source bins, each normalised to unit area and drawn with the analysis's Hann smoothing (width 0.05). Middle: the lensing kernel $W(z)$ of each bin, from the official tomographic n(z) product (DpdBinMeanRedshift, PHZ-weighted, South release, May 2026; mean redshifts 0.45 to 1.73), in common units so the bins' heights compare, at a Planck 2018 ΛCDM background. The shear kernels peak below $z \approx 0.7$ and are gone by $z \approx 2.5$.

Bottom, at the second step: the redshift kernels of three CMB secondaries, each scaled to its own peak because their units differ. CMB lensing: the same lensing efficiency with a single source plane at last scattering, $z_* \approx 1100$, peaking at $z \approx 1.9$ and above half its peak from $z \approx 0.4$ to beyond the plotted $z = 3.$ The cosmic infrared background at 545 GHz: the bias-weighted emissivity of the Shang et al. (2012) halo model at CCL's default parameters, peaking at $z \approx 2.6$ and above half its peak from $z \approx 1.2$ to beyond $z = 3$ (to $z \approx 4.5$). The thermal Sunyaev–Zel'dovich effect: the bias-weighted mean electron pressure in a generalised-NFW halo model, peaking at $z \approx 0.3$ and above half its peak below $z \approx 1.4.$ This is not a cluster selection function: a single cluster's SZ decrement does not dim with redshift, which is why SZ cluster catalogues are close to mass-limited out to high redshift. But the tSZ signal per unit redshift, which is what a cross-correlation sees, follows the abundance of massive, hot haloes, and that falls steeply beyond $z \sim 1$; most of the y signal comes from $z \lesssim 1.5.$ Bias-weighted kernels are what a cross-correlation sees on linear scales. The CIB and tSZ shapes depend on the model, the CIB peak most of all; they show where each signal comes from, not a measurement.

Lensing and the CIB weight structure behind and across our source bins, where the shear kernels have little weight; the tSZ overlaps the low-redshift bins and traces hot gas rather than all matter. All three are measured from CMB maps, with no shape noise, intrinsic alignments or photometric redshifts on that side; their noise and foregrounds are of another kind, independent of the shear catalogue. A cross-spectrum still inherits the galaxy side's systematics: $\gamma\kappa$ carries the sources' multiplicative bias, n(z) and an intrinsic-alignment term, $\delta\kappa$ the galaxy bias.
