UNIONS 3×2pt is forecast to measure $S_8$ to 2.6 %

  • first release: $\sigma(S_8) = 0.07$
  • Contours — 68 % and 95 % of chains run on the model prediction itself: no noise is added to the data vector, so the posterior sits on the input cosmology ($S_8=0.839$, $\Omega_\mathrm{m}=0.319$, the origin of both axes) and its width is the expected error, which the covariance sets. No data vector is measured or looked at. Four tomographic source bins on 4,582 deg², alone and then with the five SPHEREx all-sky lens bins at $z=0.05$–$0.55$.
  • Widths — shear alone $\sigma(S_8)=0.0278$, $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})=0.098$, $\sigma(\sigma_8)=0.127$. 3×2pt 0.0218, 0.0379, 0.061. A second sampler seed gives 0.0273 and 0.098, and 0.0215 and 0.0381, with 16,000 effective samples each. The $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$–$S_8$ figure of merit ($1/\sqrt{\det C}$) rises by a factor 3.0. The shear-only $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ width is largely the prior's, so the gain on $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ depends on it: $h<0.91$, $\ln(10^{10}A_s)<3.66$ and $0.085<\Omega_c h^2<0.2$ all cut that posterior. They do not cut the 3×2pt one.
  • With noise — twenty GLASS mocks (lognormal matter shells, lensing, intrinsic alignments, Poisson-sampled lenses, shape noise) measured with the same masked estimator and fitted with the same chain. The median width is 0.0220 on $S_8$ and 0.0386 on $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, as forecast. The posterior means average $S_8=0.844\pm0.003$ and $\Omega_\mathrm{m}=0.321\pm0.009$ and scatter by 0.014 and 0.038; no mock is further than 1.7σ from the input. On one sky the $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ width ranges from 0.030 to 0.060.
  • Scale cuts — shear uses $300<\ell<1600$, the cut on which the first release has B-modes consistent with zero. At $20<\ell<2000$ the 3×2pt gives 0.0211 and 0.0330, so its width changes by 3 % on $S_8$ and 13 % on $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$; the lenses already supply the large-scale information. Shear alone at that cut gives 0.0235 and 0.054, and at $100<\ell<1600$ 0.0274 and 0.070 (3×2pt 0.0214 and 0.035).
  • Limits on $S_8$ — baryons first: with the feedback parameter $\log T_{\rm AGN}$ fixed, $\sigma(S_8)$ is 0.016 instead of 0.0218. Its prior is flat over the emulator's whole range (7.3–8.5) and the chain fills it, so 2.6 % is conservative there. Fixing intrinsic alignments, shear calibration, redshift shifts and magnification instead, with baryons and bias still free, gives 0.016. Most of that is source redshifts: the release's own n(z) realizations (0.006–0.012 on each bin's mean, against our 0.024) give 0.019. Halving the shear-calibration prior gives 0.021, and a Planck-width $n_s$ prior leaves the width unchanged.
  • For scale — the first release's $C_\ell$ result is $0.891^{+0.057}_{-0.084}$ (Guerrini et al.), and the DES Y3 3×2pt has $\sigma(S_8)=0.017$ and $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})=0.032$ (Abbott et al. 2022).
Inputs and their sources

Sources, v2.0.0 (the release's survey files; n(z) are not blinded): $n_{\rm eff}=0.825, 0.866, 0.942, 1.446$ arcmin⁻² (4.08 summed over the bins, 54 % of the shear weight of the full v2 sample), $\sigma_e=0.265, 0.262, 0.273, 0.277$, mean redshifts 0.29, 0.46, 0.61, 0.92.

Lenses, LSx v0.92 of Calum Murray: 8.53 million galaxies on 4,099 deg², linear bias 1.05, 1.19, 1.37, 1.35, 1.53, from $w(\theta)$ (the first-3×2pt mock-chain work). The split between bins follows the v0.92 shell counts, 0.092, 0.154, 0.152, 0.118, 0.061 arcmin⁻² (all-sky ratios, as quoted in the review of Calum's own forecast code, not counted in the UNIONS overlap). With the earlier estimated split (0.121, 0.203, 0.154, 0.074, 0.027) the chain gives 0.0220 and 0.0390. The $n(z)$ slide before shows the six bins of LSx v0.9.

Still estimated, because the v0.92 files are on a machine that could not be reached: the shape of each lens bin's redshift distribution (a top hat in estimated redshift with scatter $0.012(1+z)$; the release's own clustering redshifts put 6–13 % of each bin outside it) and the lens mask (the source footprint above $|b|=29°$, 4,068 deg²). In the Fisher forecast a scatter of 0.02 changes the 3×2pt $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})$ by +1 % and half the lens counts by +3 %, with $\sigma(S_8)$ unchanged.

Nuisances and priors

Sampled: NLA $A_{\rm IA}\sim N(0.83, 0.78)$ (the first release), $\eta_{\rm IA}\sim U(-5,5)$ with pivot redshift 0.62, input values 0.83 and 0; shear $m\sim N(0, 0.01)$ and source mean-redshift shifts $N(0, 0.024)$ per source bin (clustering-redshift means are good to 0.021–0.026); lens shifts $N(0, 0.008)$; lens magnification slope $N(0.4, 0.2)$ (not measured for LSx); bias $U(0.5, 3)$ per lens bin; HMcode-2020 $\log T_{\rm AGN}\sim U(7.3, 8.5)$; $\ln(10^{10}A_s)\sim U(1.609, 3.664)$, $\Omega_c h^2\sim U(0.085, 0.2)$, $\Omega_b h^2\sim N(0.0220, 0.00055)$, $h\sim U(0.4, 0.91)$, $n_s\sim N(0.96, 0.042)$. Fixed: one massive neutrino of 0.06 eV. Not in the model: redshift-space distortions, nonlinear bias, source clustering, lens density systematics. Lenses enter at $\ell>20$ and $k<0.2\ h/$Mpc for clustering (each bin with itself only) and $0.15$ for galaxy–galaxy lensing, where 12 of the 20 lens–source pairs are kept, those the first-3×2pt lens study found to carry signal.

Covariance and checks

Our own coupled pseudo-$C_\ell$ Gaussian covariance with the mask couplings, plus super-sample covariance from pyccl (halo-model response, variance of the background mode from the masks); the super-sample term changes the widths by under 3 %. There is no connected non-Gaussian term. Against the Gaussian part alone the twenty GLASS mocks have a mean $\chi^2$ of 314 for the 315 bands kept (252–366), and sixteen Gaussian skies through the same estimator 278–347. Before noise the GLASS mock's expectation differs from the model by $\Delta\chi^2=0.33$, mostly lens clustering at $\ell<40$, where the model's Limber approximation is up to 3 % high. Fisher widths are about 10 % narrower than the chains (3×2pt $\sigma(S_8)$ 0.0195 and $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})$ 0.0334).

The 25 November chains on Leonardo, with the estimated lens split and another super-sample code, gave 0.0219 and 0.0387 (shear alone 0.0275 and 0.0986); the shear-alone and $100<\\ell<1600$ cut variants and the nuisance-prior variants above are from that set. A reviewer who did not write the code read the likelihood configuration against these notes and found no error above 2 % in a width.

Other forecasts

Calum Murray's Fisher code, given these inputs and priors, returns 0.0208 on $S_8$ and 0.0326 on $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ (his review of 25 November), against our Fisher 0.0195 and 0.0334; its June headline of 0.0125 and 0.0152 fixed $h$, $n_s$ and $\Omega_b$ and used different sources, cuts and baryons. The earlier GLASS mock chain in real space, with stand-in lens bins and no nuisances, gave 0.0093 and 0.021. The Fisher forecast made for choosing the v2 n(z), cosmic shear alone with redshift priors near 0.01–0.02, gives 0.0113–0.0132 on $S_8$; its baryon treatment and scale range were not checked.
