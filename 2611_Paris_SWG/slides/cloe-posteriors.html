Posteriors agree to Monte Carlo precision

  • Left — shift of every parameter mean, CLOE in JAX minus Cobaya (eDR1like + CCL), in units of the Cobaya posterior σ, grouped by parameter family (cosmology includes the derived $S_8$, $\sigma_8$, $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, and on data the baryonic $\log T_\mathrm{AGN}$). Teal: GLASS mock; blue: blinded TR1. Shaded: 1σ and 2σ of the Monte Carlo error of the difference, about 0.029σ for every parameter.
  • Right — 68 % and 95 % contours of $S_8$ and $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ on the GLASS mock, each axis standardized to the Cobaya mean and σ. Star: the mock's input cosmology.
  • Reading — the shifts have the size of the Monte Carlo noise: their rms in units of that error is 1.02 on the mock and 1.01 on TR1. Parameters of one family move together (the lens $\Delta z$ on TR1, say) because they are correlated in the posterior and share one pair of chains.
  • Blinding — the TR1 chains are blinded; only standardized differences are shown.

Against the mock's input

Posterior mean minus the GLASS input, in each chain's own σ: $S_8$ −0.11 (Cobaya) and −0.10 (CLOE in JAX); $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ +0.53 and +0.56; $\sigma_8$ −0.52 and −0.54. The two likelihoods recover the input equally. The half-σ offsets of $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ and $\sigma_8$, in opposite directions along their degeneracy, are also the mean over an earlier set of chains on 20 GLASS mocks (+0.55 ± 0.17σ and −0.57 ± 0.18σ, with $S_8$ at −0.04 ± 0.19σ), read there as a projection effect of marginalising a curved posterior.
