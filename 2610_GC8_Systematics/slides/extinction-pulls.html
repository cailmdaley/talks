Obscuration would lower counts ($\delta \times E \lt 0$) and leave $\gamma$ and $\kappa$ untouched.

What is plotted

Each TR1 tracer crossed with the VMPZ GALACTICEXTINCTION_VIS template, documented as built from the Planck $E(B-V)$ map: six lens bins $\delta_i$, six LensMC source bins $\gamma_i$ (E-modes), and SPT-3G CMB lensing $\kappa$. Each point is the cross's coherent amplitude over one $\ell$ range, in units of its Gaussian error: the band pulls $C_\ell^{T,\mathrm{ext}}/\sigma_\mathrm{G}$ combined with their NaMaster Gaussian correlation matrix $R$ as $S = \mathbf{1}^\top R^{-1}\mathbf{p} / \sqrt{\mathbf{1}^\top R^{-1}\mathbf{1}}$ (this is $\sum p/\sqrt{n}$ when the bands are uncorrelated). The 15 production bands are split into three ranges of five: $100$–$311$, $311$–$965$, $965$–$3000$. Shaded: $\pm 2\sigma$. Same estimator, masks and bands as the null tests on the earlier slides; $\kappa$ uses the diagonal of its covariance in Cycle 1.

Cycle 1 (TR1 v1.1; $\delta$ coverage-only, without the visibility weights)

  • $\delta_1$ to $\delta_6$: $\chi^2$ over 15 bands = 22.9, 45.0, 36.1, 70.4, 79.7, 67.7 (full Gaussian covariance); coherent amplitude over all 15 bands $S$ = +3.6, +5.8, +5.3, +6.7, +7.9, +7.0. Above $\ell = 965$: +1.3, +3.9, +3.4, +5.8, +6.2, +5.2; below $\ell = 311$: +0.2 to +1.7.
  • $\gamma_1$ to $\gamma_4$: $\chi^2$ = 10.4, 8.9, 13.7, 7.6, $S$ = +0.1 to +1.6; $\gamma_5$, $\gamma_6$: $\chi^2$ = 18.9, 19.6, $S$ = +3.3, +3.8.
  • $\kappa$: $\chi^2$ = 19.9, $S$ = +2.9 (diagonal only).

The six $\delta$ bins share one template, so their significances are correlated, not six independent detections. The Napoli and KP2-3 decks quoted the diagonal coherent pull $\sum p/\sqrt{15}$ (+2.8 to +6.5 for $\delta$); with the band correlations the amplitudes are larger, +3.6 to +7.9.

Reading

Dust obscuration would lower galaxy counts where $E(B-V)$ is high, a negative $\delta \times E$, and could not correlate with shear or with a CMB lensing map at all. Instead every density bin, the two highest-redshift source bins and $\kappa$ are positive (source bins 1–4 are consistent with zero), the $\delta$ signal grows with $\ell$ and is weakest in the lowest-redshift lens bin, and the shear signal appears only in the highest-redshift source bins, which large-scale structure at $z \approx 1$–2 lenses most. That is the signature of large-scale structure in the template: far-infrared dust-emission maps carry the cosmic infrared background (CIB) from star-forming galaxies at $z \approx 1$–3, which trace the same structure as the Euclid samples. The next slide tests this directly. For $\kappa$ alone, Galactic dust leaking into the SPT-3G lensing reconstruction would also give a positive $\kappa \times$ dust; it cannot explain the $\delta$ and $\gamma$ rows.

With the visibility weights (Cycle 1)

Dividing $\delta$ by the VMPZ test visibility weights (slide 2) removes most of this: extinction in lens bins 2, 3, 4 and 6 goes to PTE 0.12–0.97, and only $\delta_5 \times$ extinction remains (PTE $9\times10^{-8}$). If extinction is among the visibility model's inputs, the weights remove the CIB-carried correlation by construction, along with the clustering it traces (inferred; the five SOM inputs are not named in the headers). Deprojecting the template from coverage-only $\delta$ changes the $\delta\delta$ bandpowers little ($Q_{2C}$ = 0.007–0.046, at most $0.2\sigma$ per band), but in the direction of the CIB-correlated modes: it subtracts real clustering.
