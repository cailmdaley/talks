κ sees intrinsic alignments through a different window

Intrinsic alignments are a nuisance every weak-lensing analysis has to marginalise, and the question here is how much CMB lensing helps constrain them. With NLA, IA enters each spectrum differently. Cosmic shear γγ carries a GI term, linear in $A_{\rm IA}$ and negative, and an II term, quadratic. Galaxy–galaxy lensing γδ carries a GI-like term wherever the source and lens bins overlap in redshift. Shear × CMB lensing γκ carries a single term, Iκ: the source galaxies' alignment correlated with all the matter in front of the CMB. It is linear in $A_{\rm IA}$, has no II part, and its weight in redshift differs from the GI term's because the CMB lensing kernel keeps rising to $z \sim 2$. So the IA fraction of γκ falls more slowly with source redshift than that of γγ or γδ (left: at $A_{\rm IA} = 1$, $-11\,\%$ in source bin 4 against $-5\,\%$ for γγ). This is the self-calibration idea of Hall & Taylor (2014, arXiv:1401.6018) and Troxel & Ishak (2014, arXiv:1401.7051): the κ crosses see the same IA with a different redshift lever.

What it buys on TR1 (right): a Fisher forecast on our own setup. It uses the validated band mask (605 bands), the product's Gaussian covariance and the theory vector of the pipeline. Ωm and S8 are free, with Ωb, h, ns and Σmν fixed. Six linear biases, $A_{\rm IA}$ and $\eta_{\rm IA}$ are free, with $m_i \sim N(0, 0.01)$ and $\Delta z \sim N(0, 0.02)$ marginalised; the fiducial is $A_{\rm IA} = 0.5$. Adding γκ to cosmic shear alone tightens $\sigma(A_{\rm IA})$ from 0.63 to 0.54 (15 %). Adding γκ and δκ to 3×2pt takes it from 0.31 to 0.30 (4 %), because γδ already carries most of the IA information. With a loose $m$ prior, N(0, 0.1), the numbers barely move (0.34 → 0.33 for 3×2pt), but κ then tightens $S_8$ by 20 % (0.032 → 0.026): on TR1 the κ crosses calibrate shear more than they calibrate IA. The limit is κ noise: each γκ spectrum sees the IA term at $A_{\rm IA} = 1$ at only about 0.5–0.6σ. Lower-noise CMB lensing (SO, ACT DR6 + SPT-3G combined) and the DR1 area raise this.

Where this sits in the literature. Yao et al. (2023, arXiv:2301.13437), KiDS-1000 × Planck, self-calibrated the IA in γκ and narrowed $A_{\rm IA}$ about threefold against marginalising it, though it stays consistent with zero. Robertson et al. (2021, arXiv:2011.11613), KiDS-1000 × ACT, found $A_{\rm IA}$ prior-dominated, as did the ACT × DES Y3 shear analysis (arXiv:2309.04412). In DES Y3 × SPT + Planck 5×2pt/6×2pt (arXiv:2206.10824) the IA constraint comes from the lensing ratio and γγ, not from κ. The Euclid forecast with CMB lensing (Euclid Collaboration: Ilić et al. 2022, arXiv:2106.08346, Sec. 5.1) finds a 3–16 % gain on the IA parameters: the same order as the 4 % here.

On the data, the hint is in the low source bins: in the fits to the blinded TR1 vector (results/scratch/napoli/ia_nz_fit), turning on IA lifts the γκ amplitude of source bins 1–2 from 0.54 to 0.66 of the model, and the lens ratios alone give $A_{\rm IA} = 0.1 \pm 0.7$, consistent with zero and with the joint fit.
