Cycle 1's fragmented window leaks dust power to high $\ell$

  • Left — the extinction map’s own power on Cycle 1’s template window and, same data, on the Cycle-2 window. Above ℓ ≈ 500 the Cycle-1 window keeps far more power: low-ℓ dust leaks into high ℓ.
  • Right — extinction × raw δ6, $\ell C_\ell$ with Gaussian errors, on both windows. The leaked template power inflates the high-ℓ errors and hides the signal.
  • Consequence — put on the Cycle-2 window, Cycle 1’s κ $\bar\rho$ rises 0.082 → 0.150 (Cycle 2: 0.171).
How we know it is leakage

A low-ℓ-only injection through each window (window-leakage/lowell.json) reproduces the high-ℓ excess directly. The Cycle-1 template auto is 40.9 times the rewindowed auto in the highest band. Both original windows are apodized over 0.15°. The comparison plotted is Cycle-1 tracer data with the Cycle-2 template window; the reverse direction (Cycle-2 data, Cycle-1 window) was not computed.

The cycle ladder

κ $\bar\rho$: 0.0815 ± 0.0251 (C1) → 0.1504 ± 0.0238 (C1 rewindowed) → 0.1712 ± 0.0267 (C2); the window shift accounts for 77% of the rise. For raw δ6, C1 rewindowed gives 0.1702; with C2's theory normalization 0.1364, equal to C2's 0.1364. The change is not only error bars: the crosses and the template denominator also move. This does not establish a bias in the science vector.

What is plotted

Left: deconvolved extinction auto (mag²), log axes. Right: ℓ times the raw δ6 cross for $\ell\gt300$ (lower ℓ is noise-dominated), with marginal Gaussian error bars; nothing is divided by errors. Rewindowed blocks use zero template-data Wick, the original ρ blocks observed Wick. No shared-map covariance between the two windows is saved, so their difference has no significance attached.
