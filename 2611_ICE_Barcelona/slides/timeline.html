From the last night of data to cosmology takes years

  • Wash — for each survey, the observing behind the releases shown: from the first night of data in its earliest release to the last night in its latest. Sage for galaxy surveys, butter for CMB experiments.
  • Arc — a release's last night of data (the tick) to the arXiv submission of its headline cosmology paper (the dot), one arc per release, 24 in all.
  • The reading — the gap runs from 0.7 years (KiDS-450) to 7.0 (DES Y6), about three years at the median: 3.5 for the galaxy-lensing releases, 2.0 for the CMB ones. DES's grew with each release, from 2.4 years for SV to 7.0 for Y6.
  • Representative — one headline paper per release, a selection of surveys and releases rather than all of them; dates good to within a year.
Sources

The dates are those collected in the deck's timelines.json (retrieved 17 November 2026). Observing dates are as stated in each release's own papers, or its data-release paper; paper dates are the arXiv v1 submission dates from the arXiv API. The arXiv identifiers, titles and dates were checked against arXiv on 17 November 2026.

Some observing dates are given only to the month (DES SV, the HSC releases, UNIONS, the full SPT-SZ survey) or the year (KiDS-Legacy, ACT DR4 and DR6 lensing, the SPT-SZ 2008–09 and SPT-3G seasons). A month is placed at its middle; a year that ends a release's data is placed at the year's end, so those gaps are if anything short.
