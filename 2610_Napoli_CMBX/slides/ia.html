The lens ratio sees IA but cannot yet pin it

Intrinsic alignments enter the lens ratio through $\gamma\delta$ only: sources that sit physically near the lens galaxies are aligned radially with the same tidal field, which cancels part of the tangential shear (the GI term, negative for positive $A_{\rm IA}$). $\kappa\delta$ has no IA. So IA lowers μ, most where the source bin's n(z) overlaps the lens bins.

Left: noiseless data made from the TheoryVector with NLA IA at the product pivot $z = 0.62$ and no redshift evolution, fitted with IA-free templates exactly as the data are (results/scratch/napoli/lens_ratio_response). At $A_{\rm IA} = 0.5$, μ drops by 16 % in source bin 1 and under 1 % in bin 6; at $A_{\rm IA} = 1$, by 31 % and 1.5 %. Source bin 1 sits closest behind the lens bins, so its tails overlap them most.

Right: the profile $\Delta\chi^2(A_{\rm IA})$ from the $\gamma\delta$ and $\kappa\delta$ amplitudes alone (29 items, $k_{\rm max} = 0.15\,h/$Mpc, full Gaussian covariance), with one shared μ and a free amplitude per lens bin, NLA templates (results/scratch/napoli/lens_ratio_ia/ia_profile.py). μ absorbs $m$, β absorbs bias and $\sigma_8$, and cosmology enters only through distances, so this is a cosmology-insensitive IA measurement. It gives $A_{\rm IA} = 0.05^{+0.66}_{-0.69}$ (LensMC × SPT-3G), $0.16^{+0.65}_{-0.66}$ (LensMC × ACT DR6), $0.28 \pm 0.78$ (MetaCal × SPT-3G) and $0.15^{+0.75}_{-0.80}$ (MetaCal × ACT DR6): consistent with zero and with the full fit, about 2.5 times wider. The information comes from the source-bin pattern, which a source-redshift shift can mimic; with a free μ per source bin, $A_{\rm IA}$ is unconstrained (σ ≈ 1.5).

The band is the joint fit to the full blinded 5×2pt data vector with cosmology and per-bin lens n(z) widths free (results/scratch/napoli/ia_nz_fit, model M3): $A_{\rm IA} = 0.42 \pm 0.28$, driven by γγ, γδ and γκ. With cosmology fixed at the fiducial it is $0.61 \pm 0.22$; the lens-ratio slide's templates use $A_{\rm IA} = 0.6$, and any value from 0 to 0.9 moves the shared μ by under 1.5 %.

The sharper handle is the shear-ratio test of DES Y3 (Sánchez, Prat et al. 2022, arXiv:2105.13542): galaxy–galaxy lensing ratios on small scales, where $\gamma\delta$ has far more signal, with IA and magnification modelled, constrained an IA direction and narrowed the source $\Delta z$ posteriors by 11–38 %.
