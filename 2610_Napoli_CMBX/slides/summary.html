The talk in five lines

The CMBX working group's DR1 key projects cross Euclid with the secondary anisotropies of the CMB: lensing (KP1-2, KP2-3), the thermal Sunyaev–Zel'dovich effect (KP4), the kinetic one through peculiar velocities (KP5), and further probes including the cosmic infrared background. The KP2-3 pipeline goes from catalogues to a blinded likelihood and is validated on GLASS 6×2pt mocks stage by stage; Davide Luchina's FLASK simulations will give its numerical covariance. The lens ratio $C^{\gamma\delta}/C^{\kappa\delta}$, with intrinsic alignments in the templates, checks shear calibration times source lensing efficiency at about 15 % and is consistent with 1 in all four data variants (0.85 to 0.95). On TR1 data, no shear or CMB-lensing cross with any of six survey-property templates stands out, while galaxy density correlates with extinction (in part the CIB in the dust map) and with exposure and noise in the first lens bin (a depth effect). Next come chains on TR1, the numerical covariance from the FLASK simulations, and the move to DR1, starting with galaxy clustering × CMB lensing.

What CMBX offers weak lensing

Three places where the CMB-lensing crosses touch the weak-lensing and OU-SHE work in this room, offered as openings for collaboration rather than results.

Source redshifts and shear calibration: the lens ratio $C^{\gamma_i\delta}/C^{\kappa\delta}$ measures each source bin's $(1+m_i)$ times its lensing efficiency against the CMB's, with galaxy bias and $\sigma_8$ cancelling. On TR1, with intrinsic alignments in the templates, the shared amplitude is consistent with 1 in every variant: 0.85 to 0.95, with errors of about 15 %, so 0.3 to 1σ low. The four variants share galaxies (and, per CMB map, the same κ), so sitting low together is one fluctuation counted several times, not four. Per bin the errors are 15 to 40 %, against the 0.3 to 1 % the Euclid requirements on $m$ and the bin mean redshifts allow (the lens-ratio slide); it is a consistency check now, independent of image simulations, and its precision grows with area and with the CMB map's depth.

Shear calibration: the KP1 forecast (slide "KP1: in DR3, SO lensing ≈ a perfect shear calibration") finds that adding SO lensing to Euclid 3×2pt constrains σ₈ about as well as fixing all thirteen multiplicative biases, and breaks the σ₈–m degeneracy (|corr| 0.93 → 0.27). On TR1 the same mechanism shows in a Fisher forecast on our own setup: with a loose prior on m, N(0, 0.1), adding the κ crosses to 3×2pt tightens $S_8$ by 20 % for six independent $m_i$ and by about 30 % for one shared $m$, while with a tight prior, $\sigma(m) = 0.01$, they change it by 4 % (the lens-ratio slide; results/scratch/napoli/ia_kappa).

Survey-property maps: the Planck E(B−V) extinction template correlates positively with every TR1 tracer, the opposite of dust obscuration. The direct test, swapping in Planck's CIB-cleaned GNILC dust map, shows the template is dust with a cosmic-infrared-background part ($r \approx 0.99$ with GNILC dust, 0.1–0.25 with the GNILC CIB maps). κ × template falls from +2.9σ to +0.8σ, so for CMB lensing the template's signal is CIB; δ × template falls by about a third and stays positive in every lens bin, which favours residual CIB over dust dimming. A template that carries large-scale structure must not be used to deproject, or train a selection model for, the galaxy maps. Details on the systematics slide.
