What is plotted

Each σ is the square root of the diagonal of that survey's full NaMaster Gaussian covariance, the error bars of the data-vector slide, in the 15 bandpowers from $\ell = 100$ to 3000. The twelve crosses (shear in six source bins × κ, galaxy density in six lens bins × κ) give nearly the same ratio, because the CMB-lensing noise rather than the Euclid tracer sets it; the solid line is their mean.

The dashed line scales the TR1 ratio by the overlap areas, with σ ∝ (area)$^{-1/2}$ for each survey: ratio$_\mathrm{DR1}$ = ratio$_\mathrm{TR1}\times\sqrt{(A^\mathrm{TR1}_\mathrm{ACT}/A^\mathrm{DR1}_\mathrm{ACT})(A^\mathrm{DR1}_\mathrm{SPT}/A^\mathrm{TR1}_\mathrm{SPT})} = 0.751\times$ ratio$_\mathrm{TR1}$. ACT DR6's effective overlap grows from 222 to 1069 deg², SPT-3G Main's from 130 to 353 deg² (TR1 weak-lensing coverage and DR1 VMPZ coverage, as on the footprint slide). The scaling assumes the same noise per mode and ignores mode coupling. It uses SPT-3G Main alone: the Summer fields add 751 deg² of DR1 overlap outside Main (Main and Summer together 1104 deg², more than ACT DR6), at higher lensing noise, and are not in this projection.

Why Cycle 1

This is the Cycle-1 TR1 data vector, where both error bars come from the same recipe: the full NaMaster Gaussian covariance on the data-derived spectra. In Cycle 2 the ACT DR6 covariance also models ACT's spatially varying κ noise, calibrated on 400 ACT simulations, and SPT-3G has no counterpart yet; that lowers ACT's σ relative to SPT-3G's by about 12% at every ℓ, so a Cycle-2 ratio is not like for like. The SPT-3G map is the same in both cycles, the bias- and profile-hardened GMV reconstruction. With κ alone, the Cycle-2 blinded TR1 posteriors give σ($S_8$) 0.052 with SPT-3G and 0.058 with ACT DR6.
