Extinction crosses follow the CIB, closest at 857 GHzThe CIB route, Cycle 2

The question

In Napoli the systematics slide flagged $\delta_5 \times$ extinction as the one failing template null after visibility weighting, and the summary asked whether it is CIB in the template: the Planck $E(B-V)$ extinction map is built from dust emission, and dust-emission maps carry some cosmic infrared background, which traces the same large-scale structure as the Euclid galaxies. This is the test made to answer it.

The test

If the extinction map is dust plus a CIB leak, $\mathrm{ext} = \mathrm{dust} + \epsilon\,\mathrm{CIB}$, with the dust uncorrelated with the tracers, then band by band the correlation coefficient of a tracer $T$ with extinction is fixed by two others: $\rho_\ell(T,\mathrm{ext}) = \rho_\ell(T,\mathrm{CIB})\;\rho_\ell(\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext})$, with $\rho_\ell(a,b) = C_\ell^{ab}/\sqrt{C_\ell^{aa}C_\ell^{bb}}$. Nothing is fitted. The CIB is the Lenz, Doré & Lagache (2019) 857 GHz map on its $N_\mathrm{HI} \lt 2.5\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ mask (73 % of the extinction-cross support); its auto-spectrum is from odd $\times$ even halves, so it carries no noise bias. Tracer and extinction autos are total observed power, so $\rho_\ell$ is the observed correlation, not a signal-only one. Same masks, estimator and 15 bands as the template null tests.

Error bars: Gaussian

Each measured point's bar is the NaMaster Gaussian error of the cross, $\sigma_\mathrm{G}(C_\ell^{T,\mathrm{ext}})$, the square root of the diagonal of its full Gaussian covariance, divided by the fixed $\sqrt{C_\ell^{TT}C_\ell^{ee}}$. For Gaussian fields the full propagation, which lets the two autos fluctuate with the cross, gives $\sigma(\hat\rho_\ell) = (1-\rho_\ell^2)/\sqrt{\nu_\ell}$ against $\sqrt{1+\rho_\ell^2}/\sqrt{\nu_\ell}$ with the autos fixed ($\nu_\ell$ the effective number of modes), a ratio $(1-\rho_\ell^2)/\sqrt{1+\rho_\ell^2}$. At the predicted $|\rho_\ell| \le 0.17$ (857 GHz; $\le 0.12$ at 545, $\le 0.09$ at 353) that ratio is 0.955–1, and above 0.985 wherever $|\rho_\ell| \le 0.1$, which is almost every band. So the bars are the Gaussian error of $\rho_\ell$ to within 5 %, on the conservative side. The predicted band's width is the Gaussian errors of $C^{T,\mathrm{CIB}}$, $C^{\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext}}$ and $C^{\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{CIB}}$ in quadrature. Earlier versions of this figure labelled the same quantity $r$.

What it shows at 857 GHz (Cycle 1)

The prediction reproduces the high-signal-to-noise visibility-weighted density and shear crosses, $\delta_5$ included, the one that failed its null test after visibility weighting. Summarised as measured/predicted (an inverse-variance projection with the measured errors, not part of the test) and the $\chi^2$ PTE over 15 bands with the diagonal Gaussian errors: visibility-weighted $\delta_5$ 1.26 ± 0.21 (0.80), $\delta_2$ 0.98 (0.92), $\delta_1$ 0.97 (0.94); $\gamma_6$ 0.82 ± 0.24 (0.97), $\gamma_5$ 0.90 ± 0.29 (0.92); unweighted $\delta_1$ 1.03 ± 0.38 (0.86), unweighted $\delta_6$ 0.87 ± 0.15 (0.52). $\gamma_1$ sits on a near-zero prediction. Unweighted $\delta_5$ sits above the prediction at 1.41 ± 0.20 (PTE 0.46), as do unweighted $\delta_2$ and $\delta_4$, off the slide (1.80 ± 0.34, 1.65 ± 0.25): an excess consistent with selection in the coverage-only maps (inferred). The exception is visibility-weighted $\delta_6$: 0.22 ± 0.16, PTE 0.013. The weighting removes most of the CIB-carried correlation that the unweighted map keeps, plausibly because extinction is among the visibility model's five undocumented inputs (inferred, not tested). Its worst band, $-3.4\sigma$ at $\ell \approx 550$, is not from the weighting: the unweighted $\delta_6$ dips in the same band ($-2.8\sigma$). Across the three frequencies that band stays at $-3.0$ to $-3.4\sigma$ weighted and $-2.3$ to $-2.8\sigma$ unweighted. $\delta_3$ and $\delta_4$ weighted sit low too (0.31 ± 0.30, 0.53 ± 0.27; $2.3\sigma$ and $1.7\sigma$ below 1).

So the extinction-template crosses are consistent with being tracer $\times$ CIB carried by the extinction map, which is a statement about the template, not about the Euclid shear or density. The CIB correlation grows with redshift: the predicted $\rho_\ell$ over $\ell$ = 300–2000 goes 0.03 → 0.06 → 0.09 for $\delta_1$, $\delta_5$ and unweighted $\delta_6$, and 0.003 → 0.05 → 0.06 for $\gamma_1$, $\gamma_5$, $\gamma_6$.

545 and 353 GHz

The lower-frequency maps predict less: for the high-signal-to-noise bins ($\delta_5$, $\delta_6$, $\gamma_5$, $\gamma_6$) 0.73–0.77× the 857 GHz prediction at 545 and 0.59–0.72× at 353 (lower bins span 0.64–0.83 and 0.47–0.72), so measured/predicted rises. $\delta_5$ goes 1.26 ± 0.21 → 1.63 ± 0.27 → 1.89 ± 0.32 (857 → 545 → 353), unweighted $\delta_6$ 0.87 → 1.13 → 1.29, $\gamma_6$ 0.82 → 1.09 → 1.13. The PTEs stay acceptable except in unweighted $\delta_4$, which goes 0.083 → 0.009 → 0.001 (worst band $\ell \approx 1090$, $+3.4\sigma$ at 353), and unweighted $\delta_5$ at 353 (0.042, $+2.8\sigma$ at $\ell \approx 1090$). So the CIB in the extinction map correlates with the Euclid tracers the way the 857 GHz CIB does. That fits the template: the Planck 2013 $E(B-V)$ is built from dust radiance, which the 857 GHz and IRAS 100 μm data dominate.

The drop is larger than the decoherence of the CIB between frequencies would give alone: Lenz's published spectra give a squared coherence with 857 GHz of 0.91 at 545 and 0.80 at 353 ($\ell$ = 300–2000). The rest comes from the tracers: their correlation with the CIB, $\rho_\ell(T,\mathrm{CIB})$, falls by 20–30 % from 857 to 545 GHz (about 20 % in the high-redshift bins, up to 40 % in the low ones), as expected if lower-frequency CIB comes from higher redshift than the Euclid samples (inferred).

The 353 GHz map carries no primary CMB to bias this: its odd $\times$ even auto-spectrum matches Lenz's published 353 $\times$ 353 spectrum as closely as the 857 one does (within 10–30 % at $\ell$ = 150–1700, no band more than $2\sigma$ off), where primary CMB would be about $10^3$ times the CIB.

Caveats

  • The PTEs are conservative: they use the diagonal of each Gaussian covariance and ignore the measured–predicted covariance (shared tracer noise and sky), and most sit high (0.8–0.98), as expected.
  • The template-cross estimator leaks the $\ell \lt 100$ modes of steep-spectrum maps into the science bands (a GNILC dust map cut to $\ell \le 99$ shows order-unity apparent power at $\ell$ = 550–2700). Template crosses stay valid as nulls, but the $\ell$ of a detection and a dust/CIB split read off single bands wait on closing that low-$\ell$ response.
  • On its own the shape of a tracer $\times$ extinction cross cannot separate CIB from dust ($\Delta\chi^2 \le 3$ per bin); the correlation-coefficient test is what discriminates.
  • The ratios share maps across bins and frequencies, so they are correlated, and their errors omit the prediction's own uncertainty.
  • Checks behind it: the 857/545 GHz ratio of CIB $\times$ extinction (1.9–2.2) matches the CIB SED (1.95), not dust (3.06); the Lenz 857 odd $\times$ even auto reproduces the published spectrum to about 10 % for $\ell$ = 150–1700 on the TR1 footprint.
