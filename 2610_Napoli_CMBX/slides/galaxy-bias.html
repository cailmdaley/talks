δκ and δδ measure galaxy bias without $\sigma_8$

  • in δδ / δκ, $\sigma_8$ cancels but $\Omega_{\rm m}$ doesn't: $b$ stays blinded
  • errors from the full covariance; one to five δκ bands per bin
  • bins 4 and 5 sit high: under investigation

Galaxy bias per lens bin from the ratio of two amplitudes, $b = (C^{\delta\delta}/T^{\delta\delta}) / (C^{\delta\kappa}/T^{\delta\kappa}),$ each fitted against a unit-bias template with the full Gaussian covariance of the blinded TR1 × SPT-3G data vector. On linear scales $\delta\delta \propto b^2\sigma_8^2$ and $\delta\kappa \propto b\,\sigma_8^2,$ so $\sigma_8$ cancels, without needing galaxy–galaxy lensing. $\Omega_{\rm m}$ does not cancel: the CMB-lensing kernel carries a factor $\Omega_{\rm m}$ that the clustering auto-spectrum does not, so $b \propto \Omega_{\rm m}^{-(0.5\text{–}0.8)},$ and a cosmology shift the size of the blind moves it by ×0.80 to ×1.41 at fixed true bias. So this bias is blinded along with the cosmology, and the black lines are the fiducial biases (the GLASS mock inputs), not a fit.

Scale cut: $\delta\delta$ at $\ell \lt k_{\rm max}\chi$ with $k_{\rm max} = 0.2\,h/$Mpc and $\delta\kappa$ at $0.15\,h/$Mpc, the split that a nonlinear-bias contamination test validates (a $0.22\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ shift in $(S_8, \Omega_{\rm m})$ against 0.52 with 0.2 everywhere). That leaves one to five $\delta\kappa$ bands per bin.

Every bin is drawn alike: the point is the median and the bar the 16–84 % interval of $b$ over Monte Carlo draws of the two amplitudes from their joint Gaussian covariance, so the ratio's skew shows up as an asymmetric bar. With SPT-3G, $b$ = 0.49, 1.23, 1.09, 1.76, 3.08 and 1.89 in lens bins 1 to 6, against fiducial 1.13, 1.17, 1.32, 1.37, 1.50 and 1.71. Two cautions on the bars. Where $\delta\kappa$ is weak, the interval is conditioned on the measured denominator: lens bin 1 keeps a single $\delta\kappa$ band at $\ell \approx 112$ with S/N 2.8 in the data but about 1 expected on the mocks, where $b/b_{\rm true}$ in that bin ranges from −1.3 to 7.7 over ten seeds; its short bar understates the uncertainty. And the ratio is biased high at low S/N: on the mocks it returns $b$ 15–21 % high in bins 4 and 5 at S/N 3–4.

Bins 4 and 5 come out high. That tracks the $\delta\delta$ auto-spectra, which sit high against the fiducial by a constant factor in ℓ (one fitted amplitude per pair: autos 1.0 to 1.7), while crosses between non-adjacent lens bins sit low (0.4 to 0.6) and $\delta\kappa$ sits near 1. That pattern points at less inter-bin n(z) tail overlap than the delivered n(z) predict, not a higher bias (which would raise $\delta\kappa$ and the crosses too); magnification at the measured flux slopes does not explain it, and it is under investigation. Because this ratio estimator assumes the fiducial n(z), it reads an n(z) error as bias. The mocks are lognormal with linear bias, so they validate the estimator, not the scale cut.

The variant control switches the CMB lensing map; the shear method does not enter $b$, so LensMC and MetaCal give identical points. With ACT DR6, $b$ = 0.84, 1.06, 1.22, 1.42, 2.44 and 2.02, with $\delta\kappa$ S/N 1.6 to 4.5 per bin against 1.9 to 5.0 with SPT-3G. The two maps share the Euclid side, so their points are correlated and their agreement is not an independent check. These are exploratory estimators (results/scratch/napoli/crosses), not an lc output.
