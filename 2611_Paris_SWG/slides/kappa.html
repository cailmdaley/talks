Should κκ be blinded with the crosses?

  • For: one shift on every spectrum keeps the likelihood self-consistent
  • Shifted crosses against an unshifted κκ: the tension points to the shift
  • But: ACT DR6 and SPT-3G κκ constraints are public
  • Blinding κκ hides the combination, not κκ alone

And for sizing: σ of the crosses, or of crosses + κκ?

  • Self-consistency — the CMBX self-blind applies one cosmology shift (theory at the blind cosmology minus theory at the fiducial) to every spectrum it writes. If κκ entered unshifted, the blinded crosses and the true κκ would pull toward different cosmologies, and the direction of that internal tension would reveal the direction of the shift.
  • Residual leak — the ACT DR6 and SPT-3G κκ likelihoods and their κκ-only constraints are public, so the κκ-only answer stays known whatever we do. What blinding κκ protects is the joint constraint; the discipline that remains is not reading κκ-only results against the blinded crosses.
  • Sizing — κκ tightens σ($S_8$) substantially, so the choice of whose σ sets the envelope (crosses, or crosses + κκ) changes its size. JC-2's 2×2pt test shows what happens when a shift is large in units of the tighter probe's σ: the posterior is blinded "much too strongly" and hits the prior.
Related decision

How κκ enters the likelihood at all is open: at the CMBX telecon of 5 November the group leaned to keeping the external κκ likelihood and conditioning the rest of the likelihood and covariance on it, with a decision wanted by the end of November. A blind of κκ has to fit whichever form is chosen.
