How big should the blind be?

A shift sized on one probe can over-blind another

Flagship-2 test: a shift 1σ from the WL best fit

  • WL recovers it
  • 2×2pt is tighter: several σ, hits the prior
  • A weaker probe would barely move

  • The tension — one shift in cosmology is a different number of σ for each probe. Sized for a weak probe it is several σ for a tight one, which is pushed against its prior (2×2pt here). Sized for a tight probe it is a fraction of a σ for a weak one, which is then hardly blinded: its posterior still shows where it would land. The second half is my inference, not part of Keil's test.
  • For CMBX — the κ crosses are the weak probe: on a TR1-sized GLASS mock they constrain $S_8$ to ±0.033 against ±0.013 for 3×2pt (2.6 times wider). If the shift is drawn from a posterior, whose posterior sets the size: 3×2pt, 6×2pt, or 6×2pt with κκ, which is tighter still?
CMBX in units of σ

The CMBX envelope today is ±0.075 in $S_8$. The blinded TR1 chain on the full vector (κ crosses and 3×2pt, SPT-3G κ) has posterior width σ($S_8$) ≈ 0.016. TR1 is about 30 % of DR1, so DR1 σ($S_8$) ≈ 0.009 and 2σ ≈ 0.02 (an estimate, not computed): the current envelope is three to four times the ~2σ other groups aim for. Adding κκ is expected to tighten σ further (not computed). The CMBX GLASS mock chains can give the DR1 posterior directly.

Why too large is a problem

The shift is computed at fixed nuisances, so it is exact only to first order (Muir et al. 2020, App. A). On a CMBX GLASS mock, a blind at 82 % of the $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ envelope narrowed the $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ posterior threefold; a blind at 44 % translated cleanly. The 2×2pt failure above is the same effect.

Why too small is a problem

If the envelope is about the size of the $S_8$ tension, a blinded offset reads as "that is probably the tension", and that reading leaks into analysis choices.
