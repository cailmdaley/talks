Correlating extinction with Euclid tracers

$\displaystyle\rho_\ell^{X,\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{C_\ell^{X\,\mathrm{ext}}}{\sqrt{C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}\,C_\ell^{\mathrm{ext\,ext}}}},\qquad \bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{ext}}:\ \text{GLS mean over } 100\lt\ell\lt3000$

If extinction carries LSS only through CIB: $\;\bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{ext}}\approx\bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{CIB}}\,\bar\rho_{\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext}}$

  • $\bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{ext}}$ — each tracer’s correlation coefficient with the extinction map: the cross normalized by the tracer’s theory signal and the template’s measured auto, GLS-averaged over $100\lt\ell\lt3000$.
  • Solid blue — measured: δ with visibility correction; γE and κ carry no visibility weights. Nearly all positive; κ cannot be obscured by dust, so this is LSS in the map. Space flips to Cycle 2: every tracer at ρ̄ ≈ 0.05–0.18.
  • Open blue (space) — predicted from tracer × CIB-857 and CIB × extinction alone, never using tracer × extinction. Cycle 2: tracks δ2–δ6 and γE2–γE6; δ1 and γE1 sit above it. Cycle 1: visibility-corrected δ3, δ4, δ6 fall well below it (δ4 0.015 vs 0.036, δ6 0.016 vs 0.060).
  • Orange (space) — δ before visibility correction. Cycle 2: the correction moves ρ̄ by ≲ 1σ (δ2 0.099 → 0.089). Cycle 1: it removes most of it (δ4 0.072 → 0.015, δ6 0.069 → 0.016), more than the CIB route accounts for.
How $\bar\rho$ is computed

All points compress the same range, $100\lt\ell\lt3000$, into a GLS constant-ρ mean with its error, using the full Gaussian cross blocks divided by the normalization outer product. $C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}$ is the release-specific fiducial tracer signal model (no shot, shape or reconstruction noise), averaged uniformly over the science bins; $C_\ell^{\mathrm{ext\,ext}}$ is the measured template auto. This model-normalized estimate avoids clipping negative noise-subtracted shear autos; it is not bounded to [−1, 1] in a noisy realization, and the denominator’s uncertainty is not propagated. The GLS mean is a descriptive compression, not a fitted physical model.

How the CIB prediction is computed

$\bar\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}=\langle\rho_{X,\mathrm{CIB}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext}}\rangle$, the bandwise product averaged with the measured GLS weights (the slide’s product of averages is a schematic of this). For δ the prediction uses the visibility-corrected δ × CIB, matching the solid points. CIB is the Lenz et al. 857 GHz map; its auto is the odd-ring × even-ring spectrum with the cached interpolation transfer correction. No CMB-κ × CIB cross is saved, so there is no κ prediction. Input cross-covariances and denominator scatter are omitted, so no prediction-residual significance is claimed.

What the comparison does and does not show

The δ1/γE1 excess in Cycle 2 is descriptive. There is no monotonic-redshift claim; Cycle-1 low-z shear means are mostly within one or two Gaussian errors of zero, and high-z shear and κ carry the clearer support. Extinction cannot directly obscure a CMB-lensing reconstruction; the joint sign, high-z shear and CIB-route comparison support the LSS interpretation. This does not rule out real Galactic selection effects or CMB reconstruction foreground response. Only the cycle toggle is active: matched MetaCal/ACT ρ normalizations and route predictions have not been saved.
