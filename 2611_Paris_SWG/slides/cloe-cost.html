A full chain takes 32 minutes with CLOE in JAX

  • Left — core-seconds per full-vector evaluation on a Leonardo DCGP node (Sapphire Rapids). CLOE + CAMB 22.6 s and CLOE + emulator 8.0 s: medians over 5 prior draws on one core (the first of 6 excluded as warm-up). Projection only (a nuisance-only step, $P(k)$ not recomputed) 5.3 s, the floor for any plain CLOE evaluation. eDR1like + CCL about 32 s, measured over the points of a production chain (111 workers on 112 cores). CLOE in JAX: 4 ms of node time per point in the full chain, which is at most 0.45 core-s if every one of the 112 cores was busy.
  • Right — wall time of one full chain on one node. Solid: measured, the blinded TR1 × SPT-3G full-vector chain with CLOE in JAX, 32 min (38 parameters, 247 000 points evaluated in 247 calls of 1000, effective sample size 2714). Of those, 16.4 min are likelihood and 15.9 min everything else, mostly nautilus itself; the ACT DR6 chain is 33 min with the same split. Hatched: estimated, not run. A nautilus chain calling plain CLOE needs about 200 000–250 000 evaluations, so 4–5 h with the emulator and 11–14 h with CAMB, assuming perfect parallel efficiency.
  • Why the emulator alone gains only 3× — the Theory WG quotes about 200× for its emulator against CAMB; in this 651-band vector CLOElib's angular projection then dominates the cost.
Emulator accuracy

CLOElib's CosmoPower-JAX HMCode-2020 emulator (the Theory WG network) agrees with CLOE + CAMB to 0.006σ per band at five of the six draws and 0.042σ at the sixth. The GLASS mocks use dark-matter-only HMCode-2020, which the emulators do not provide, so the emulator is compared at the theory level on the data configuration, not in a chain.
