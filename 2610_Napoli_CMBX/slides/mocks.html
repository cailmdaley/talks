Two kinds of mocks for two jobs

GLASS 6×2pt
validate the pipeline

  • correlated Euclid and CMB-lensing fields from one set of matter shells
  • run through the production pipeline: same masks, noise and code as the data
  • check every stage: bandpowers, covariance against scatter, blind, likelihood
  • fast and flexible: any footprint, n(z) or CMB noise

FLASK lognormal
the numerical covariance

  • 400 realisations; 3×2pt constrained on ACT DR6 simulations, and Planck-like with their own lensing reconstruction
  • calibrated on DEMNUni N-body simulations
  • non-Gaussian and with real reconstruction noise: more accurate
  • Planck-constrained version next

The key project uses two kinds of simulation, for different jobs. GLASS 6×2pt mocks draw lognormal matter shells and derive from the same shells the Euclid galaxy density and shear fields and the CMB-lensing convergence, so every cross-correlation in the data vector is present in the mocks. They go through the production pipeline with the data's masks and noise and check each stage of it: that the bandpowers recover the input spectra, that the Gaussian covariance matches the seed-to-seed scatter (over 10 seeds the bulk agrees, while the stiffest low-$\ell$ clustering modes scatter about 1.3 times more than the covariance allows), that the blind behaves (a rehearsal on a mock), and that the likelihood recovers the input cosmology. They are quick to regenerate for a new footprint, n(z) or CMB noise level, which is what pipeline validation needs.

FLASK lognormal simulations, run by Davide Luchina with Carlo Baccigalupi, Matteo Calabrese, Melita Carbone, Sandeep Haridasu and Pauline Vielzeuf in the simulations-and-covariance workstream, are for the numerical covariance. As shown at the KP2-3 telecon of 8 October 2026: 400 simulations, 3×2pt lognormals constrained on the ACT DR6 Gaussian simulations, and full lognormals in a Planck-like configuration with their own lensing reconstruction; the Planck-constrained version is in preparation. The lognormal fields are calibrated on the DEMNUni N-body simulations, and constraining on the CMB experiments' own simulations carries their reconstruction noise into the covariance, which is why this covariance is expected to be more accurate than the analytic Gaussian one.
