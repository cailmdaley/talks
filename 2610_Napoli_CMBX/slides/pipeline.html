Data vectors and systematics

Euclid TR1 shear and positions are pixelised into maps with their masks and redshift distributions; the CMB-lensing inputs are the Planck PR4, ACT DR6 and SPT-3G Main convergence maps. NaMaster pseudo-$C_\ell$ turns them into bandpowers for all six families: the three Euclid 3×2pt spectra (left column) and the three involving CMB lensing (right column). The same estimator crosses the data with systematics maps, the templates carried by Euclid's VMPs (extinction, exposures, noise, PSF, zodiacal light, stars) and external CMB foreground maps, which gives the null tests and validation of the data vectors.

Simulations and covariance

GLASS and FLASK lognormal mocks on the TR1 footprint with the delivered n(z) run through the same NaMaster step as the data. GLASS validates the pipeline (the mock-recovery slide); FLASK, calibrated on DEMNUni N-body simulations, gives the numerical covariance. The covariance the pipeline uses now is analytic and Gaussian, in NaMaster's improved narrow-kernel approximation (iNKA), and covers every block including the cross-blocks between families.

Theory and likelihood, and the blind

The blind is a parameter-shift blind in the style of Muir et al. (2019): a sealed shift in cosmology is turned into a $\Delta C_\ell$ added to the whole six-family data vector, so shapes, signal-to-noise, the covariance and the null tests stay visible while the inferred cosmology is hidden. We run it ourselves because the official pipeline blinds only the Euclid 2pt spectra; it is the pipeline's own step, shared across the workstreams. The theory model computes the six families with CCL and passes them through the same bandpower windows as the data. The likelihood varies it with the nuisances: linear galaxy bias $b$, photometric-redshift shifts $\Delta z$, shear calibration $m$ and intrinsic alignments. The blinded TR1 likelihood input exists; the TR1 chains come next.
