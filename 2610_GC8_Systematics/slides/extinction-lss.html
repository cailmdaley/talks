The CIB route predicts tracer–extinction $\bar\rho$

If the template’s LSS enters only through CIB:
$\bar\rho(X,\mathrm{ext})\approx\bar\rho(X,\mathrm{CIB})\,\bar\rho(\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext})$

The points show density without visibility correction, LensMC shear E and SPT-3G kappa in both cycles. All compress the same full range, $100\lt\ell\lt3000$, into a GLS constant-rho mean with its error. The definition is $\rho_\ell=C_\ell^{XT}/\sqrt{C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}C_\ell^{TT}}$. The slide's product of averaged coefficients is a schematic reduction, not the exact compression: the plotted prediction is the bandwise product averaged with the measured GLS weights, which need not equal the product of separate averages. It predicts tracer–extinction from tracer–CIB and CIB–extinction, without using the tracer–extinction cross in the prediction amplitude; it does use the measured extinction auto and GLS weights. The density-bin-1 excess is descriptive, not a quantified prediction-residual significance. Cycle-1 δ2–δ5 also lie above the CIB prediction; why remains open, because the window/theory ladder explains the cycle shift rather than decomposing the measured-minus-CIB residual. Only the cycle toggle is active because matched MetaCal/ACT rho normalizations and route predictions have not been saved. Positive means are not a claim that every band is positive, or that every tracer rejects zero. There is no monotonic-redshift claim: Cycle-1 density bin 6 is below bin 5. Cycle-1 low-redshift shear means are mostly within one or two Gaussian errors of zero; high-redshift shear and kappa carry the clearer support.

In these exploratory figures, $C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}$ is the release-specific fiducial tracer signal model, with no shot, shape or reconstruction noise, averaged uniformly over exact integer science bins. It is neither an observed total auto nor the exact masked-auto bandpower-window average. $C_\ell^{TT}$ is the measured template auto; for CIB comparisons it is the odd-ring × even-ring spectrum with the cached interpolation transfer correction. The normalization is held fixed in the error propagation; the errors are the full Gaussian cross blocks divided by the normalization outer product. This is a model-normalized signal-correlation estimate; it avoids clipping negative noise-subtracted shear autos. It is not bounded to lie within [-1,1] in a noisy realization. No denominator uncertainty is included. The GLS mean is a descriptive compression, not a fitted physical constant-rho model.

Extinction cannot directly obscure a CMB-lensing reconstruction; the joint sign, high-redshift shear and CIB-route comparison support the LSS interpretation. This does not rule out real Galactic selection effects or CMB reconstruction foreground response. B-mode evidence is from the backup scorecard, not a rho normalization for B.
