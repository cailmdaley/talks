CMB lensing checks shear × n(z) to 15–20 %

Lens ratio $\mu_i = C^{\gamma_i\delta}/C^{\kappa\delta}$ ÷ fiducial
$\propto (1+m_i) \times$ n(z) efficiency

  • bias and $\sigma_8$ cancel; a blind shift moves μ by ×0.98–1.04
  • mocks: shared $\mu = 1.03 \pm 0.06$
  • TR1 × SPT-3G: shared $\mu = 0.88^{+0.19}_{-0.13},$ 0.6σ low; the six bins share κδ, so they are correlated
  • a consistency check, not a calibration; ACT to come

The lens ratio compares how strongly a Euclid source bin is lensed with how strongly the CMB is lensed, by the same foreground galaxies. For source bin $i$ and a lens bin $j$ in front of it, $C^{\gamma_i\delta_j}$ and $C^{\kappa\delta_j}$ share the galaxy bias of lens bin $j$ and the matter clustering at its redshift; their ratio is the ratio of the two lensing efficiencies at the lens redshift, times $(1+m_i)$ for the source bin's multiplicative shear bias. The fit gives each lens bin a free amplitude $\beta_j$ (absorbing bias and $\sigma_8$) and each source bin one amplitude $\mu_i$ relative to the fiducial theory, using lens bins at least 0.2 in mean redshift in front of the source bin. So $\mu_i$ is a $(1+m_i)$ × n(z)-efficiency amplitude: it moves if the shear calibration is off, or if the source n(z) puts the wrong fraction of galaxies at high redshift, and it cannot tell those apart. Because the cosmology cancels in the ratio, it is nearly blind-immune: over the public blinding envelope ($\pm 0.075$ in $S_8,$ $\pm 0.1$ in $\Omega_{\rm m}$) a cosmology shift moves the per-bin $\mu_i$ by ×0.98 to ×1.04 and the shared μ by ×0.98 to ×1.02, the pale bars in the figure.

Scale cut: both $\gamma\delta$ and $\kappa\delta$ keep $\ell \lt k_{\rm max}\chi(\bar z_j)$ with $k_{\rm max} = 0.15\,h/$Mpc, so ℓ below 128 to 310 depending on the lens bin. That is the cut the nonlinear-bias contamination test validates: a noiseless data vector with perturbative nonlinear bias, fitted with our linear-bias model, shifts $(S_8, \Omega_{\rm m})$ by $0.22\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ with $\delta\delta$ at 0.2 and the crosses at 0.15, against $0.52\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ with 0.2 everywhere. With 0.2 everywhere the shared μ was $0.87^{+0.15}_{-0.11}$; the stricter cut costs about a quarter of the precision and moves the centre by less than 0.1σ.

First step: the estimator on ten GLASS 6×2pt mocks (lognormal, linear bias, $m = 0,$ ACT-like κ noise on the TR1 footprint), where the truth is 1. Dots are the ten seeds, points the median with the robust seed scatter divided by $\sqrt{10}.$ Per-bin medians are 0.96 to 1.05 and the shared μ is $1.03 \pm 0.06$ (mean of seeds), so the estimator is unbiased at this precision. The seed scatter is 0.96 to 1.39 times the propagated per-bin error (1.13 for the shared μ, coverage 58 %), so the propagated errors may be 10 to 30 % too small; read the data errors with that in mind.

Second step: TR1 × SPT-3G data, through the blinded likelihood input and its full Gaussian covariance. Per bin, μ is 0.79 to 1.07 with errors of 15 to 40 %. The six points are strongly correlated, because every source bin is divided by the same $\kappa\delta_j$ spectra: a fluctuation in $\kappa\delta_j$ moves every $\mu_i$ that uses lens bin $j$ the same way, so five points below 1 are not five independent low points. The fit with one shared amplitude gives $\mu = 0.88$ ($-0.13,$ $+0.19$), 0.6σ below 1. That is a 15–20 % consistency check of shear calibration times n(z) efficiency, an order of magnitude looser than the ~1 % image-simulation priors on $m$: CMB lensing does not calibrate $m$ here, it checks that nothing is grossly wrong. Caveats: SPT-3G only, the ACT product is pending; the mocks are ACT-like, so they test the estimator, not SPT's noise; intrinsic alignments of the sources enter $\gamma\delta$ and are not modelled; and these are exploratory estimators (results/scratch/napoli/crosses), not an lc output.
