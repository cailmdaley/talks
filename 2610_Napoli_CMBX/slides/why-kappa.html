CMB secondaries: anchors with their own systematics

What the CMB picks up on its way
  • lensing κ: all matter, $z \approx 0.5$–$5$
  • CIB: dusty star formation, $z \approx 1$–$4$
  • tSZ: hot gas in haloes, $z \lesssim 1$
  • no shape noise, IA or photo-z on the CMB side

Bottom: the lensing kernel $W(z)$ of each of the six Euclid DR1 weak-lensing source bins, from the official tomographic n(z) product (DpdBinMeanRedshift, PHZ-weighted, South release, May 2026; mean redshifts 0.45 to 1.73), in common units so the bins' heights compare, at a Planck 2018 ΛCDM background. The shear kernels peak below $z \approx 0.7$ and are gone by $z \approx 2.5$.

Top, at the second step: the redshift kernels of three CMB secondaries, each scaled to its own peak because their units differ. CMB lensing: the same lensing efficiency with a single source plane at last scattering, $z_* \approx 1100$, peaking at $z \approx 1.9$ and above half its peak from $z \approx 0.4$ to beyond $z = 5.$ The cosmic infrared background at 545 GHz: the bias-weighted emissivity of the Shang et al. (2012) halo model at CCL's default parameters, peaking at $z \approx 2.6$ and above half its peak over $z \approx 1.2$–$4.5.$ The thermal Sunyaev–Zel'dovich effect: the bias-weighted mean electron pressure in a generalised-NFW halo model, peaking at $z \approx 0.3$ and above half its peak below $z \approx 1.4.$ Bias-weighted kernels are what a cross-correlation sees on linear scales. The CIB and tSZ shapes depend on the model, the CIB peak most of all; they show where each signal comes from, not a measurement.

Lensing and the CIB weight structure behind and across our source bins, where the shear kernels have little weight; the tSZ overlaps the low-redshift bins and traces hot gas rather than all matter. All three are measured from CMB maps, with no shape noise, intrinsic alignments or photometric redshifts on that side; their noise and foregrounds are of another kind, independent of the shear catalogue. A cross-spectrum still inherits the galaxy side's systematics: $\gamma\kappa$ carries the sources' multiplicative bias, n(z) and an intrinsic-alignment term, $\delta\kappa$ the galaxy bias.
