The CIB route predicts the extinction correlation

If extinction carries LSS only through CIB: $\;\bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{ext}}\approx\bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{CIB}}\,\bar\rho_{\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext}}$

  • $\bar\rho_{X,\mathrm{ext}}$ — each tracer’s correlation coefficient with the extinction map, $\rho_\ell=C_\ell^{X\,\mathrm{ext}}/\sqrt{C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}C_\ell^{\mathrm{ext\,ext}}}$, GLS-averaged over $100\lt\ell\lt3000$.
  • Filled points — measured. Nearly all positive; κ cannot be obscured by dust, so this is LSS in the map.
  • Open points (next step) — predicted from tracer × CIB-857 and CIB × extinction alone, never using tracer × extinction.
  • Read — in Cycle 2 the prediction tracks δ2–δ6 and γE2–γE6, with δ1 and γE1 above it; in Cycle 1, δ2–δ5 sit above it (open question).
How $\bar\rho$ is computed

All points compress the same range, $100\lt\ell\lt3000$, into a GLS constant-ρ mean with its error, using the full Gaussian cross blocks divided by the normalization outer product. $C_\ell^{XX,\mathrm{th}}$ is the release-specific fiducial tracer signal model (no shot, shape or reconstruction noise), averaged uniformly over the science bins; $C_\ell^{\mathrm{ext\,ext}}$ is the measured template auto. This model-normalized estimate avoids clipping negative noise-subtracted shear autos; it is not bounded to [−1, 1] in a noisy realization, and the denominator’s uncertainty is not propagated. The GLS mean is a descriptive compression, not a fitted physical model.

How the CIB prediction is computed

$\bar\rho_{\mathrm{pred}}=\langle\rho_{X,\mathrm{CIB}}\,\rho_{\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext}}\rangle$, the bandwise product averaged with the measured GLS weights (the slide’s product of averages is a schematic of this). CIB is the Lenz et al. 857 GHz map; its auto is the odd-ring × even-ring spectrum with the cached interpolation transfer correction. Saved CIB crosses cover density and all six shear E bins in both cycles (compute/rho_ladder.py). No CMB-κ × CIB cross is saved, so there is no κ prediction. Input cross-covariances and denominator scatter are omitted, so no prediction-residual significance is claimed.

What the comparison does and does not show

The density-bin-1 excess is descriptive. Cycle-1 δ2–δ5 also lie above the prediction; why remains open. There is no monotonic-redshift claim: Cycle-1 density bin 6 is below bin 5. Cycle-1 low-z shear means are mostly within one or two Gaussian errors of zero; high-z shear and κ carry the clearer support. Extinction cannot directly obscure a CMB-lensing reconstruction; the joint sign, high-z shear and CIB-route comparison support the LSS interpretation. This does not rule out real Galactic selection effects or CMB reconstruction foreground response. Only the cycle toggle is active: matched MetaCal/ACT ρ normalizations and route predictions have not been saved.
