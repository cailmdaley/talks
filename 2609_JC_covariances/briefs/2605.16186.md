# Nagura, Terasawa, Terawaki & Takada 2026 — analytic covariance of the cosmic shear 2PCF
arXiv:2605.16186 (Kavli IPMU + Chiba). Source: `~/Documents/projects/talks/2609_JC_covariances/papers/2605.16186/`

## 1. Thesis in plain words

If you want an analytic Gaussian covariance for a real-space two-point function on a masked sky, one tempting route is: compute the covariance of the *pseudo*-$C_\ell$ analytically with the improved Narrow Kernel Approximation (iNKA) — the fast, standard, NaMaster-implemented thing — and then Legendre-project it into $\theta$ bins, because $\xi(\theta)$ is a linear transform of the windowed $\tilde C_\ell$. This paper builds that machinery carefully (spin-0 in the main text, spin-2 $\xi_\pm$ in the appendix), validates every link in the chain against 1000 Gaussian realizations on the HSC-Y3 mask, and finds that **the route fails**. Not marginally: the projected iNKA covariance undershoots the simulated $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi,\xi)$ diagonal by roughly 25–30% at $\theta\sim 6'$. The diagnosis is that iNKA is built to be right on the *diagonal* of $\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C_\ell,\tilde C_{\ell'})$ — where it is good to 10% — but the Legendre projection is a double sum over $\ell,\ell'$ that eats the off-diagonal structure, where iNKA's errors live and are unconstrained by design. The control experiment is decisive: project the *simulated* harmonic covariance through the same machinery and you recover the simulated $\xi$ covariance essentially exactly, so the transform is fine and the input covariance is what's wrong. As an alternative they resurrect the Schneider et al. (2002) / Shirasaki et al. (2019) weighted quartic-counts estimator — a configuration-space, pair-counting object that knows the geometry exactly — recast in a fast matrix form, and show it tracks the simulations well where iNKA and $f_{\rm sky}$ both miss. Moral: harmonic-space diagonal accuracy is not sufficient for real-space covariance.

## 2. The machinery

All equation labels are from `source/main.tex`.

### (a) The window is a mode-coupling matrix — `eq:cell_to_tildecell`

$$\tilde C_\ell = \sum_{\ell'} \mathcal{M}_{\ell\ell'}(w,w)\, C_{\ell'}$$

*Intuition:* masking multiplies in real space, so it convolves in harmonic space; $\mathcal{M}$ is the exact convolution kernel built from the mask's power spectrum via Wigner-3j. *Assumption:* none beyond $\tilde\kappa = w\kappa$ — this is exact, and Fig. 1 confirms it at the mean level. It sets up the whole paper: everything downstream is a statement about *windowed* spectra, not deconvolved ones.

### (b) $\xi$ is a linear map of the *windowed* spectrum — `eq:tildexi_tildecell_binned`, `eq:xi_window-xi_relation_binned`

$$\tilde\xi_i = 2\pi\sum_\ell \big[P_{\ell+1}(x)-P_{\ell-1}(x)\big]_{\cos\theta_{i,+}}^{\cos\theta_{i,-}}\,\tilde C_\ell,\qquad \xi_i = \frac{n^2}{N_{\mathrm{pair},i}}\,\tilde\xi_i$$

*Intuition:* this is the key structural fact, from Friedrich et al. (2020) App. C. The pair-counting estimator of $\xi$ is *unbiased* — the mask doesn't bias it — but its natural harmonic representation is in terms of $\tilde C_\ell$, with the normalisation supplied by the actual pair counts $N_{\mathrm{pair},i}$ in the masked footprint rather than by the idealised $n^2 \times$ solid angle. So the geometry enters twice: through $\tilde C_\ell$ and through $N_{\rm pair}$. The $[P_{\ell+1}-P_{\ell-1}]$ bracket is the bin-averaged Legendre polynomial via the recursion. *Assumption:* flat weights within a bin, a survey-averaged $n$; the paper calls it "non-trivial" and validates it in Fig. 2. The spin-2 analogue (App. A, `eq:app_xip_final`/`eq:app_xim_final`) replaces $P_\ell$ with Wigner $d^\ell_{22}$, $d^\ell_{2,-2}$ and $\tilde C_\ell \to \tilde C^{EE}_\ell \pm \tilde C^{BB}_\ell$.

### (c) The projection to covariance — `eq:maskedCellCovariance_projection`

$$\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_i,\xi_j) = \frac{n^4}{N_{\mathrm{pair},i}N_{\mathrm{pair},j}}(2\pi)^2 \sum_{\ell,\ell'} \big[P_{\ell+1}-P_{\ell-1}\big]^{(i)}\big[P_{\ell'+1}-P_{\ell'-1}\big]^{(j)}\,\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C_\ell,\tilde C_{\ell'})$$

*Intuition:* a linear transform of a random vector propagates its covariance by sandwiching — exact, given (b). **This is the load-bearing equation of the paper**, because it is a *double* sum: every off-diagonal element of the harmonic covariance contributes, weighted by two oscillating Legendre kernels. *Assumption:* only (b). The catch is cost — the authors note the exact $\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C_\ell,\tilde C_{\ell'})$ (Friedrich Eq. C12) "requires $O(\ell_{\rm max}^6)$ operations and is therefore computationally intractable" for $\ell_{\rm max}\sim$ few$\times10^3$–$10^4$. Hence an approximation is *forced*, and that is where the trouble enters. For spin-2, `eq:app_xip_cov`/`eq:app_xim_cov` show $\mathrm{Cov}^{\pm\pm}$ needs $\mathrm{Cov}^{EE,EE}$, $\mathrm{Cov}^{BB,BB}$ **and** $\pm2\,\mathrm{Cov}^{EE,BB}$ — the $EE$–$BB$ cross-covariance is not optional.

### (d) iNKA — `eq:iNKA`

$$\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C_\ell,\tilde C_{\ell'}) \simeq \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde C_\ell + \tilde C_{\ell'}}{\langle w^2\rangle_{\rm pixel}}\right)^2 \Xi_{\ell\ell'}(w^2,w^2), \qquad \Xi_{\ell\ell'} \equiv \frac{\mathcal{M}_{\ell\ell'}(w^2,w^2)}{2\ell'+1}$$

*Intuition:* the exact Gaussian covariance couples the signal at some intermediate multipole inside a 3j-weighted sum; NKA yanks the (assumed slowly varying) signal out of that sum, evaluated at $\ell,\ell'$, leaving a pure geometry factor built from the *squared* mask. The "i" in iNKA (Nicola et al. 2021) is the symmetrised $\tfrac12(\tilde C_\ell+\tilde C_{\ell'})^2$ combination using windowed rather than true spectra. Cost: $O(\ell_{\rm max}^3)$, i.e. one mode-coupling-matrix computation. *Assumption, stated explicitly:* "the iNKA relies on the mode-coupling kernel being sharply localized around $\ell\simeq\ell'$. It is therefore effective for diagonal-dominated quantities, but its errors can be amplified in situations where off-diagonal information is required with high precision." That is the whole paper in one sentence, and it is the authors'.

### (e) The $f_{\rm sky}$ approximation

$$\mathrm{Cov}(C_\ell,C_{\ell'})^{f_{\rm sky}} = \delta^K_{\ell\ell'}\frac{2C_\ell^2}{(2\ell+1)f_{\rm sky}}$$

*Intuition:* pretend the mask only removes modes, never couples them — a strictly diagonal harmonic covariance inflated by $1/f_{\rm sky}$. Then Legendre-project. *Assumption:* mode-coupling is negligible, which for the HSC-Y3 mask (~$f_{\rm sky}$ of order 1%, six disjoint fields) is badly wrong at large $\theta$. Included as the straw man everyone actually uses.

### (f) Weighted quartic counts — `eq:shirasaki_quartic`, `eq:shirasaki_matrix_form`

$$\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_i,\xi_j) = \frac{1}{N_{{\rm pair},i}N_{{\rm pair},j}}\sum_{a,b,c,d} w_aw_bw_cw_d\,\Delta_{\theta_i}(\theta_{ab})\Delta_{\theta_j}(\theta_{cd})\big\{\xi(\theta_{ac})\xi(\theta_{bd}) + \xi(\theta_{ad})\xi(\theta_{bc})\big\}/2$$

*Intuition:* this is just Wick's theorem written in configuration space and evaluated on the *actual galaxies*. Take every pair $(ab)$ in bin $i$ and every pair $(cd)$ in bin $j$; the Gaussian four-point function factorises into the two cross-pairings, each a product of the theory $\xi$ at the actual separations. **Why it gets geometry right:** there is no window function anywhere — no $\mathcal{M}$, no $f_{\rm sky}$, no approximation about mode-coupling — because the sum runs over real positions, so the footprint, the holes, the field boundaries, and the weights are all encoded exactly by which quadruplets exist. All the geometry information that iNKA has to compress into $\Xi_{\ell\ell'}$ is simply *there*. Cost is the problem: naively $O(N^4)$. Their contribution is the matrix rewrite

$$\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_i,\xi_j) = \frac{1}{N_{{\rm pair},i}N_{{\rm pair},j}}\Big[\mathbf{w}_i^{\mathrm{T}}\big(\mathbf{\Xi}_{1,i}\mathbf{\Xi}_{1,j}^{\mathrm{T}} + \mathbf{\Xi}_{2,i}\mathbf{\Xi}_{2,j}^{\mathrm{T}}\big)\mathbf{w}_j\Big]/2$$

turning it into BLAS-3 products over pair-indexed vectors, plus coarse-graining galaxies onto an $N_{\rm side}=256$ or $512$ grid depending on $\theta_i$. *Assumptions:* Gaussian field (no trispectrum, no SSC); **shape-noise terms explicitly dropped** ("We have neglected the terms involved with the shape noise"); still expensive enough that they restrict Fig. 5 to the single XMM field with 10 bins "to ease the computational expense."

### (g) How the sims are built

CCL for the input $C_\ell$ at a flat $\Lambda$CDM point ($\Omega_c=0.233$, $\Omega_b=0.046$, $\sigma_8=0.82$, $n_s=0.97$, $h=0.7$), a **single source redshift**; HEALPix `synfast`-style Gaussian realizations at $N_{\rm side}=2048$ (1.7 arcmin pixels); apply the HSC-Y3 mask (Li et al. 2022); **1000 realizations**. $\tilde C_\ell$ measured with NaMaster, $\ell_{\rm max}=3N_{\rm side}-1=6143$. For $\xi$, they build a *catalogue* per realization by sampling the masked map at the real HSC-Y3 galaxy positions and running TreeCorr — 20 log bins over $\theta\in[6,300]$ arcmin. The spin-2 appendix repeats all of this with input $\{C^{EE}_\ell = C^{\kappa\kappa}_\ell,\ C^{EB}_\ell=C^{BB}_\ell=0\}$.

## 4. Headline numbers

Only one quantitative figure appears in the text; the rest I read off the plots and mark as such.

- **iNKA harmonic *diagonal*, $\kappa$:** accurate. Paper's words: "The ratio between simulations and iNKA is consistent within~10\%" (Fig. 2 caption and §4.2). Same conclusion for $\mathrm{Cov}^{EE,EE}$ and $\mathrm{Cov}^{BB,BB}$ in the spin-2 appendix: "The analytical predictions reproduce the simulated diagonal auto-covariances well over the multipole range considered."
- **iNKA harmonic *off-diagonal*:** no number is quoted. The paper says only "noticeable differences remain in the off-diagonal components" and "visible discrepancies." The Fig. 4 ratio colour bar spans $\pm10^2$ and the off-diagonal is uniformly near $-1$, but see §5 — that is not a defensible error measurement.
- **Real-space failure, $\kappa$ (my read of `cov_xi.pdf`, full HSC-Y3 mask):** iNKA underestimates the $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi,\xi)$ diagonal by **~25–30% at $\theta\approx6'$** ($\approx9\times10^{-12}$ vs $\approx1.2\times10^{-11}$), with the deficit shrinking monotonically to ~zero by $\theta\gtrsim200'$. In error-bar terms that is ~13–16% on $\sigma(\xi)$ at small scales — **unmistakably larger than the 5–10% level at which people worry about the non-Gaussian trispectrum contribution.**
- **Real-space, XMM field, 10 bins (my read of `analytic_comp.pdf`):** iNKA low by ~20–30% at small $\theta$, drifting to ~10–15% low at large $\theta$. $f_{\rm sky}$ high by ~25–30% at $\theta\lesssim20'$ and then **low by a factor $\gtrsim2$ by $\theta\sim300'$** — the worst behaviour of the three, and worst exactly where survey geometry bites. **Quartic counts sits on the simulations within a few percent across the full range**, including reproducing the upturn at $\theta\gtrsim150'$ that both harmonic methods miss.
- **$\xi_\pm$ (my read of `covxipm.pdf`):** iNKA is fine at $\theta\lesssim20'$ for both $\xi_+$ and $\xi_-$, then diverges — $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_+)$ too high by roughly $2\times$ at $\theta\sim300'$, $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_-)$ turning over and falling off a cliff past $\sim150'$ while the sims rise. The $f_{\rm sky}$ curves are, ironically, *closer* to the sims for $\xi_-$ at large $\theta$ than iNKA is.
- **Cost:**
  - $f_{\rm sky}$: negligible, $O(\ell_{\rm max}^2)$ for the projection.
  - iNKA: $O(\ell_{\rm max}^3)$, one $\mathcal{M}_{\ell\ell'}(w^2,w^2)$ — the reason it is ubiquitous. Then the $\ell,\ell'$ double sum for the projection at $\ell_{\rm max}=6143$.
  - Exact harmonic covariance: **$O(\ell_{\rm max}^6)$** — the paper's word is "intractable."
  - Quartic counts: naively $O(N_{\rm gal}^4)$; their matrix form is a product of pair-indexed matrices of length $N_{\rm pair,i}$, still heavy enough that they must coarse-grain to $N_{\rm side}=256$–$512$ and restrict to one of six HSC fields with half the bins. **No wall-clock numbers, no scaling law, no memory footprint are given anywhere.** That omission is a genuine weakness — the whole practical case for the method rests on a cost they do not report.
  - Sims: 1000 HEALPix $N_{\rm side}=2048$ Gaussian realizations + NaMaster + TreeCorr on the full HSC-Y3 catalogue. Cheap for Gaussian fields; the paper's framing is that this is what becomes impossible for Stage-IV areas with $N$-body.

## 5. Where the argument could be weak

**The off-diagonal ratio panel of Fig. 4 does not measure what it appears to measure.** With $N_{\rm sim}=1000$ and unbinned multipoles to $\ell=6143$, the sample correlation coefficient between two distinct multipoles has a sampling scatter of $\approx1/\sqrt{1000}\approx0.03$ about a true value that is itself tiny. The "sims" panel is visibly pure speckle off the diagonal — that is noise, not signal. Dividing a smooth, coherent iNKA prediction by a noisy near-zero denominator produces a ratio panel that is meaningless element by element, and the uniform blue is exactly what you'd get from $|{\rm iNKA}| \ll |{\rm noise}|$ with random sign. **The authors never state a noise floor for their sample covariance and never bin the multipoles.** This does *not* invalidate the main result — Fig. 3's blue curve shows the projection of the noisy sim covariance lands on the direct $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi,\xi)$, so the noise evidently averages down under the double Legendre sum — but it does mean the *diagnosis* ("the errors are in the off-diagonal") is argued by elimination and by plausibility, not demonstrated. A cleaner demonstration would be: bin $\ell$ into bandpowers, or plot the projected covariance as a function of how many off-diagonal bands of the iNKA matrix you retain. Neither is done. **This is my sharpest technical reservation.**

**Gaussian-only simulations.** By construction there is no trispectrum and no super-sample covariance, so the test is a pure test of the *geometry* modelling — which is the honest scope, and the authors say so ("we focus on the Gaussian term which is dominant on the large scales"). But it cuts two ways: it means the ~25% error is a floor, not the full story; and it means the quartic-counts method's success is demonstrated only in the regime where its own Gaussian assumption is exactly true by construction. **The quartic-counts estimator is being graded on the test it was designed to pass.** In a real analysis it would carry an unquantified non-Gaussian error of its own.

**Shape noise is absent from the quartic-counts comparison** — "We have neglected the terms involved with the shape noise" — while for real cosmic shear the noise and mixed terms dominate the small-$\theta$ covariance. The paper cites Troxel et al. (2018) for exact pair counting of the noise term and Reischke et al. (2025) for triplet counting of the mixed term, so the pieces exist in the literature, but the head-to-head here is sample-variance-only. A reader could reasonably ask whether a 25% error on the sample-variance term matters at all once shape noise is added in quadrature at $\theta\sim6'$.

**One mask, one field, one redshift, one cosmology.** The HSC-Y3 footprint is six disjoint patches of a few hundred deg$^2$ each — very high perimeter-to-area, the hardest possible case for a narrow-kernel approximation, and the case where mode-coupling is broadest. The paper never says how the conclusion scales with $f_{\rm sky}$ or mask compactness. For a contiguous Rubin/Euclid-like footprint the mode-coupling kernel is much narrower and iNKA might well be fine — **the headline could be a statement about HSC's geometry rather than about iNKA.** And Fig. 5 (the only place quartic counts appears) uses XMM alone with 10 bins, so the best method is never tested on the full footprint that the failure was diagnosed on. The two key figures are not on the same configuration.

**Convergence, not shear, in the main text.** Sensible for clarity, and the appendix does the spin-2 case honestly. But the appendix is where it gets *worse*, not better (Fig. 11), and where a genuinely new effect appears (the oscillatory $EE$–$BB$ cross-covariance) that the main-text narrative never mentions. The organisation buries the most interesting result.

**Binning and $\ell_{\rm max}$.** They justify $\ell_{\rm max}=3N_{\rm side}-1=6143$ by the blue-on-black agreement in Fig. 3, which is fair. But the smallest bin is $\theta=6'$ against a 1.7' pixel — pixel-window and aliasing effects at the few-percent level are plausible there and are not discussed. The catalogue is built by *sampling the map at galaxy positions*, so every galaxy in a pixel gets an identical value; that is a pixelisation artefact that the Wolz et al. companion exists precisely to avoid, and it is not assessed here.

**No parameter-level impact.** The paper stops at covariance diagonals. Nobody is shown what a 25% covariance error does to $S_8$ or its error bar — and the answer is not obvious, since a smooth multiplicative error on the covariance largely rescales the error bar without biasing the mean, whereas a scale-dependent one tilts the effective weighting. **The paper's implied urgency is not backed by an inference test.**

**Presentation.** The text is thin — several results are asserted in one sentence with no number attached ("noticeable differences," "better agreement"). There is no table anywhere. `tables/` is empty. This reads like a short paper that would be much stronger with three numbers and one $\chi^2$.

## 6. Relation to the two companion papers

### Wolz, Farah, Reischke, Alonso & Nicola — "Analytical covariances for catalogue-based pseudo-$C_\ell$s" (arXiv:2607.14843)

Same iNKA lineage — Nicola is an author of both the iNKA paper (2010.09717) that Nagura+ test and this one. Wolz+ extend iNKA to *catalogue-based* pseudo-$C_\ell$ estimators (fields sampled at discrete source positions, using irregular-grid SHTs, no pixel map), splitting the covariance into distinct-source pairs — "approximated using the standard iNKA approach, treating each source as a finite-sized 'cloud'" — and self-pairs, "calculated exactly as an effective source of white noise." They keep $O(\ell_{\rm max}^3)$, validate on 32 field combinations, and ship it in NaMaster.

**Is Nagura+ a critique of the iNKA that Wolz+ build on? Partly, but they are not in conflict, and this is the key point for the room.** They are testing different claims about the same object:
- Wolz+ validate the covariance *of the pseudo-$C_\ell$ themselves*, in bandpowers, where the diagonal (and near-diagonal within a bandpower) is what matters. Nagura+ agree with them: iNKA is good to 10% there.
- Nagura+ test iNKA as an input to a *double sum over all $\ell,\ell'$*. That is a different and much more demanding use of the same matrix.

So it is less "iNKA is wrong" than "iNKA's known and accepted domain of validity — the diagonal — does not extend to the operation Friedrich et al. proposed." The narrow-kernel assumption is not a bug being discovered; it is a documented assumption being pushed outside its range. **A fair framing for the talk: this is a boundary-of-validity paper, not a refutation.**

**Would Wolz+'s catalogue version inherit the problem?** Yes, structurally and unavoidably — the "cloud" treatment changes the effective window and adds an exact self-pair term, but the distinct-pair contribution is still iNKA, so its off-diagonal structure carries the same narrow-kernel approximation. **If anyone tried to Legendre-project a Wolz+ catalogue covariance into $\xi_\pm$, they should expect the same failure.** Nothing in Wolz+ claims otherwise; they validate a harmonic-space product for harmonic-space use. Worth noting that the two papers may partly cancel on one axis: Wolz+ eliminate exactly the pixelisation of the source field that Nagura+ introduce by sampling maps at galaxy positions.

**Would anyone doing $\xi_\pm$ from a catalogue be affected?** Only if they take the harmonic route. In practice most $\xi_\pm$ analyses (CosmoLike/CosmoCov, OneCovariance, the DES and KiDS pipelines) compute the real-space Gaussian covariance directly, usually flat-sky with an $f_{\rm sky}$ or effective-area treatment plus exact pair counts for the noise term. Those pipelines are *not* using iNKA and so are not directly indicted — but Fig. 5 says the $f_{\rm sky}$ variants they do use are worse than iNKA at large $\theta$, by a factor of two. **The uncomfortable reading for a $\xi_\pm$ practitioner is not "don't use iNKA" (you weren't) but "the thing you are using is also wrong, and the fix is expensive pair counting."** HSC-Y3's own real-space analysis used simulation covariances, which is why this group can pose the question at all.

### Hadzhiyska & White — "Fewer simulations, sharper covariances: Zeldovich control variates" (arXiv:2605.28817)

Different axis entirely: not "replace sims with analytics" but "make sims converge faster." Pair each mock with a cheap Zeldovich realization sharing initial conditions, subtract $\beta(C-\mu_C)$ with $\beta$ and the correlation $\rho$ derived analytically under a Gaussian disconnected approximation; for masked redshift-space lognormal DESI-LRG mocks they get "approximately an order of magnitude" variance reduction on the covariance at $k\lesssim0.05\,h\,$Mpc$^{-1}$, factor 2–3 acceleration at higher $k$, so "$\sim10^3$ paired simulations achieve the statistical precision of $\sim10^4$."

**What Nagura+ says about control-variate approaches that use an analytic covariance as the control:** this is the most interesting connection, and it cuts favourably. A control variate is *unbiased by construction* — $Y = X - \beta(C-\mu_C)$ has the same expectation as $X$ whatever $\beta$ is; a wrong $\beta$ (or a wrong control) only costs you variance reduction, never accuracy. So an iNKA covariance that is 25% off in the real-space diagonal is a perfectly legitimate control variate, whereas it is an illegitimate *replacement*. **Control variates are exactly the right way to use an analytic covariance you don't fully trust** — and Nagura+, by quantifying how far iNKA is from truth, effectively tells you how much variance reduction to expect ($\rho$ high $\Rightarrow$ big gains; the iNKA/sim shapes in Fig. 3 track each other well, so $\rho$ would be high and the gains large). Note the symmetry: Hadzhiyska & White's $\beta$ and $\rho$ are themselves derived "under a Gaussian disconnected approximation," which is the same approximation whose geometric implementation Nagura+ is stress-testing. Their $\mu_C$ is the analytically-known Zeldovich mean, not a masked-sky analytic covariance — but the obvious cross-pollination is to use quartic counts (accurate, expensive, Gaussian) as the control and Gaussian sims as $X$, or vice versa. **Nobody in these three papers has written that down; it is a good thing to say out loud in the room.**

## 7. Five questions for the room

1. **On the diagnosis.** "Your Fig. 4 right panel divides iNKA by a sample covariance estimated from 1000 realizations at 6000 unbinned multipoles — off the diagonal that denominator is consistent with noise, so the ratio isn't a pointwise error. Fig. 3 shows the projection of the noisy sim covariance is nonetheless unbiased, so I believe the *result*. But what's the direct evidence for the *diagnosis*? Concretely: if you keep only the first $N$ off-diagonal bands of the iNKA matrix and project, at what $N$ does the answer converge, and how does that compare to the width of $\Xi_{\ell\ell'}$?"

2. **On generality — the one they can't answer.** "The HSC-Y3 footprint is six disjoint patches with an enormous perimeter-to-area ratio, i.e. the broadest possible mode-coupling kernel. Everything here is one mask. Is the 25% a statement about iNKA, or a statement about HSC? What happens for a contiguous 15,000 deg$^2$ Rubin footprint, where the narrow-kernel assumption is far better justified — and which way does the error scale with $f_{\rm sky}$?" *(They have one mask and no scaling argument; this cannot be answered from the paper.)*

3. **On whether it matters.** "You stop at the covariance diagonal. A smooth ~25% multiplicative error mostly rescales the error bar; a scale-dependent one re-weights the fit. Have you run the inference? What does the iNKA covariance do to $S_8$ and $\sigma(S_8)$ relative to the sim covariance for an HSC-Y3-like data vector — and is the shift in the mean or only the width?"

4. **On the winner's cost and its missing terms.** "Quartic counts wins in Fig. 5, but with shape noise dropped, on one of six fields, with 10 bins instead of 20, after coarse-graining to $N_{\rm side}=256$–$512$. What's the actual wall clock and memory, how does it scale with $N_{\rm gal}$ and $\theta_{\rm max}$, and how much of the coarse-graining is a real approximation versus a convenience? For a full Rubin catalogue with the shape-noise and mixed terms restored, is this tractable at all?"

5. **On the physics you half-found.** "Your most striking result is buried in Appendix B: the simulated $EE$–$BB$ cross-covariance oscillates, iNKA doesn't reproduce it at all, and Figs. 12–13 show that term is *required* for the projection to configuration space. Where does the oscillation come from physically — is it the mask's power spectrum imprinting a characteristic angular scale, and could you predict its period from the footprint geometry? And does an analogous coherent structure exist in $\mathrm{Cov}(\tilde C^{EE},\tilde C^{EE})$ that's simply hidden under your noise floor?"

## 8. Surprising

- **The failure is *not* a subtle off-diagonal leakage effect at the percent level — it's ~25% on the diagonal of the thing you actually use.** The Legendre projection amplifies rather than averages down the harmonic covariance's errors. That's counterintuitive: you'd naively expect summing over 6000 multipoles to be forgiving.
- **$f_{\rm sky}$ is worse than iNKA at large $\theta$ by a factor of two, and *better* than iNKA for $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_-)$ at large $\theta$.** The crossing in Fig. 5 (blue passing black at $\theta\approx60'$ and then collapsing) is a nice, uncomfortable visual: the crudest method is neither uniformly worst nor uniformly biased in one direction.
- **The $EE$–$BB$ cross-covariance is not a nuisance — it's load-bearing.** For a field with zero input $B$-mode, the leaked $B$-modes and their *correlation with $E$* carry a contribution you cannot drop when projecting to $\xi_\pm$. And the sign flips between $\xi_+$ and $\xi_-$ ($+2\mathrm{Cov}^{EE,BB}$ vs $-2$), so it pushes the two statistics in opposite directions.
- **The old method wins.** Schneider et al. (2002) quartic counts — a 24-year-old configuration-space idea — beats the modern harmonic machinery, because it never approximates the geometry at all. The paper's own conclusion: "highlighting the importance of accurately modeling pair-based correlations in configuration space."
- **iNKA $\mathrm{Cov}(\xi_-)$ literally falls off a cliff past $\theta\sim150'$** in Fig. 11 — the curve turns over and drops out of the plot while the simulations are still rising. That's not a calibration offset; that's a qualitative failure of shape, and nobody in the text comments on it.
