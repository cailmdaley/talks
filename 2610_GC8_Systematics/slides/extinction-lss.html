Extinction correlates positively with every tracer

$\rho_\ell\equiv C_\ell^{XT}/\sqrt{C_\ell^{XX}C_\ell^{TT}}$

  • Obscuration predicts $\delta\times E\lt0$; the mean is positive
  • Stronger toward high redshift
  • Also in $\gamma_E$ and $\kappa$, not $\gamma_B$: LSS in the template

The first frame shows every band and its Gaussian error; the second compresses the same full range, $100\lt\ell\lt3000$, into a GLS constant-rho mean with its error. Density is without visibility correction in both cycles; shear is LensMC E, kappa is SPT-3G. Positive means are not a claim that every band is positive, or that every tracer rejects zero. The redshift trend is broad, not strictly monotonic; low-redshift shear is noisy, especially in Cycle 1.

In these exploratory figures, $C^{XX}$ is the release-specific fiducial signal model averaged over exact integer science bins, not a measured noisy auto. $C^{TT}$ is measured template power. The normalization is held fixed in the error propagation; the errors are the full Gaussian cross blocks divided by the normalization outer product. This is a model-normalized signal-correlation estimate; it avoids clipping negative noise-subtracted shear autos. It is not bounded to lie within [-1,1] in a noisy realization. No denominator uncertainty is included. The GLS mean is a descriptive compression, not a fitted physical constant-rho model.

Extinction cannot directly obscure a CMB-lensing reconstruction; the joint sign, high-redshift shear and CIB-route comparison support the LSS interpretation. This does not rule out real Galactic selection effects or CMB reconstruction foreground response. B-mode evidence is from the preceding scorecard, not a rho normalization for B.
