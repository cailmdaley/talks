Euclid TR1 v1.1 LensMC; SPT-3G Main GMV; ACT DR6 baseline

The ratio of error bars, $\sigma_\mathrm{ACT}/\sigma_\mathrm{SPT}$, in the CMB-lensing cross-spectra, in the 15 NaMaster bandpowers from $\ell = 100$ to 3000. On TR1 it is measured: each σ is the square root of the diagonal of that survey's full NaMaster Gaussian covariance, the error bars of the data-vector slide. The twelve crosses (shear in six source bins × κ, galaxy density in six lens bins × κ) give the same ratio to within a few per cent, at most 10 % apart in one low-$\ell$ bandpower, because the CMB-lensing noise rather than the Euclid tracer sets it; the solid line is their mean. Only error bars enter, so the figure carries no measured amplitude.

The dashed DR1 line scales the TR1 ratio by the overlap areas, with σ ∝ (area)$^{-1/2}$ for each survey: ratio$_\mathrm{DR1}$ = ratio$_\mathrm{TR1}\times\sqrt{(A^\mathrm{TR1}_\mathrm{ACT}/A^\mathrm{DR1}_\mathrm{ACT})(A^\mathrm{DR1}_\mathrm{SPT}/A^\mathrm{TR1}_\mathrm{SPT})} = 0.874\times$ ratio$_\mathrm{TR1}$. ACT DR6 gains more of DR1 than SPT-3G Main does: from 345 to 1069 deg² against 149 to 353 deg². TR1 overlaps are from the RR2 photometric footprint at NSIDE 512 and DR1 overlaps are effective areas from the DR1 VMPZ coverage (the footprint slide); each ratio of areas is within one convention. The scaling assumes the same noise per mode and ignores mode coupling, and it uses SPT-3G Main alone.

ACT DR6 is tighter at the largest scales, where its larger overlap gives more modes: by up to about 7 % below $\ell \approx 180$ on TR1, and by about 15–20 % on DR1, where the crossover moves from $\ell \approx 200$ to about 250. Above it SPT-3G is tighter, by about 1.5 (TR1) and 1.3 (DR1) from $\ell \approx 550$ to 1400, and 2.4 (TR1) and 2.1 (DR1) at $\ell \approx 2700$, where the depth and resolution of the Main field dominate. On TR1 the κ-only blinded posteriors of the results slide give $\sigma(S_8)$ 0.056 with SPT-3G and 0.069 with ACT DR6.
