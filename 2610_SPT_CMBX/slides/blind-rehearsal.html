Muir-style blinding: a hidden shift of the data vector

  • First step — the unblinded chain on one GLASS mock, with the input cosmology inside its 68 % contour.
  • Second step — two throwaway blinds move the posterior far from the unblinded one, which is the point of the blind.
  • Third step — moved back by their injected shifts, both overlap the unblinded posterior: recovered shifts within $0.75\sigma$ of the injected ones.
  • Caveat — a blinded chain's widths are not the unblinded widths; the real TR1 blind was never opened.
The blind

The blind follows the Euclid 3×2pt analysis: a parameter-shift blind in the style of Muir et al. (2020, arXiv:1911.05929), the same method as the 3×2pt blinding tool (blindspot) and LSST DESC's Smokescreen. A sealed shift in cosmology is turned into a $\Delta C_\ell$ added to the data vector, so shapes, signal-to-noise, the covariance and the null tests stay visible while the inferred cosmology is hidden. The official pipeline blinds only the Euclid 2pt spectra, so the κ crosses are blinded by a separate module, over the whole 5×2pt data vector.

The rehearsal chain

The likelihood chain run on one GLASS mock (seed 3000): all 90 spectra of the 5×2pt data vector, 15 bands each, $\ell$ from 100 to 3000, scored with the fixed Gaussian covariance. The model is reduced to eight parameters, $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ and $S_8$ plus six linear galaxy biases, with $\Omega_\mathrm{b}$, $h$, $n_s$ and the neutrino mass held at the mock's truth and no intrinsic-alignment, shear-calibration or photo-z nuisances, so the 0.4 % error on $S_8$ is optimistic. The unblinded chain gives $\Omega_\mathrm{m} = 0.326 \pm 0.010$ and $S_8 = 0.8338 \pm 0.0035$ against an input of 0.315 and 0.832: $+1.0\sigma$ and $+0.4\sigma,$ with the input inside the 68 % contour.

That $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ offset is the shear-mask bias found on the mocks (the mocks slide's notes): this chain weights the shear mask by source counts, which over 10 mocks puts $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ $+1.6\sigma$ high and leaves $S_8$ unbiased. A binary mask removes the $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ bias and uncovers an $S_8$ offset of about $-1\sigma$ that the excess had hidden, still under study. The blind test does not depend on it: a blind is judged by how its posterior moves relative to the unblinded one on the same mock.

The blinds

Two throwaway blinds were drawn at random by the same blinding module the TR1 analysis uses, each a hidden shift in $(\Omega_\mathrm{m}, S_8)$ turned into a $\Delta C_\ell$ added to the data vector, and the full chain was rerun on each blinded vector; the shifts were revealed only after all chains had converged. The recovered shifts match the injected ones to $-0.75$ and $+0.14$ unblinded $\sigma$ (blind A, in $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ and $S_8$) and $+0.13$ and $+0.40\sigma$ (blind B).

Caveats

The blind is a translation only approximately. The residuals are larger than the chains' Monte Carlo error, and the posterior's shape changes with where it lands: blind A's $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ width shrinks to a third of the unblinded one, while blind B keeps 83 % and 92 % of the widths. So widths read off a blinded chain are not the unblinded widths, and are not to be quoted as such. The real TR1 blind was never opened.
