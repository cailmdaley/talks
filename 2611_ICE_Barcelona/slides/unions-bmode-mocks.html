B-mode excess in v2 data lies outside 69 GLASS mocks

  • mocks matched to v2 $n(z),$ footprint and noise
  • same measurement code and covariance
  • template amplitude: data 0.89, mocks 0.07 ± 0.22
  • no mock reaches it: an additive systematic in the data
  • Histogram — the amplitude of an additive B-mode template fitted to $C_\ell^{BB}$ over all ten bin pairs: gray for 69 sampled GLASS mocks, red for the blinded v2 tomographic data vector.
  • Mocks — sampled GLASS lognormal mocks with the v2 four-bin $n(z),$ the tomographic footprint and each bin's $n_{\rm eff}$ and $\sigma_e,$ measured by the same code as the data with the data's covariance. They contain no PSF leakage and no additive B-mode by construction.
  • Reading — the data's 0.89 (GLS σ 0.23) sits at +3.7σ against the mocks, and none of the 69 reaches it, so the B-mode excess is an additive systematic in the data. On $\chi^2$ alone it is a tension (PTEs 0.02–0.03 once recalibrated on the mocks), because $\chi^2$ ignores sign; the template, coherent in sign across bands and pairs, is what makes it significant.
Caveats

The template shape was fitted to the B-modes of the non-tomographic run, largely the same galaxies, so its significance is mildly a posteriori. With revised tomographic bins and their own matched ensemble of 69 mocks, the amplitude is 0.77 at +3.3σ, again with no mock reaching it. The blind adds no B-mode, so these null tests are blind-insensitive. The fitted PSF-leakage model accounts for about 26–30 % of the excess; the next mock ingredient is PSF leakage injected at the measured per-bin amplitude.
