Four dust maps; CSFD passes all 13 tracer nulls

This comparison exists only for Cycle 2; no Cycle-1 dust-map comparison is implied. The means and errors use full-range GLS compression, visibility-corrected density, LensMC E and SPT kappa, with the same fixed fiducial-signal normalization as slide 3. The first frame puts all four maps on one equatorial plate-carree grid and exactly the same union GC/WL support, after subtracting each footprint median and dividing by its pixel rms. The common colour scale is clipped for readability; the map panels are not beam-matched. Their similar Galactic structure hides the small extragalactic imprint, so the second frame is the quantitative centrepiece.

The GNILC input is the delivered Planck R2.00 Dust-GNILC-F545 intensity map (MJy/sr), not the separate CIB-GNILC product. No GNILC reddening or optical-depth model was found in the local Planck inputs. No conversion to E(B-V) is needed: rho is invariant under a positive multiplicative template calibration. The Galactic-to-equatorial rotation, template support, density weights and Gaussian cross-block policy match the SFD/CSFD path. The saved SFD theory normalization is reused exactly; a regenerated shear archive has identical numerical bin arrays to the saved snapshot (only provenance differs).

GNILC uses component separation to suppress CIB, but this is not a guarantee of zero residual LSS. The recorded prediction was weaker correlations than VMPZ/SFD, without necessarily reaching CSFD-like nulls; that is the descriptive result. Five of thirteen GNILC spectrum nulls have Gaussian PTE below 0.05: density bins 1, 4, 5, 6 and shear E bin 6. Kappa passes (PTE 0.8209). The largest individual GNILC pull is shear E6 at ell=2148, +3.33 Gaussian errors; the bandpower backup shows the residuals. It has nominal 5-arcmin resolution with spatially adaptive smoothing where dust is weak, as described in Planck intermediate results XLVIII (arXiv:1605.09387). A uniform 5-arcmin Gaussian alone transmits about 0.18 in amplitude at ell=3000, so the resolution is not negligible. We retain the delivered beam/interpolation response in both cross and measured template auto, exactly as for SFD/CSFD: it cancels in ideal narrow-band rho, but not exactly with mask coupling, broad bands or spatially varying smoothing. Noise or residual CIB in the template auto can dilute rho; these are exploratory delivered-map comparisons, not a matched-beam precision ranking.

The provenance audit identifies the source as Planck 2013 thermal-dust-model R1.20 EBV, converted to VIS extinction in magnitudes as $3.1\times0.68=2.108$. Every original source value is identical in the two cycles on the audited common pixels; coverage and window preparation changed, not the dust values. All thirteen CSFD full-spectrum zero-null PTEs exceed 0.27. That does not make every GLS mean consistent with zero at one or two errors: for example density bin 1 has mean 0.0182 ± 0.0070, while its full-spectrum null passes. The headline refers to the spectrum nulls, not an assertion of exactly zero LSS. SFD density means span 0.0414–0.0575, apart from rounding.

CSFD's LSS template uses WISE galaxies, constrained by spectroscopic galaxy/quasar correlations. A galaxy null is partly by construction; it is not independent certification that all cosmological signal is preserved. Compare alternative dust templates individually, not as simultaneous regressors.
