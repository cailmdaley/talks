The 857 GHz CIB predicts the extinction correlation

$\bar\rho_{\rm pred}=\overline{\rho(\delta,I)\,\rho(I,E)}$
$I$: 857 GHz CIB

  • The CIB predicts a positive extinction cross
  • Visibility weighting can suppress it
  • Removing this component removes clustering

The prediction is a bandwise product, compressed with exactly the same weights as the measured extinction mean; it is not the product of two separately compressed means. Only the Lenz 857 GHz CIB map is used. All bars are propagated from Gaussian NaMaster blocks with fixed signal normalizations; the prediction includes its CIB-auto uncertainty but omits covariance among its ingredients and with the measurement. No difference significance is claimed.

Cycle 2: corrected bins 2–6 track the prediction closely, while bin 1 retains an excess: measured 0.0903 ± 0.0063 versus predicted 0.0639 ± 0.0039. Cycle 1: visibility weighting drives bins 3, 4 and 6 below the simple prediction, so a blanket equality in both cycles would be false. For bin 6, without correction the means are 0.0687 ± 0.0092 versus 0.0646 ± 0.0053; with correction they are 0.0161 ± 0.0091 versus 0.0600 ± 0.0051. Cycle-1 bins 2–5 without correction also have excesses; the simple one-CIB-component model is not exact.

In a simplified linear regression, subtracting a template component correlated with LSS subtracts clustering. The observed comparisons do not identify the visibility model's actual input set or quantify a science-vector bias. Cycle-2 raw removes correction on supported pixels only; it does not restore visibility-excluded area.
