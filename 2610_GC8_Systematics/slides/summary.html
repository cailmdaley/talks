Cail Daley · CEA Paris-Saclay

Six VMPZ templates crossed with TR1 density, shear and CMB lensing (Cycle 1, TR1 v1.1): shear and $\kappa$ are clean against every template; with the visibility weights, density fails only against extinction in lens bin 5. Where the implied contamination is resolved, no template moves any band of the data vector by more than about 0.4 of its Gaussian error $\sigma_{ab}$ (…/six-template-scorecard; the contamination's own error bar uses a Knox error for the template auto-spectrum, see slide 3).

The extinction template, documented as Planck $E(B-V)$, correlates positively with every density bin, the highest-redshift source bins and $\kappa$, rising with $\ell$ and with redshift, which dust obscuration cannot do. The CIB route reproduces the high-signal crosses with no free parameter, best with the 857 GHz CIB (…/extinction-template-carries-lss, …/lenz-cib-route).

Against the Cycle-2 lens bins, SFD correlates at $\bar\rho = +0.031$ to $+0.056$ ($\chi^2$ = 27.7–72.5 for 15 bands), and CSFD, which subtracts a WISE-galaxy-built LSS template, is consistent with zero (PTE 0.39–0.98) except a $+2.7\sigma$ offset in bin 1; since the template was built from galaxies, that null is partly by construction (slides 6–7, …/sfd-csfd-gc-cross).

The consequence for galaxy clustering. A weight, regression or deprojection built on a far-infrared dust-emission map (SFD, the Planck $E(B-V)$ map behind the VMPZ template) also removes the clustering that the map's CIB traces. Regressing $\delta$ on a map it correlates with at $\rho$ removes a fraction $\rho^2$ of $\delta$'s observed power: with the per-bin constant-$\rho$ fits to $\delta \times$ SFD ($\bar\rho$ = 0.056, 0.031, 0.031, 0.038, 0.039, 0.042, each ± 0.007; science/cmbx/gc8-systematics-update/sfd-csfd-gc.json), that is 0.31 % in bin 1 and 0.10–0.17 % in bins 2–6, about 0.1–0.3 % per bin. That is a fraction of the observed power, shot noise included, so the fraction of the clustering signal is larger. A CIB-corrected map (CSFD), an HI-based map, or stellar reddening avoids it. If extinction is one of the visibility model's five unnamed SOM inputs, the visibility weights do this by construction; the CIB-route test shows them removing more of the CIB-carried correlation than predicted in lens bin 6 (measured/predicted 0.22 ± 0.16, PTE 0.013 at 857 GHz), which fits that reading (inferred, not tested).
