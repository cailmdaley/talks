CMB lensing checks source n(z) and shear calibration

$\mu_i = C^{\gamma_i\delta}/C^{\kappa\delta}$ ÷ fiducial
$\propto (1+m_i) \times$ lensing efficiency

  • bias, $\sigma_8$ cancel; cosmology via distances
  • IA in the templates, $A_{\rm IA} = 0.6$
  • mocks: $\mu = 1.03 \pm 0.06$

  • TR1 × ACT DR6: $\mu = 0.85^{+0.15}_{-0.12}$
  • TR1 × SPT-3G: $\mu = 0.95^{+0.21}_{-0.15}$

Derivation

Lensing efficiency. In the Born and Limber approximations, the convergence of sources at comoving distance $\chi_s$ is the projected matter overdensity weighted by geometry: $\kappa(\hat n) = \int_0^{\chi_s} d\chi\, \tfrac{3}{2}\Omega_{\rm m}\tfrac{H_0^2}{c^2}\,\tfrac{\chi}{a(\chi)}\,\tfrac{\chi_s-\chi}{\chi_s}\,\delta_{\rm m}(\chi\hat n, \chi)$. A matter fluctuation at distance $\chi$ lenses best when it sits roughly halfway to the sources and not at all when it sits on them; the factor $(\chi_s-\chi)/\chi_s$, the lens–source distance over the observer–source distance, carries that geometry. For a source bin with redshift distribution $n_i(\chi)$, average it over the sources: the lensing efficiency $q_i(\chi) = \int_\chi^\infty d\chi'\, n_i(\chi')\,(\chi'-\chi)/\chi'$, between 0 and 1. It is the only place the source n(z) enters. The CMB is a single source plane at $\chi_* \approx 14\,$Gpc, so $q_*(\chi) = (\chi_*-\chi)/\chi_*$, which depends on nothing but distances.

The two cross-spectra. A lens bin $j$ with redshift distribution $n_j(\chi)$ and linear bias $b_j$ traces $\delta_g = \int d\chi\, n_j b_j\, \delta_{\rm m}$. The E-mode shear equals the convergence up to $\sqrt{(\ell+2)(\ell-1)/\ell(\ell+1)} \approx 1$, the same in data and template, and the measured shear is $(1+m_i)$ times the true one. In Limber, with $P$ the matter power spectrum at $k = (\ell+\tfrac12)/\chi$,

$C_\ell^{\gamma_i\delta_j} = (1+m_i)\,\tfrac{3}{2}\Omega_{\rm m}\tfrac{H_0^2}{c^2}\int d\chi\, \frac{n_j(\chi)\, b_j}{a(\chi)\,\chi}\; q_i(\chi)\; P\big(\tfrac{\ell+1/2}{\chi}, z\big)$, and $C_\ell^{\kappa\delta_j}$ is the same integral with $q_*$ in place of $q_i$ and no $(1+m_i)$.

The ratio. Everything but the efficiency is common: $\Omega_{\rm m}H_0^2$, the galaxy bias, the amplitude and shape of $P$ (so $\sigma_8$), and the lens bin's n(z) as a weight. For a thin lens bin at $\chi_l$ the integrals collapse and $C^{\gamma_i\delta_j}/C^{\kappa\delta_j} = (1+m_i)\, q_i(\chi_l)/q_*(\chi_l)$. For a source plane this is $\frac{(\chi_s-\chi_l)/\chi_s}{(\chi_*-\chi_l)/\chi_*}$, a pure ratio of distances (history below). For real bins it is $(1+m_i)\langle q_i\rangle_j/\langle q_*\rangle_j$, averaged over the lens bin with weight $n_j b_j P/(a\chi)$, which drifts slightly with ℓ. So we don't divide spectra: we fit the measured pair against the fiducial theory pushed through the same windows. $C^{\gamma_i\delta_j} = \mu_i\beta_j T^{\gamma\delta}_{ij}$ and $C^{\kappa\delta_j} = \beta_j T^{\kappa\delta}_j$, with $\beta_j$ free per lens bin (bias × σ₈-like amplitude, cancels) and

$\mu_i \simeq (1+m_i)\,\dfrac{\langle q_i\rangle_j}{\langle q_i^{\rm fid}\rangle_j}\,\dfrac{\langle q_*^{\rm fid}\rangle_j}{\langle q_*\rangle_j}$,

where the fiducial has $m = 0$ and the delivered source n(z). The CMB factor depends on cosmology only through distances, which is why a blind-sized shift moves μ by a few per cent while it moves the cross-spectra themselves by tens of per cent ($A^{\gamma\kappa} \propto S_8^{2.6}$ moves −25 %/+40 % over the envelope). So $\mu_i - 1 \approx m_i + \delta\ln\langle q_i\rangle$: the multiplicative bias plus the error in the source efficiency.

How the estimator responds (noiseless injections: TheoryVector bands at the fiducial plus one effect, fitted with the fiducial templates exactly as the data are; results/scratch/napoli/lens_ratio_response). On the TR1 n(z), $q_i/q_*$ at the lens planes the fit uses is 0.27 to 0.76: the Euclid sources sit much closer than the CMB.

  • $m = +0.01$ moves every $\mu_i$, and every lens plane, by exactly 1 %.
  • A source shift $\Delta z = +0.01$ raises $\mu_i$ by 4.4 % (bin 1) down to 0.6 % (bin 6), most where the lens plane sits just in front of the sources and $q_i$ is small. The Euclid requirement shift, $0.002(1+\bar z)$, gives 1.4 % to 0.3 %. A ±5 % width stretch gives ≤ 1.8 %, which is why the requirement and the literature talk about the mean: the ratio is mostly a mean-redshift probe.
  • Intrinsic alignments (NLA, $A_{\rm IA} = 0.5$): sources physically near the lens bin align radially with it, which lowers $\gamma\delta$, by 16 % in bin 1 falling to 0.8 % in bin 6.
  • Lens n(z): the lens weight enters numerator and denominator differently. A +0.02 lens shift lowers $\mu_i$ by 11 % (bin 1) to 2 % (bin 6); a 10 % width change moves bin 1 by 3 % and bin 6 by 0.1 %. Source bin 1 is the sensitive one: it sits just behind lens bin 1, where $q_1$ falls steeply, while bin 6 sits far behind every lens bin.
  • Fed the IA and lens-width values that the joint fit to the full TR1 data vector prefers (the gg-pattern study: $A_{\rm IA} \approx 0.6$, per-bin lens stretches), the estimator returns $\mu_i$ = 0.93, 0.85, 0.91, 0.94, 0.96, 0.98 and a shared 0.99. So the nuisances the rest of the data vector asks for account for a few per cent of the per-bin shortfall, and about 1 % of the shared one. IA is in the headline templates (next item); the lens n(z) widths are not.
  • The slide's numbers use NLA intrinsic alignments in the $\gamma\delta$ templates, $A_{\rm IA} = 0.6$ with no redshift evolution at the product pivot $z = 0.62$, about the value the full-vector fit prefers (results/scratch/napoli/lens_ratio_ia; $A_{\rm IA} = 0$ reproduces the IA-free fit exactly). The shared μ is then 0.886 (LensMC × SPT-3G), 0.846 (LensMC × ACT DR6), 0.953 (MetaCal × SPT-3G) and 0.900 (MetaCal × ACT DR6), within 1.5 % of the IA-free values (0.883, 0.834, 0.941, 0.891) in every variant and for every $A_{\rm IA}$ from 0 to 0.9. The mocks carry no IA, so their recovery uses IA-free templates. Per bin, IA lifts source bin 1 most (1.07 → 1.32), bins 2–4 by 4–14 % and bins 5–6 by under 2 %, so bins 2–6 stay at 0.86–0.92: IA does not explain the shortfall, and it makes the pattern less flat, not more.

m against redshift. One $\mu_i$ cannot tell $m_i$ from a source-redshift shift: Miyatake et al. (2017) call them "totally degenerate". They separate across lens planes: at fixed source bin, $m$ moves every lens plane alike, while a source shift moves the nearest lens plane most (source bin 4: +0.7 % at lens bin 1, +2.2 % at lens bin 5, per 0.01). That slope is what the shear-ratio test exploits; our one-μ-per-source-bin fit throws it away, and at 15–40 % per point it would not resolve it. Read per bin, $\mu \approx 0.85$ would need $m \approx -0.15$, or source bins sitting about 0.05 (bin 2) to 0.2 (bin 6) lower in redshift than delivered: neither is credible, so this is a consistency check.

The shared μ is not an average of the six. The $\gamma_i\delta_j$ spectra are strongly correlated through the common lens field, and a one-amplitude GLS fit to a pattern that is not uniform across source bins extrapolates: under IA every per-bin $\mu_i$ is below 1 but the shared μ comes out at 1.004 (0.987 with a diagonal covariance). The shared μ is therefore the test of a bin-uniform effect, $m$ or a common $\kappa\delta$ normalisation, and moves by ≲ 1.5 % under IA and n(z) errors; those live in the per-bin pattern.

History

Jain & Taylor (2003, PRL 91, 141302, astro-ph/0306046) proposed the ratio of tangential shear around the same foreground halos for two source bins: "the change in the cross-correlation with background redshift does not depend on the galaxy-mass power spectrum, nor on θ", leaving distances; they named source photo-z as the limiting systematic. Bernstein & Jain (2004, ApJ 600, 17, astro-ph/0309332) developed it as "cross-correlation cosmography", and Hu & Jain (2004, astro-ph/0312395) wrote the harmonic-space form. Hu, Holz & Vale (2007, PRD 76, 127301, arXiv:0708.4391) first used the CMB as the far source plane, with stacked cluster profiles. Das & Spergel (2009, PRD 79, 043509, arXiv:0810.3931) wrote it as a ratio of cross-spectra, $r = C_\ell^{\kappa_{\rm CMB}\Sigma}/C_\ell^{\kappa_{\rm gal}\Sigma}$ (our μ inverted), "independent of the angular scale, tracer bias and the power spectrum". Miyatake et al. (2017, PRL 118, 161301, arXiv:1605.05337) made the first measurement, CFHTLenS × Planck around BOSS CMASS: 17 % at $z = 0.53$. Prat et al. (2019, MNRAS 487, 1363, arXiv:1810.02212) measured it with DES Y1 × SPT+Planck at about 10 % combined, with photo-z as a shift only and m and $\Delta z_s$ "largely degenerate". Galaxy–galaxy ratios became a calibration tool in DES Y3 (Sánchez, Prat et al. 2022, PRD 105, 083529, arXiv:2105.13542): with IA and magnification modelled, the shear ratio narrows the source $\Delta z$ posteriors by 11–38 % and constrains IA, but not $m$. Our 15–20 % shared μ sits with Miyatake and Prat; Euclid's requirement would need 0.3–1 %. The papers are in cmbx/papers/.

The measurement

The dark slivers around 1 are the Euclid requirements propagated to $\mu_i$ as stated in the Euclid overview paper (Euclid Collaboration: Mellier et al., arXiv:2405.13491, Sect. on weak lensing): an uncertainty on the multiplicative bias below $2\times10^{-3}$ (from Cropper et al. 2013) and mean bin redshifts known to $\sigma_{\langle z\rangle} \lt 0.002\,(1+z)$. For each source bin the TR1 n(z) is shifted by $\pm 0.002(1+\bar z_i)$ and the source lensing efficiency recomputed, averaged over the n(z) of each lens bin the fit uses (equal weight); the change in its logarithm and $\sigma(m)$ add in quadrature. The result is a 1σ half-width of 1.0 % in bin 1 falling to 0.3 % in bin 6: the low-redshift bins are the most sensitive because their lens planes sit close in front of the sources. Our per-bin errors are 15 to 40 %, so on TR1 the lens ratio is a consistency check a factor 20 to 100 looser than the requirement, not a calibration.

Scale cut: both $\gamma\delta$ and $\kappa\delta$ keep $\ell \lt k_{\rm max}\chi(\bar z_j)$ with $k_{\rm max} = 0.15\,h/$Mpc, so ℓ below 128 to 310 depending on the lens bin. That is the cut the nonlinear-bias contamination test validates: a noiseless data vector with perturbative nonlinear bias, fitted with our linear-bias model, shifts $(S_8, \Omega_{\rm m})$ by $0.22\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ with $\delta\delta$ at 0.2 and the crosses at 0.15, against $0.52\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ with 0.2 everywhere. With 0.2 everywhere the IA-free shared μ was $0.87^{+0.15}_{-0.11}$ (against 0.88 at the split cut); the stricter cut costs about a quarter of the precision and moves the centre by less than 0.1σ.

First step: the estimator on ten GLASS 6×2pt mocks (lognormal, linear bias, $m = 0,$ ACT-like κ noise on the TR1 footprint), where the truth is 1. Dots are the ten seeds, points the median with the robust seed scatter divided by $\sqrt{10}.$ Per-bin medians are 0.96 to 1.05 and the shared μ is $1.03 \pm 0.06$ (mean of seeds), so the estimator is unbiased at this precision. The seed scatter is 0.96 to 1.39 times the propagated per-bin error (1.13 for the shared μ, coverage 58 %), so the propagated errors may be 10 to 30 % too small; read the data errors with that in mind.

Second step: TR1 × SPT-3G data, through the blinded likelihood input and its full Gaussian covariance. Per bin, μ is 0.86 to 1.32 with errors of 15 to 40 %; source bin 1, the one IA lifts most, is the high one. The six points are strongly correlated, because every source bin is divided by the same $\kappa\delta_j$ spectra: a fluctuation in $\kappa\delta_j$ moves every $\mu_i$ that uses lens bin $j$ the same way, so five points below 1 are not five independent low points. The fit with one shared amplitude gives $\mu = 0.89$ ($-0.13,$ $+0.18$), 0.6σ below 1. That is a 15–20 % consistency check of shear calibration times n(z) efficiency, an order of magnitude looser than the ~1 % image-simulation priors on $m$: CMB lensing does not calibrate $m$ here, it checks that nothing is grossly wrong. Caveats: the mocks are ACT-like, so they test the estimator, not SPT's noise; intrinsic alignments are modelled at a fixed amplitude, not marginalised; and these are exploratory estimators (results/scratch/napoli/crosses), not an lc output.

The variant control switches the shear method and the CMB map. Shared μ with IA: LensMC × SPT-3G $0.89^{+0.18}_{-0.13}$, LensMC × ACT DR6 $0.85^{+0.15}_{-0.12}$, MetaCal × SPT-3G $0.95^{+0.21}_{-0.15}$, MetaCal × ACT DR6 $0.90^{+0.17}_{-0.12}$. All four sit within about 1σ of 1. They share galaxies and, per map, the same κ, and no joint covariance across variants exists, so differences between them are not calibrated significances.
