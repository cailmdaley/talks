CLOE with its slow pieces in JAX

  • Top — plain CLOE with the Theory WG emulator for $P(k)$: 8.0 core-s per full-vector evaluation, of which the Limber projection alone is 5.3 s (a nuisance-only step, $P(k)$ not recomputed). The 2.7 s for background and $P(k)$ is the difference. With CAMB for $P(k)$ an evaluation is 22.6 core-s. So emulating $P(k)$ gains only about 3×: the projection sets the cost.
  • Bottom — the same theory with three pieces in JAX: the background (closed-form $H(z)$, with CAMB's radiation and neutrino density tables computed once), the CosmoPower-JAX emulator call and the $P(k)$ table built from it, and the projection, which is CLOElib's own tracers and Limber integrals compiled under jit and vmap. The Gaussian $\chi^2$ is the same formula. The whole likelihood is one compiled function that nautilus calls on 1000 points at a time, so a node's cores work on a batch together.
  • Right — cost per point. Plain CLOE: 8.0 s on one core (median of 5 prior draws). JAX: about 0.5 core-s, which is the likelihood wall time of a full chain × 112 cores ÷ points evaluated: 0.45 on the blinded TR1 chain (247 000 points in 986 s) and 0.57 on the GLASS mock chain (206 000 points). It is an upper bound if not every core was busy.
  • Agreement — over the fiducial point and 24 prior draws, 90 pairs and 651 bands, the largest difference from plain CLOE is $3.8\times10^{-4}\sigma$ of the Gaussian bandpower error on the data model.

Where the port differs from CLOE

Numerically, in the interpolation of $P$ onto the Limber wavenumbers, the quadrature of the comoving distance and $z_* = 1089$ in the CMB-lensing kernel; and the emulator is clamped to its training box, where CLOE raises. Together these stay below the agreement quoted above.

Declared patches to CLOElib

Carried as small mixin classes, each listed in the package's README with what it changes and why: the $P(k, z)$ lookup at the Limber wavenumbers on a table uniform in $\ln k$ (30 → 3 ms, bands move by ≤ $6\times10^{-5}\sigma$); a power-law continuation of $P(k)$ beyond the table in place of CLOElib's cubic extrapolation; the lensing efficiency in $O(N)$ instead of through an $O(N^2)$ distance-ratio matrix; one $P(k)$ interpolation shared by all tracer pairs; and a table that holds $\ln P$ directly. Nothing else reimplements physics that CLOElib has.

On the GLASS mocks

The mocks use dark-matter-only HMCode-2020, which the emulators do not provide, so there CLOE in JAX uses the feedback emulator with HMCode's feedback response removed and a correction table fixed at the reference cosmology; it agrees with plain CLOE + CAMB to 0.038σ per band at prior draws (0.005σ on the κ crosses).
