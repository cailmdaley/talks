Euclid DR1 overlaps every CMB lensing survey

The blue regions are the two wide areas of Euclid DR1 that a cosmology analysis will use, seen from the south (left) and north (right) celestial poles at the same scale. They come from the DR1 observed footprint: the union of the MER VIS tiles in the DR1_R1 and R2 releases, as listed in the archive in October 2025 (2341 deg² in all). That is where VIS mosaics exist, not a shear mask: there are no effective-coverage, star or bright-object cuts yet, and DR1 shear processing is not finished, so the analysis areas will be smaller. DR1-wide keeps, in each hemisphere, the largest connected region of that footprint and any piece within 1° of it: 1648 deg² in the south and 389 deg² in the north. The 304 deg² left out are isolated fields: the patches near the equator, the fields around the north ecliptic pole, and small pieces in the far south.

The CMB lensing footprints arrive one at a time. Planck PR4 (23 356 deg²) covers 1930 deg² of DR1-wide, nearly all of it in both hemispheres. ACT DR6, with its baseline lensing mask (9646 deg²), overlaps 1446 deg², all in the south. SPT-3G Main, the 1500 deg² winter field (1672 deg² in its lensing mask), overlaps 423 deg² on the southern side of the DR1 south region, partly inside ACT. The north region is seen by Planck alone.

Conventions: a CMB footprint's area counts mask pixels above 0.5 at the mask's native NSIDE 2048; for these apodized masks this agrees with the weighted area to 0.4 %. The overlap with DR1-wide is binary, mask above zero and DR1-wide, with DR1 upgraded from NSIDE 1024 to 2048. The maps are drawn at NSIDE 512. The orthographic view shrinks areas far from the pole: near Dec −20°, at the northern edge of the DR1 south region, regions appear at about a third of their true size relative to areas near the pole.
