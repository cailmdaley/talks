Collaborations grew to the size the data needed

  • reducing and understanding the data took that many people
  • still person-limited
  • overhead: coordination, review, consensus
  • much of the time lost is latency, not work
  • Crowds — three collaborations this talk draws on, one dot per person at the order of magnitude of their size (30, 300 and 3,000 dots, not head counts): UNIONS weak lensing, SPT, the Euclid Consortium.
  • The observation — the bottleneck of a large analysis has been people's time, which is why the collaborations grew, and that person power carries its own overhead.
  • Latency — a product is finished on a Wednesday; the person who needs it looks on Monday, has something by the end of the next week, then is away for two: a month has passed. That waiting, more than the work, often sets the pace of an analysis.
