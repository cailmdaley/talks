CMB lensing checks source n(z) and shear calibration

Lens ratio $\mu_i = C^{\gamma_i\delta}/C^{\kappa\delta}$ ÷ fiducial
$\propto (1+m_i) \times$ source lensing efficiency

  • bias and $\sigma_8$ cancel; cosmology enters only via distance ratios
  • mocks: shared $\mu = 1.03 \pm 0.06$
  • TR1 × SPT-3G: shared $\mu = 0.88^{+0.19}_{-0.13}$
  • TR1 × ACT DR6: shared $\mu = 0.83^{+0.15}_{-0.12}$
  • TR1 × SPT-3G: shared $\mu = 0.94^{+0.20}_{-0.14}$
  • TR1 × ACT DR6: shared $\mu = 0.89^{+0.18}_{-0.12}$
  • consistent with 1; per-bin errors 15–40 %, against a 0.3–1 % requirement

Derivation

Lensing efficiency. In the Born and Limber approximations, the convergence of sources at comoving distance $\chi_s$ is the projected matter overdensity weighted by geometry: $\kappa(\hat n) = \int_0^{\chi_s} d\chi\, \tfrac{3}{2}\Omega_{\rm m}\tfrac{H_0^2}{c^2}\,\tfrac{\chi}{a(\chi)}\,\tfrac{\chi_s-\chi}{\chi_s}\,\delta_{\rm m}(\chi\hat n, \chi)$. A matter fluctuation at distance $\chi$ lenses best when it sits roughly halfway to the sources and not at all when it sits on them; the factor $(\chi_s-\chi)/\chi_s$, the lens–source distance over the observer–source distance, carries that geometry. For a source bin with redshift distribution $n_i(\chi)$, average it over the sources: the lensing efficiency $q_i(\chi) = \int_\chi^\infty d\chi'\, n_i(\chi')\,(\chi'-\chi)/\chi'$, between 0 and 1. It is the only place the source n(z) enters. The CMB is a single source plane at $\chi_* \approx 14\,$Gpc, so $q_*(\chi) = (\chi_*-\chi)/\chi_*$, which depends on nothing but distances.

The two cross-spectra. A lens bin $j$ with redshift distribution $n_j(\chi)$ and linear bias $b_j$ traces $\delta_g = \int d\chi\, n_j b_j\, \delta_{\rm m}$. The E-mode shear equals the convergence up to $\sqrt{(\ell+2)(\ell-1)/\ell(\ell+1)} \approx 1$, the same in data and template, and the measured shear is $(1+m_i)$ times the true one. In Limber, with $P$ the matter power spectrum at $k = (\ell+\tfrac12)/\chi$,

$C_\ell^{\gamma_i\delta_j} = (1+m_i)\,\tfrac{3}{2}\Omega_{\rm m}\tfrac{H_0^2}{c^2}\int d\chi\, \frac{n_j(\chi)\, b_j}{a(\chi)\,\chi}\; q_i(\chi)\; P\big(\tfrac{\ell+1/2}{\chi}, z\big)$, and $C_\ell^{\kappa\delta_j}$ is the same integral with $q_*$ in place of $q_i$ and no $(1+m_i)$.

The ratio. Everything but the efficiency is common: $\Omega_{\rm m}H_0^2$, the galaxy bias, the amplitude and shape of $P$ (so $\sigma_8$), and the lens bin's n(z) as a weight. For a thin lens bin at $\chi_l$ the integrals collapse and $C^{\gamma_i\delta_j}/C^{\kappa\delta_j} = (1+m_i)\, q_i(\chi_l)/q_*(\chi_l)$. For a source plane this is $\frac{(\chi_s-\chi_l)/\chi_s}{(\chi_*-\chi_l)/\chi_*}$, the distance-ratio test of Jain & Taylor (2003) and, with the CMB as the far source, Das & Spergel (2009). For real bins it is $(1+m_i)\langle q_i\rangle_j/\langle q_*\rangle_j$, averaged over the lens bin with weight $n_j b_j P/(a\chi)$, which drifts slightly with ℓ. So we don't divide spectra: we fit the measured pair against the fiducial theory pushed through the same windows. $C^{\gamma_i\delta_j} = \mu_i\beta_j T^{\gamma\delta}_{ij}$ and $C^{\kappa\delta_j} = \beta_j T^{\kappa\delta}_j$, with $\beta_j$ free per lens bin (bias × σ₈-like amplitude, cancels) and

$\mu_i \simeq (1+m_i)\,\dfrac{\langle q_i\rangle_j}{\langle q_i^{\rm fid}\rangle_j}\,\dfrac{\langle q_*^{\rm fid}\rangle_j}{\langle q_*\rangle_j}$,

where the fiducial has $m = 0$ and the delivered source n(z). The CMB factor depends on cosmology only through distances, which is why a blind-sized shift moves μ by a few per cent while it moves the cross-spectra themselves by tens of per cent ($A^{\gamma\kappa} \propto S_8^{2.6}$ moves −25 %/+40 % over the envelope). So $\mu_i - 1 \approx m_i + \delta\ln\langle q_i\rangle$: the multiplicative bias plus the error in the source efficiency.

Sizes. On the TR1 n(z), $q_i/q_*$ at the lens planes the fit uses is 0.27 to 0.76: the Euclid sources sit much closer than the CMB. Moving a source bin's n(z) up by $\Delta z = 0.01$ raises $\langle q_i\rangle$ by 3.3 % for source bin 1 against lens bin 1, 0.2 to 0.6 % for source bin 6, and most when the lens plane sits just in front of the sources, where $q_i$ is small and its fractional change large. A 1 % error in $m$ moves μ by 1 % in every bin, independent of the lens plane. Two consequences: one $\mu_i$ cannot separate $m_i$ from a source-redshift shift, and using several lens planes per source bin could, in principle, since only the n(z) term varies with $\chi_l$ (the shear-ratio idea); our one-μ-per-bin fit does not attempt it. Scaled the same way, the shared $\mu = 0.88$ corresponds to $m \approx -0.12$, or to the high-z sources sitting roughly 0.1 to 0.2 lower in redshift than delivered: neither is credible at that size, so this is a consistency check.

What else moves it (none of it modelled). Intrinsic alignments: sources physically near the lens bin are aligned radially with it, which lowers $\gamma\delta$ and so μ; the $\bar z_s - \bar z_l \ge 0.2$ separation reduces but does not remove the overlap of n(z) tails. Lens n(z): the lens weight enters numerator and denominator differently, so a lens tail reaching behind the sources lowers $\gamma\delta$ and not $\kappa\delta$. Magnification of the lenses adds a term that does not cancel exactly. All three push or can push μ below 1, so a low μ is not by itself evidence for $m$.

The measurement

The dark slivers around 1 are the Euclid requirements propagated to $\mu_i$ as stated in the Euclid overview paper (Euclid Collaboration: Mellier et al., arXiv:2405.13491, Sect. on weak lensing): an uncertainty on the multiplicative bias below $2\times10^{-3}$ (from Cropper et al. 2013) and mean bin redshifts known to $\sigma_{\langle z\rangle} \lt 0.002\,(1+z)$. For each source bin the TR1 n(z) is shifted by $\pm 0.002(1+\bar z_i)$ and the source lensing efficiency recomputed, averaged over the n(z) of each lens bin the fit uses (equal weight); the change in its logarithm and $\sigma(m)$ add in quadrature. The result is a 1σ half-width of 1.0 % in bin 1 falling to 0.3 % in bin 6: the low-redshift bins are the most sensitive because their lens planes sit close in front of the sources. Our per-bin errors are 15 to 40 %, so on TR1 the lens ratio is a consistency check a factor 20 to 100 looser than the requirement, not a calibration.

Scale cut: both $\gamma\delta$ and $\kappa\delta$ keep $\ell \lt k_{\rm max}\chi(\bar z_j)$ with $k_{\rm max} = 0.15\,h/$Mpc, so ℓ below 128 to 310 depending on the lens bin. That is the cut the nonlinear-bias contamination test validates: a noiseless data vector with perturbative nonlinear bias, fitted with our linear-bias model, shifts $(S_8, \Omega_{\rm m})$ by $0.22\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ with $\delta\delta$ at 0.2 and the crosses at 0.15, against $0.52\,\sigma_{\rm 2D}$ with 0.2 everywhere. With 0.2 everywhere the shared μ was $0.87^{+0.15}_{-0.11}$; the stricter cut costs about a quarter of the precision and moves the centre by less than 0.1σ.

First step: the estimator on ten GLASS 6×2pt mocks (lognormal, linear bias, $m = 0,$ ACT-like κ noise on the TR1 footprint), where the truth is 1. Dots are the ten seeds, points the median with the robust seed scatter divided by $\sqrt{10}.$ Per-bin medians are 0.96 to 1.05 and the shared μ is $1.03 \pm 0.06$ (mean of seeds), so the estimator is unbiased at this precision. The seed scatter is 0.96 to 1.39 times the propagated per-bin error (1.13 for the shared μ, coverage 58 %), so the propagated errors may be 10 to 30 % too small; read the data errors with that in mind.

Second step: TR1 × SPT-3G data, through the blinded likelihood input and its full Gaussian covariance. Per bin, μ is 0.79 to 1.07 with errors of 15 to 40 %. The six points are strongly correlated, because every source bin is divided by the same $\kappa\delta_j$ spectra: a fluctuation in $\kappa\delta_j$ moves every $\mu_i$ that uses lens bin $j$ the same way, so five points below 1 are not five independent low points. The fit with one shared amplitude gives $\mu = 0.88$ ($-0.13,$ $+0.19$), 0.6σ below 1. That is a 15–20 % consistency check of shear calibration times n(z) efficiency, an order of magnitude looser than the ~1 % image-simulation priors on $m$: CMB lensing does not calibrate $m$ here, it checks that nothing is grossly wrong. Caveats: the mocks are ACT-like, so they test the estimator, not SPT's noise; intrinsic alignments of the sources enter $\gamma\delta$ and are not modelled; and these are exploratory estimators (results/scratch/napoli/crosses), not an lc output.

The variant control switches the shear method and the CMB map. Shared μ: LensMC × SPT-3G $0.88^{+0.19}_{-0.13}$, LensMC × ACT DR6 $0.83^{+0.15}_{-0.12}$, MetaCal × SPT-3G $0.94^{+0.20}_{-0.14}$, MetaCal × ACT DR6 $0.89^{+0.18}_{-0.12}$. All four sit within about 1σ of 1. They share galaxies and, per map, the same κ, and no joint covariance across variants exists, so differences between them are not calibrated significances.
