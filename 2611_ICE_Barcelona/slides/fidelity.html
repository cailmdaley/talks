Simulations range from approximate to sophisticated

  • hill-climbable!
  • Rungs — Gaussian κ realizations (SPT lensing calibration); lognormal GLASS for UNIONS and Euclid × κ, FLASK for the covariance; Agora, N-body lightcones with correlated tSZ, CIB and radio sources; a full forward model such as the ACT DR6 κ pipeline (M. Gatti). Shear, clustering and CMB lensing come from one simulated sky.
  • Two uses — fast simulations give many realizations on which the pipeline has to recover the input; a few expensive ones characterize subtle effects. Forward models are used for explicit or implicit inference.
  • Hill-climbable — a simulation with a known answer gives a score that an agent, or a person, can improve against.
  • Track — four rungs of simulation fidelity, each slower and more faithful than the last: Gaussian fields, lognormal fields, N-body lightcones, a full forward model of the measurement.
  • Marks — where the simulations in this talk sit: the GLASS lognormal mocks under UNIONS and Euclid × CMB lensing (their κ conditioning on ACT's own reconstruction simulations is implemented and validated); the FLASK covariance mocks calibrated on DEMNUni; Marco Gatti's forward model of the ACT DR6 lensing reconstruction.
  • Explicit and implicit — a forward model fast and faithful enough to compare directly with the data serves inference two ways: explicitly, by writing the likelihood of the data given the simulated field (field-level inference), or implicitly, by learning the posterior from simulations (simulation-based inference).
Pieces that exist

GLASS (Tessore et al. 2023) for shear, clustering and CMB lensing; FLASK (Xavier et al. 2016) on the DEMNUni simulations; SimBIG for galaxy clustering; the ACT DR6 lensing forward model of the Euclid forward-modeling work package (Gatti). Joining them and making them look like the data is where most of the work is.
