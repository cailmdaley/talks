Hands-on Tutorial: Dark Energy from 580 Supernovae with Lightcone

Cail Daley (CosmoStat, CEA Paris-Saclay) · ~35–40 minutes · Runs on any laptop with uv and a coding agent.

In this exercise you will build a reproducible flat-ΛCDM fit to the 580 Type Ia supernovae of the Union2.1 compilation (Suzuki et al. 2012), mechanically verify the published constraint against the cached PDF, discover why your initial uncertainty is three times too tight, and record the systematic covariance matrix as an explicit ASTRA decision across two universes.

0. Setup (5–8 minutes)

Install uv and lightcone-cli

lightcone-cli bundles git-annex and manages Python via uv. Make sure git knows your name and email (git config --global user.name / user.email).

1. Specify and materialize flat ΛCDM (~10 min)

Paste this prompt into your coding agent (not your shell):

Start a new Lightcone analysis fitting a flat LCDM model to the Union2.1 supernova compilation to recover Omega_Lambda, holding H0 = 70 km/s/Mpc as given in the file's header.

Download the data into data/:
  https://supernova.lbl.gov/Union/figures/SCPUnion2.1_mu_vs_z.txt

Use numpy, scipy (scipy.integrate.quad for luminosity distance), and matplotlib. Produce two outputs in astra.yaml:
1. best_fit (metric, format: json) — Omega_Lambda, 1-sigma uncertainty from delta-chi2 = 1, chi2, dof
2. hubble_diagram (figure, format: png) — distance modulus vs redshift with best-fit LCDM and Einstein-de Sitter curves, plus residuals panel

Don't add any decisions yet. Initialize with `lc init`, launch a local cluster with `lc compute launch --wait`, materialize both outputs with `lc materialize local`, and report the fitted Omega_Lambda.

You can inspect what Lightcone committed from a second terminal:

lc status
cat results/baseline/best_fit.json

Expected result: ΩΛ = 0.722 ± 0.013 (χ2/dof = 562.2 / 579 = 0.97).

2. Verify the published constraint against the PDF (~8 min)

Paste into your agent:

The paper that published this catalogue is Suzuki et al. 2012, arXiv:1105.3470.

Cache the paper with `uvx --from astra-tools==0.2.18 astra paper add 10.48550/arXiv.1105.3470`, locate their SNe-only flat-LCDM Omega_Lambda constraint (including systematics), record it in astra.yaml as a prior_insight with the exact quote and page number, and run `uvx --from astra-tools==0.2.18 astra validate astra.yaml --verify-evidence`.

Then compare our constraint to theirs.

Suzuki et al. (2012), page 17, report ΩΛ = 0.705+0.040−0.043 including systematics. Our central value agrees, yet our uncertainty is three times tighter on the exact same 580 supernovae.

3. Diagnose systematics and record an ASTRA decision (~10 min)

Paste into your agent:

Let's figure out why our error bars are 3x tighter than Suzuki et al. (2012). Download both Union2.1 covariance matrices into data/:
  https://supernova.lbl.gov/Union/figures/SCPUnion2.1_covmat_nosys.txt
  https://supernova.lbl.gov/Union/figures/SCPUnion2.1_covmat_sys.txt

Check which covariance matrix's diagonal matches the data table's error column. Then add a `covariance` decision to astra.yaml with options `stat_only` and `full` (default `full`), define two universes (`universes/baseline.yaml` and `universes/stat_only.yaml`, nesting options under `decisions:`), materialize both universes with `lc materialize local`, and print a comparison table.
AnalysisΩΛχ2 / dof
Statistical only (stat_only)0.722 ± 0.013562.2 / 579 (0.97)
Statistical + systematic (full)0.714 ± 0.030545.2 / 579 (0.94)
Suzuki et al. (2012), page 170.705 (+0.040, −0.043)—

The table's error column matches $\sqrt{\mathrm{diag}(C_{\mathrm{nosys}})}$ to 6.7×10−12 relative difference. Including the systematic covariance barely shifts the central value (0.008) while more than doubling the error bar, bringing the fit within 0.2σ of the paper.

4. Going further (if you finish early)

  • Redshift lever arm: add a z_max decision (all vs z_lt_0.8) and see how high-$z$ supernovae drive both precision and systematics.
  • Dark energy equation of state: add a de_model decision (lcdm vs wcdm) and plot the $(\Omega_m, w)$ degeneracy.
  • Visual inspection: install Lightcone Lab (uv tool install jupyterlab --with "jupyterlab-lightcone[full]") and open sn-cosmology/ in JupyterLab.
  • Clean up: stop your local worker with lc compute down local.
