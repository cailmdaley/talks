Extinction crosses match the CIB prediction

The question

In Napoli the systematics slide flagged δ₅ × extinction as the one failing template null after visibility weighting, and the summary asked whether it is CIB in the template: the Planck E(B−V) extinction map is built from dust emission, and dust-emission maps carry some cosmic infrared background, which traces the same large-scale structure as our galaxies. This is the test made during the week to answer it.

The test

If the extinction map is dust plus a CIB leak, $\mathrm{ext} = \mathrm{dust} + \epsilon\,\mathrm{CIB}$, with the dust uncorrelated with the tracers, then band by band the correlation coefficient of a tracer $T$ with extinction is fixed by two others: $r(T,\mathrm{ext})_\ell = r(T,\mathrm{CIB})_\ell\; r(\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext})_\ell$, with $r(a,b) = C^{ab}/\sqrt{C^{aa}C^{bb}}$. Nothing is fitted. The CIB is the Lenz, Doré & Lagache (2019) 857 GHz map on its $N_\mathrm{HI} \lt 2.5\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ mask (73 % of the extinction-cross support); its auto-spectrum is from odd × even halves, so it carries no noise bias. Tracer and extinction autos are total observed power. Same masks, estimator and 15 bands as the template null tests.

What it shows

The prediction reproduces every high-signal-to-noise cross, δ₅ included, the one that failed its null test after visibility weighting. Summarised as measured/predicted (an inverse-variance projection, not part of the test) with the diagonal χ² PTE over 15 bands: visibility-weighted δ₅ 1.26 (0.80), δ₂ 0.98 (0.92), δ₁ 0.97 (0.94); γ₆ 0.82 (0.97), γ₅ 0.90 (0.92); unweighted δ₆ 0.87 (0.52). γ₁ sits on a near-zero prediction. The exception is visibility-weighted δ₆: 0.22, PTE 0.013, worst band −3.4σ at ℓ ≈ 550. The visibility weighting removes the CIB-carried correlation there that the unweighted map keeps, plausibly because extinction is among the visibility model's five undocumented inputs (inferred, not tested). δ₃ and δ₄ weighted sit low too (0.31, 0.53), not significantly.

So the extinction-template crosses are consistent with being tracer × CIB carried by the extinction map, which is a statement about the template, not about our shear or density. The CIB correlation grows with redshift: the predicted $r$ over ℓ = 300–2000 goes 0.03 → 0.06 → 0.08 for δ₁, δ₅, δ₆ and 0.003 → 0.05 → 0.06 for γ₁, γ₅, γ₆.

Caveats

  • The PTEs are conservative: the measured–predicted covariance (shared tracer noise and sky) is ignored, and they cluster high (0.8–0.97), as expected.
  • Our template-cross estimator leaks the ℓ < 100 modes of steep-spectrum maps into the science bands (a GNILC dust map cut to ℓ ≤ 99 shows order-unity apparent power at ℓ = 550–2700). Template crosses stay valid as nulls, but the ℓ of a detection and a dust/CIB split read off single bands wait on closing that low-ℓ response.
  • On its own the shape of a tracer × extinction cross cannot separate CIB from dust (Δχ² ≤ 3 per bin); the correlation-coefficient test is what discriminates.
  • Checks behind it: the 857/545 GHz ratio of CIB × extinction (1.9–2.2) matches the CIB SED (1.95), not dust (3.06); the Lenz 857 odd × even auto reproduces the published spectrum to about 10 % for ℓ = 150–1700 on our footprint.
