Cycle 1's window leaks dust power to high $\ell$

  • Left — the extinction map’s own power, $\ell^2C_\ell$, on its Cycle-1 and Cycle-2 windows. Same dust values; different masks.
  • Cycle 1 — its fragmented window leaks low-ℓ dust power into high ℓ, so the auto turns up instead of falling.
  • Right — extinction × raw δ6, $\ell^2C_\ell$ with ±1σ Gaussian bands. The cross error scales with $\sqrt{C^{\rm ext\,ext}C^{\delta\delta}}$, so less template power means a tighter Cycle-2 band at high ℓ.
  • Consequence — Cycle 1 hid the high-ℓ signal; put on the Cycle-2 window, Cycle 1’s κ $\bar\rho$ rises 0.082 → 0.150 (Cycle 2: 0.171).
How we know it is leakage

A low-ℓ-only injection through each window (window-leakage/lowell.json) reproduces the high-ℓ excess directly. The Cycle-1 template auto is 40.9 times the rewindowed auto in the highest band. Both original windows are apodized over 0.15°. Cycle-1 data put on the Cycle-2 template window (c1_rewindowed_g5.json) recovers the Cycle-2 behaviour; that control is not plotted, to keep the slide to the two cycles.

The cycle ladder

κ $\bar\rho$: 0.0815 ± 0.0251 (C1) → 0.1504 ± 0.0238 (C1 rewindowed) → 0.1712 ± 0.0267 (C2); the window shift accounts for 77% of the rise. For raw δ6, C1 rewindowed gives 0.1702; with C2's theory normalization 0.1364, equal to C2's 0.1364. The change is not only error bars: the crosses and the template denominator also move. This does not establish a bias in the science vector.

What is plotted

Left: deconvolved extinction auto (mag²) times $\ell^2$, linear ordinate, log ℓ. Right: $\ell^2$ times the raw δ6 cross (mag) for $\ell\gt300$ (lower ℓ is noise-dominated), with the marginal Gaussian error as a band; nothing is divided by errors and no point is dropped above ℓ = 300. Rewindowed blocks use zero template-data Wick, the original ρ blocks observed Wick. No shared-map cross-cycle covariance exists, so the closeness of rewindowed and C2 means is descriptive.
