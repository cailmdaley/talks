UNIONS 3×2pt is forecast to measure $S_8$ to 2.6 %

$\sigma(S_8)$ and $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})$
  • first release: 0.07 on $S_8$
  • v2 shear: 0.028 and 0.10
  • 3×2pt: 0.022 and 0.039

Intrinsic alignments, shear calibration, redshifts, bias, magnification and baryons sampled. Gaussian and super-sample covariance. Lens counts per bin and mask estimated.

  • Contours — 68 % and 95 % of noiseless UNIONS v2 chains, $S_8$ against $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$, with the input cosmology ($S_8=0.839$, $\Omega_\mathrm{m}=0.319$) as the cross. No data vector is measured or looked at. Four tomographic source bins on 4,582 deg², alone and then with the five SPHEREx all-sky lens bins at $z=0.05$–$0.55$.
  • Widths — shear alone $\sigma(S_8)=0.0275$, $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})=0.099$, $\sigma(\sigma_8)=0.127$. 3×2pt 0.0219, 0.0387, 0.062. The $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$–$S_8$ figure of merit ($1/\sqrt{\det C}$) rises by a factor 3.0. The shear-only $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ width is largely the prior's, so the gain on $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ depends on it: $h<0.91$, $\ln(10^{10}A_s)<3.66$ and $0.085<\Omega_c h^2<0.2$ all cut that posterior. They do not cut the 3×2pt one.
  • Scale cuts — shear uses $300<\ell<1600$, the cut on which the first release has B-modes consistent with zero. At $100<\ell<1600$ shear alone gives 0.0274 and 0.070 and the 3×2pt 0.0214 and 0.035. At $20<\ell<2000$ shear alone gives 0.0235 and 0.054 and the 3×2pt 0.0211 and 0.034, so the 3×2pt width changes by 4 % on $S_8$ and 13 % on $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$; the lenses already supply the large-scale information.
  • Limits on $S_8$ — baryons first: with the feedback parameter $\log T_{\rm AGN}$ fixed, $\sigma(S_8)$ is 0.016 instead of 0.0219. Its prior is flat over the emulator's whole range (7.3–8.5) and the chain fills it, so 2.6 % is conservative there. Fixing intrinsic alignments, shear calibration, redshift shifts and magnification instead, with baryons and bias still free, gives 0.016. Most of that is source redshifts: the release's own n(z) realizations (0.006–0.012 on each bin's mean, against our 0.024) give 0.019. Halving the shear-calibration prior gives 0.021, and a Planck-width $n_s$ prior leaves the width unchanged.
  • For scale — the first release's $C_\ell$ result is $0.891^{+0.057}_{-0.084}$ (Guerrini et al.), and the DES Y3 3×2pt has $\sigma(S_8)=0.017$ and $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})=0.032$ (Abbott et al. 2022).
Inputs and their sources

Sources, v2.0.0 (the release's survey files; n(z) are not blinded): $n_{\rm eff}=0.825, 0.866, 0.942, 1.446$ arcmin⁻² (4.08 summed over the bins, 54 % of the shear weight of the full v2 sample), $\sigma_e=0.265, 0.262, 0.273, 0.277$, mean redshifts 0.29, 0.46, 0.61, 0.92.

Lenses, LSx v0.92 of Calum Murray (the first-3×2pt mock-chain work): 8.53 million galaxies on 4,099 deg², linear bias 1.05, 1.19, 1.37, 1.35, 1.53, from $w(\theta)$. The $n(z)$ slide before shows the six bins of LSx v0.9.

Estimated here, because the machine holding the files was unreachable: the split of the lens counts between bins ($dN/dz\propto z^2\exp[-(z/0.165)^{1.5}]$, giving 0.121, 0.203, 0.154, 0.074, 0.027 arcmin⁻²), the redshift scatter $0.012(1+z)$, and the mask (the footprint above $|b|=29°$, 4,068 deg²). In the Fisher forecast, a distribution peaking at higher redshift ($z_0=0.22$) changes the 3×2pt $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})$ by −3 % ($\sigma(S_8)$ by −1 %), a scatter of 0.02 by +1 %, and half the lens counts by +3 % ($\sigma(S_8)$ unchanged).

Nuisances and priors

Sampled: NLA $A_{\rm IA}\sim N(0.83, 0.78)$ (the first release), $\eta_{\rm IA}\sim U(-5,5)$, centred on 0.83 and 0; shear $m\sim N(0, 0.01)$ and source mean-redshift shifts $N(0, 0.024)$ per source bin (clustering-redshift means are good to 0.021–0.026); lens shifts $N(0, 0.008)$; lens magnification slope $N(0.4, 0.2)$ (not measured for LSx); bias $U(0.5, 3)$ per lens bin; HMcode-2020 $\log T_{\rm AGN}\sim U(7.3, 8.5)$; $\ln(10^{10}A_s)\sim U(1.609, 3.664)$, $\Omega_c h^2\sim U(0.085, 0.2)$, $\Omega_b h^2\sim N(0.0220, 0.00055)$, $h\sim U(0.4, 0.91)$, $n_s\sim N(0.96, 0.042)$. Not in the model: redshift-space distortions, nonlinear bias, source clustering, lens density systematics. Lenses enter at $k<0.2\ h/$Mpc (clustering, automatic pairs only) and $0.15$ (galaxy–galaxy lensing), $\ell>20$.

Covariance

Our own coupled pseudo-$C_\ell$ Gaussian covariance with the mask couplings, plus super-sample covariance (removing it changes the $S_8$ and $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ widths by under 3 %). There is no connected non-Gaussian term. On the real-space mocks the Gaussian covariance was 5–20 % tight. Fisher widths are about 10 % narrower than the chains (3×2pt $\sigma(S_8)$ 0.0196 and $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})$ 0.034).

Other forecasts

These use fewer nuisances or different data, so they are narrower. The GLASS mock chain, in real space with stand-in lens bins and no nuisances, gives $\sigma(S_8)=0.0093$ and $\sigma(\Omega_\mathrm{m})=0.021$. The Fisher forecast made for choosing the v2 n(z), cosmic shear alone with redshift priors near 0.01–0.02, gives 0.0113–0.0132 on $S_8$; its baryon treatment and scale range were not checked. Calum Murray's SPHEREx Fisher forecast is in a private repository and is not compared.
