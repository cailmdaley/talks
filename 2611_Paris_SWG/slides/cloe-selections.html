3×2pt and the κ crosses on the GLASS mock

  • What is run — three nautilus chains with CLOE in JAX on the GLASS mock (seed 3004, unblinded, so physical values are shown): the full 5×2pt vector (651 bands), 3×2pt alone (539 bands) and the κ × shear and κ × positions crosses alone (112 bands). Same priors, scale cuts and covariance as the production chains.
  • $S_8$ — 0.824 ± 0.013 (3×2pt + κ crosses), 0.823 ± 0.013 (3×2pt), 0.812 ± 0.033 (κ crosses); the mock input is 0.825. All three recover it within 0.4σ.
  • Reading — on this TR1-sized mock with SPT-3G Winter-like κ noise, the κ crosses alone constrain $S_8$ about 2.6 times less tightly than 3×2pt and leave $\Omega_\mathrm{m}$ nearly free (0.43 ± 0.14); added to 3×2pt they do not change the $S_8$ width at the precision of these chains.
  • Where these come from — the likelihood packaged for the SPT × Euclid 6×2pt analysis: the same CLOE theory and JAX port, built directly from a data-vector file and a configuration. Run with the production chain's random seed, its full-vector chain is identical to the production mock chain rerun with the current code: all 41 000 points, weights and log-likelihoods are equal.

The package against production

At 25 points drawn from the prior, the package's bandpowers and log-likelihood equal those of the production likelihood exactly on the full vector, with either $P(k)$ model, and to floating-point rounding on the two subsets (largest band difference $3\times10^{-14}\sigma$, largest $\Delta\ln L$ $7\times10^{-12}$).

The mocks are made with dark-matter-only HMCode-2020, which the emulators do not provide. On the mock, CLOE in JAX therefore uses the feedback emulator with HMCode's feedback response removed and a correction table fixed at the reference cosmology; against plain CLOE with CAMB this is within 0.038σ in every band (0.005σ on the κ crosses). On the data model the same comparison gives $3.9\times10^{-4}\sigma$.
