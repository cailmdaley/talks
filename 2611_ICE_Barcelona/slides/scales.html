Choose work that scales with compute

  • the bitter lesson, one level up: put expertise into what scales, not into hand-tuned steps
  • the spiral turns an analysis into the right-hand kind
  • The principle — Sutton's bitter lesson, applied to research practice rather than to models: the methods that win are the ones that grow with computation, so expertise is best spent on search, learning and the engineering that lets them run, rather than frozen into hand-tuned procedures or tips and tricks for using agents.
  • Two kinds of work — neither a ranking nor a fixed category; a project holds both. The bottleneck decides which kind a task is.
  • The spiral — every check is a known answer an agent can run against, so validation is verifiable work, and it grows with the compute behind it.
Sources

R. Sutton, "The Bitter Lesson" (2019). The two kinds of work follow the FORTH Crete keynote (September 2026).
