Galaxy density may have problems, especially lens bin 1

  • Shear, κ: clean
  • δ1: exposures, noise (depth)
  • δ2–6: extinction

Test visibility weights remove most δ signals; PTEs not yet calibrated on mocks.

Each cell is a null test: one Euclid tracer (lens bin δ, source bin γ, the rows) or the CMB lensing map κ, crossed with one VMPZ systematics template (the columns). The number is the χ² probability to exceed over 15 bands, $100 \lt \ell \lt 3000$, with the full Gaussian covariance; κ uses the diagonal only. Values below $10^{-6}$ are written as such. These template crosses are blinding-independent. The variant control switches the shear method (γ rows) and the CMB map (κ row); the δ rows are the same in every variant.

No template stands out against shear, for either shear method: across all 72 shear tests (six templates, six source bins, two methods) the lowest PTE is 0.03, about what the smallest of 72 independent tests gives. κ is quiet for both maps (lowest 0.17). Galaxy density has two signatures. Extinction fails across the lens bins (PTE below $10^{-6}$ in lens bins 4–6, χ² 70–80 over 15), but its sign is positive in every tracer, the opposite of dust obscuration, and it reaches high-z shear and κ too, with both shear methods and both CMB maps. The Planck E(B−V) map behind the template is built from far-infrared dust emission, and the cosmic infrared background, the integrated emission of dusty star-forming galaxies at z ≈ 1–2, leaks into it; those galaxies trace the same large-scale structure we measure. So it is most likely structure in a foreground map, not contamination of ours. That reading rests on the sign and on the shear and κ rows; the direct test, not yet run, is to swap in a CIB-cleaned dust map (Planck GNILC) and see the δ signal drop. Exposures and noise fail in lens bin 1 (PTE below $10^{-6}$, χ² 87 and 72 over 15), mirror images of each other because pixel noise falls as exposure$^{-1/2}$: one depth dependence of the faintest lens sample, counted once. PSF, zodiacal light and stars are clean apart from single cells near 0.01.

The PTEs themselves are not yet trustworthy as significances: with about a hundred template crosses per variant, correlated with one another through shared tracers and templates, and an analytic covariance not yet checked against the scatter of mocks, the distribution of these numbers under the null is not known. The plan is to run the same null tests on the GLASS mocks and calibrate them, as for the UNIONS B-mode null tests. Read the dark cells as clear detections and the 0.01–0.1 cells as unknown.

Visibility maps. The galaxy-density maps here are plain number counts over the footprint, not corrected with the visibility (selection-function) maps that are meant to remove depth, exposure and extinction modulations, so the δ rows are not a fair test of the GC sample as it will be analysed. Dividing each δ by the VMPZ v1.1 test visibility weights (Odier, Alguero, Macías-Pérez; January 2026, not yet validated) removes most of them: PTE before → after, δ₁ × exposures $3\times10^{-12} \to 0.03$, δ₁ × noise $2\times10^{-9} \to 0.10$, δ₂–₄, δ₆ × extinction from $10^{-4}$–$10^{-9}$ to 0.12–0.97. δ₅ × extinction survives ($9\times10^{-8}$, +3.5σ at ℓ ≈ 1090), and δ₃ × exposures/noise shows ±4.5σ at the tiling harmonic ℓ ≈ 550. Not yet adopted: the five templates the visibility model is fit to are not documented (if extinction is one, its crosses vanish partly by construction), and the weight convention needs the producers' confirmation.

Is the extinction signal CIB? Open. The extinction template is documented as Planck 2013 radiance-based E(B−V), which carries cosmic infrared background; of the six templates it is the only one that correlates with the Planck GNILC CIB maps, and κ × extinction is about what the CIB route predicts. A swap to the GNILC CIB-reduced dust map weakens every cross, but it cannot apportion dust and CIB: GNILC dust is CIB-reduced, not CIB-free, and our estimator leaks the large-scale (ℓ < 100) modes of steep-spectrum maps like dust into the ℓ ≥ 100 bands, so a template cross at a given ℓ is not a measurement at that ℓ. The null tests stand as nulls (an uncorrelated template gives zero in every band whatever leaks); which scale and which component carry a detection is not yet resolved.
