Extinction crosses follow the CIB, closest at 857 GHz

The question

In Napoli the systematics slide flagged δ₅ × extinction as the one failing template null after visibility weighting, and the summary asked whether it is CIB in the template: the Planck E(B−V) extinction map is built from dust emission, and dust-emission maps carry some cosmic infrared background, which traces the same large-scale structure as our galaxies. This is the test made during the week to answer it.

The test

If the extinction map is dust plus a CIB leak, $\mathrm{ext} = \mathrm{dust} + \epsilon\,\mathrm{CIB}$, with the dust uncorrelated with the tracers, then band by band the correlation coefficient of a tracer $T$ with extinction is fixed by two others: $r(T,\mathrm{ext})_\ell = r(T,\mathrm{CIB})_\ell\; r(\mathrm{CIB},\mathrm{ext})_\ell$, with $r(a,b) = C^{ab}/\sqrt{C^{aa}C^{bb}}$. Nothing is fitted. The CIB is the Lenz, Doré & Lagache (2019) 857 GHz map on its $N_\mathrm{HI} \lt 2.5\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{cm^{-2}}$ mask (73 % of the extinction-cross support); its auto-spectrum is from odd × even halves, so it carries no noise bias. Tracer and extinction autos are total observed power. Same masks, estimator and 15 bands as the template null tests.

The build

Measured points first (density bins visibility-weighted), then the prediction from the 857 GHz CIB map, then unweighted δ₆ beside the weighted one; the last two clicks swap the prediction to the 545 and 353 GHz maps. The measured points do not change between frequencies.

What it shows at 857 GHz

The prediction reproduces the high-signal-to-noise visibility-weighted density and shear crosses, δ₅ included, the one that failed its null test after visibility weighting. Summarised as measured/predicted (an inverse-variance projection with the measured errors, not part of the test) and the diagonal χ² PTE over 15 bands: visibility-weighted δ₅ 1.26 ± 0.21 (0.80), δ₂ 0.98 (0.92), δ₁ 0.97 (0.94); γ₆ 0.82 ± 0.24 (0.97), γ₅ 0.90 ± 0.29 (0.92); unweighted δ₆ 0.87 ± 0.15 (0.52). γ₁ sits on a near-zero prediction. Unweighted δ₂, δ₄, δ₅ sit above it (1.80 ± 0.34, 1.65 ± 0.25, 1.41 ± 0.20), an excess consistent with selection in the coverage-only maps (inferred). The exception is visibility-weighted δ₆: 0.22 ± 0.16, PTE 0.013. The weighting removes most of the CIB-carried correlation that the unweighted map keeps, plausibly because extinction is among the visibility model's five undocumented inputs (inferred, not tested). Its worst band, −3.4σ at ℓ ≈ 550, is not from the weighting: the unweighted δ₆ dips in the same band (−2.8σ). Across the three frequencies that band stays at −3.0 to −3.4σ weighted and −2.3 to −2.8σ unweighted. δ₃ and δ₄ weighted sit low too (0.31 ± 0.30, 0.53 ± 0.27; 2.3σ and 1.7σ below 1).

So the extinction-template crosses are consistent with being tracer × CIB carried by the extinction map, which is a statement about the template, not about our shear or density. The CIB correlation grows with redshift: the predicted $r$ over ℓ = 300–2000 goes 0.03 → 0.06 → 0.09 for δ₁, δ₅ and unweighted δ₆, and 0.003 → 0.05 → 0.06 for γ₁, γ₅, γ₆.

545 and 353 GHz

The lower-frequency maps predict less: for the high-signal-to-noise bins (δ₅, δ₆, γ₅, γ₆) 0.73–0.77× the 857 GHz prediction at 545 and 0.59–0.72× at 353 (lower bins span 0.64–0.83 and 0.47–0.72), so measured/predicted rises. δ₅ goes 1.26 ± 0.21 → 1.63 ± 0.27 → 1.89 ± 0.32 (857 → 545 → 353), unweighted δ₆ 0.87 → 1.13 → 1.29, γ₆ 0.82 → 1.09 → 1.13. The PTEs stay acceptable except in unweighted δ₄, which goes 0.083 → 0.009 → 0.001 (worst band ℓ ≈ 1090, +3.4σ at 353), and unweighted δ₅ at 353 (0.042, +2.8σ at ℓ ≈ 1090). So the CIB in the extinction map correlates with our tracers the way the 857 GHz CIB does. That fits the template: the Planck 2013 E(B−V) is built from dust radiance, which the 857 GHz and IRAS 100 μm data dominate.

The drop is larger than the decoherence of the CIB between frequencies would give alone: Lenz's published spectra give a squared coherence with 857 GHz of 0.91 at 545 and 0.80 at 353 (ℓ = 300–2000). The rest comes from the tracers: their correlation with the CIB, $r(T,\mathrm{CIB})$, falls by 20–30 % from 857 to 545 GHz (about 20 % in the high-redshift bins, up to 40 % in the low ones), as expected if lower-frequency CIB comes from higher redshift than our samples (inferred).

The 353 GHz map carries no primary CMB to bias this: its odd × even auto-spectrum matches Lenz's published 353 × 353 spectrum as closely as the 857 one does (within 10–30 % at ℓ = 150–1700, no band more than 2σ off), where primary CMB would be about 10³ times the CIB.

Caveats

  • The PTEs are conservative: the measured–predicted covariance (shared tracer noise and sky) is ignored, and most sit high (0.8–0.98), as expected.
  • Our template-cross estimator leaks the ℓ < 100 modes of steep-spectrum maps into the science bands (a GNILC dust map cut to ℓ ≤ 99 shows order-unity apparent power at ℓ = 550–2700). Template crosses stay valid as nulls, but the ℓ of a detection and a dust/CIB split read off single bands wait on closing that low-ℓ response.
  • On its own the shape of a tracer × extinction cross cannot separate CIB from dust (Δχ² ≤ 3 per bin); the correlation-coefficient test is what discriminates.
  • The ratios share maps across bins and frequencies, so they are correlated, and their errors omit the prediction's own uncertainty.
  • Checks behind it: the 857/545 GHz ratio of CIB × extinction (1.9–2.2) matches the CIB SED (1.95), not dust (3.06); the Lenz 857 odd × even auto reproduces the published spectrum to about 10 % for ℓ = 150–1700 on our footprint.
