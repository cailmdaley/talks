Control variates for SPT covariance

$$\hat\Sigma = \hat\Sigma_y^N - \beta\,\bigl(\hat\Sigma_c^N - \hat\Sigma_c^M\bigr)$$
  • $\beta = \dfrac{\mathrm{cov}(y, c)}{\mathrm{var}(c)}$ per element
  • Noise per element: $\dfrac{1-\rho^2}{N} + \dfrac{\rho^2}{M}$ in place of $\dfrac{1}{N}$
  • Unbiased if constrained mocks are unbiased
  • Left — each SPT-constrained mock $y$ (blue, SPT's own reconstruction) beside a mock $c$ of the same sky, the same κ and Euclid fields, reconstructed generically; identical dots mark the shared sky.
  • Right — SPT-like mocks alone, each on a new sky, as many as can be afforded: they pin down the covariance of the generic reconstruction.
  • Estimator — $\hat\Sigma_y^N$ and $\hat\Sigma_c^N$ are the sample covariances of $y$ and $c$ over the $N$ pairs, $\hat\Sigma_c^M$ that of $c$ over the $M$ SPT-like mocks. The constrained mocks' covariance is corrected by how far the generic covariance on the same $N$ skies sits from its value over the $M$. Its expectation is SPT's covariance, whatever the generic reconstruction gets wrong.
  • $\beta$ — a regression coefficient, one per covariance element: the part of an element's fluctuation in $y$ that its fluctuation in $c$ predicts, $\beta = \mathrm{cov}(y,c)/\sigma_c^2 = \rho\,\sigma_y/\sigma_c$, fitted on the pairs. $\beta = 0$ ignores the SPT-like mocks; $\beta = 1$ is the SPT-like covariance plus the difference between the two reconstructions measured on the pairs.
  • Noise — with $\rho$ the correlation of an element's terms between the two members of a pair, the element's variance is $\sigma_y^2\,[(1-\rho^2)/N + \rho^2/M]$ against $\sigma_y^2/N$ for the constrained mocks alone (my derivation for a fixed $\beta = \rho\,\sigma_y/\sigma_c$ and $M$ mocks independent of the pairs; the papers quote the $M \to \infty$ limit, $1-\rho^2$). The first term is the part of $y$ that $c$ does not predict; the second is the noise of the $M$ mocks' own covariance. For $N = 400$ and $\rho = 0.9$: equivalent to 2100 constrained mocks as $M \to \infty$, 1500 at $M = 4000$, 780 at $M = 1000$. How large $\rho$ is here is open. An idea for discussion, not a worked method.
The estimator

In CARPool (Chartier et al. 2021) a costly simulation $\boldsymbol{y}$ and a cheap surrogate $\boldsymbol{c}$ are run from the same initial conditions, and $\boldsymbol{x} = \boldsymbol{y} - \boldsymbol{\beta}(\boldsymbol{c} - \boldsymbol{\mu_c})$ is unbiased for $\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{y}]$ for any $\boldsymbol{\beta}$, with $\boldsymbol{\mu_c}$ the surrogate's mean from a separate set of seeds (their eqs. 5, 10–11 and 14). For one element the best $\beta$ is $\mathrm{cov}(y,c)/\sigma_c^2$ and the variance falls by $1-\rho_{y,c}^2$ (eqs. 3–4). Chartier & Wandelt apply the same estimator to a covariance by treating the centred outer products $\boldsymbol{Y}_n = \tilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_n\tilde{\boldsymbol{y}}_n^\mathsf{T}$ and $\boldsymbol{C}_n$ as the samples: $\boldsymbol{\Sigma_{yy}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}) = \gamma\overline{\boldsymbol{Y}} - \gamma\boldsymbol{\beta}(\overline{\boldsymbol{C}} - \boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_C)$, with $\gamma = N/(N-1)$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_C$ the surrogate covariance from separate realizations (their eq. 13), and $\beta$ fitted separately for each element on the same $N$ pairs (their section 3, "$q=1$").

Here: $\boldsymbol{y}_n$ is the data vector (κ × shear, κ × positions, κκ and the galaxy-only spectra) measured on SPT-constrained mock $n$; $\boldsymbol{c}_n$ is the same data vector on the same sky with the generic reconstruction; $\hat\Sigma_y^N$ and $\hat\Sigma_c^N$ in the drawing are $\gamma\overline{\boldsymbol{Y}}$ and $\gamma\overline{\boldsymbol{C}}$, and $\hat\Sigma_c^M$ is $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{M}}_C$ from the $M$ SPT-like mocks. Fitting $\beta$ on the same pairs makes the estimate unbiased only up to small finite-$N$ terms; the papers accept this. Elements without κ are identical in $y$ and $c$, so $\beta = 1$ there and they come entirely from the $M$ SPT-like mocks. Setting $\beta = 1$ everywhere gives a simpler variant to read: the SPT-like covariance plus the difference between the two reconstructions, measured on the pairs.

What it needs to work
  • A high per-band correlation. For a covariance the samples are products of data vectors, and for Gaussian fluctuations, if a band in $y$ and in $c$ correlates with coefficient $r$, its diagonal element correlates as $\rho = r^2$ (my derivation, not from the papers). The element noise then falls by $1-r^4$: about 3× fewer constrained mocks needed at $r = 0.9$, 5× at 0.95, 25× at 0.99.
  • The same CMB and noise realization in each pair. If the generic reconstruction runs on the same simulated CMB and noise maps that SPT reconstructed, the reconstruction noise is shared and $r$ is limited only by the differences between the two estimators (filtering, weights, masks). If the pair shares only the input κ and the Euclid fields, the reconstruction noise is independent between $y$ and $c$, and for κ × galaxy bands where that noise is comparable to the lensing signal, $r$ falls to roughly 0.5–0.7 and the gain disappears (my rough estimate, for reconstruction noise between half of and equal to the κ power per mode, which leaves at most a 1.3× gain). So the useful ask of SPT would be its simulated CMB and noise maps, or a generic reconstruction run on them, beyond the reconstructed κ maps of the previous slide. Whether SPT can share those is not known.
  • A positive-definite result. The estimate is not positive-definite by construction. Chartier & Wandelt reached positive-definiteness from about 80 pairs for a 79-bin power spectrum, but not for their most ill-conditioned covariances; shrinkage or tapering would fix that at the cost of strict unbiasedness (their section 4).
  • Cost to the covariance team. One generic reconstruction per constrained mock, about 400, on top of the SPT-like mocks they make anyway; the estimator itself is a few lines on stored data vectors. With $\beta$ fitted from data it never does worse than ignoring $c$, up to the noise in $\beta$.
What could be checked first
  • With SPT-like mocks alone: run two different generic reconstructions (for example different multipole ranges or filtering) on the same simulated skies, once with the same noise realization and once with independent ones, and measure $r$ per band of κ × shear and κ × positions. The two cases bracket the two pairings above.
  • A dry run of the estimator on those mocks: treat one reconstruction as "costly" with only 400 realizations and the other as the surrogate with many, and compare the result with the sample covariance of the costly one from all realizations: element noise, positive-definiteness, and the $\chi^2$ and Fisher errors it gives.
  • Once SPT-constrained mocks exist, $r$ per band from the pairs themselves decides whether the combination is worth using.
